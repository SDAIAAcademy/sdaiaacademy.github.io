USE-FLU-302

Team Build-and-Own Studio: One Application for One Entity

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Studio
  • 40 h
  • In development

Teams of two to four from one organisation take one sponsor-owned problem, such as answering staff questions from a policy handbook, to a working assistant or agent with mentor engineers. They frame the problem, agree data rules with the data owner, build on an approved low-code or hosted platform, test with questions written with users, run a supervised trial and hand the application to a named owner with a support routine and a retirement condition. Production engineering belongs to DEV-LLM-201 and DEV-SEC-201.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Staff in any role and sector, from new hires to managers, who use or will use dashboards, AI assistants and agents in routine work; no technical background is assumed and delivery is Arabic-first in the Kingdom.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Draft a problem statement with users, process, success measures, risk tier and constraints.

  2. Applied

    Prepare a data-use record classifying each source and stating what the application may read, store and send.

  3. Performance

    Build a working assistant or agent on an approved platform within the data rules.

  4. Applied

    Test the application with user-written questions, including required refusals, hidden instructions in documents and data-extraction attempts.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate a supervised user trial and decide whether to continue, change or stop.

  6. Performance

    Deliver a hand-over pack naming owner, technical contact, support routine, limits, retirement condition and cases for developers.

Format and days

Format
Studio: four-week coached team project with mentor engineers, weekly reviews, a supervised trial and a defence
Total hours
40 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-202 AI Productivity for Knowledge Work Level 200
  • One member has completed USE-FLU-205 or built a custom assistant
  • A sponsor who owns the problem and a data owner who approves data use

More about this course

Topics

Framing the problem

  • Problems an assistant can solve
  • Risk tier and problems to turn down
  • Sponsor and decision rights

Data rules

  • Classification of every source
  • Read, store and send permissions
  • Minimisation and owner approval

Building on a platform

  • Reference files, tools and connectors
  • Answers that cite their source
  • Limits of low-code platforms

Testing and trial

  • User-written question sets
  • Refusal, leak and injection tests
  • Usage logs

Ownership

  • Support and update routines
  • Retirement conditions
  • Hand-over to developers

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Assistant and agent builder
Open WebUI with knowledge collections, a self-hostable builder such as Dify or n8n, an approved enterprise assistant builder
Model access
a locally served open-weight model, the entity's approved endpoint
Test scoring
a shared spreadsheet with pass criteria, promptfoo, run with the mentor

How the work is assessed

  • Individual supervised AI-off check on a data-use scenario and a test report (10%)
  • Milestones (25%)
  • Application and documents on a six-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Team defence with individual questions (25%)
To pass
70% overall, 60% on the rubric, data-use approval before building and a sponsor-accepted hand-over pack

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and for writing test questions with users; AI-tutor for the problem statement and data-use record; AI-full in building and testing, where the team defends every configuration choice.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
