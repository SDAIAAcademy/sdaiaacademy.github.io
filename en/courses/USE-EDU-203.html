USE-EDU-203

AI Literacy for School Teachers

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 4 days · 12 h
  • Available

Teachers of any subject map competence frameworks for teachers and pupils, run age-banded activities as pupils would (unplugged sorting, training and breaking a classifier, checking a chatbot), plan a lesson for their subject, apply safeguarding rules, write to families about the class's AI rules, and teach the lesson once for review.

Open the coursePractice questions

Under review before its first delivery

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

University students in any discipline, learners in training programmes, and the faculty, school teachers and trainers who teach and assess them; no technical background is assumed.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Compare the competence frameworks for teachers and pupils and identify those a lesson addresses.

  2. Applied

    Use age-appropriate activities to teach how AI learns, fails and is checked.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a lesson for one's subject with an AI-literacy objective and an AI-off understanding check.

  4. Applied

    Apply safeguarding rules for pupils' data, tool age limits and generated images of real people.

  5. Applied

    Draft a plain-language letter to families on how AI is and is not used in class.

Format and days

Format
Workshop: three three-hour sessions, guided pre-work and a tracked trial lesson
Total hours
12 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 الأطر، وكيف يتعلم الذكاء الاصطناعي

    2. الصفوف الأولية: لعبة تصنيف دون حاسوب
    3. الصفوف العليا: درّب مصنِّفًا ثم أفشِله

    1. حماية الطلاب: البيانات والأدوات والصور

    3. الاختبار القصير والدرس التجريبي

  4. Day 4 مراجعة الدرس التجريبي

More about this course

Topics

Frameworks

  • UNESCO teacher and student competences
  • OECD and European Commission domains: engage, create, manage, shape
  • AI literacy versus tool use

Activities by age band

  • Early primary: unplugged sorting games
  • Upper primary: train and break a classifier
  • Secondary: check a chatbot, spot bias and synthetic media

Safeguarding and families

  • Pupils' data and approved tools
  • Synthetic images of real people and reporting
  • Homework, unequal home access and disclosure

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Classifier trainer for pupils
Orange Data Mining, a browser-based image or sound trainer needing no pupil accounts
Teacher's AI assistant
an open-weight model run locally, an assistant approved by the school authority

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off safeguarding check (25%)
  • Map, plan and letter (45%)
  • Trial-lesson review (30%)
To pass
70% overall and five of six safeguarding decisions correct

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the safeguarding check; AI-tutor while planning; AI-full for the family letter, which the teacher checks and owns.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
