AE-408

Multimodal and Speech Systems Engineering

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

Participants build and evaluate applications that see, listen and speak, with Arabic as the main case. For the fictional Heritage Trails Authority they extract grounded fields from visitor and ranger photos with vision-language models, benchmark open-weight speech recognisers on dialects and code-switched speech, test synthesised read-back, and assemble a bilingual voice booking line with turn-taking, interruption and tool calls within a latency budget. They test it with simulated callers and choose between open-weight and hosted, cascaded and speech-to-speech designs on measured quality, latency and cost. Photos are public-domain or synthetic; calls use synthetic voices and public speech corpora.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Engineers who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a production AI system they built, operated and can defend.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement image applications with vision-language models that return validated structured fields grounded in the image.

  2. Performance

    Evaluate speech recognisers on Arabic dialects and code-switched speech with explicit normalisation rules.

  3. Performance

    Build a bilingual voice agent that handles turn-taking, interruption and confirmed tool calls within a latency budget.

  4. Performance

    Diagnose failure modes specific to image and audio inputs, including invented objects, text produced on silence and instructions hidden in images.

  5. Performance

    Design a multimodal test set and an end-to-end evaluation with simulated callers.

  6. Performance

    Decide between open-weight and hosted, cascaded and speech-to-speech designs on quality, latency and cost per call.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
26 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Seeing

    1. How a vision-language model sees
    2. Structured extraction from photos
    3. Grounding answers in pixels
    4. Arabic text and hidden instructions

  2. Day 2 Listening

    1. From waveform to text
    2. Normalisation decides the error rate
    3. Dialects and code-switching
    4. Streaming and silence

  3. Day 3 Speaking

    1. Synthesis and read-back
    2. Cascaded versus speech-to-speech
    3. Turn-taking and interruption
    4. Tool calls during speech

  4. Day 4 Evaluating and choosing

    1. Multimodal test sets
    2. Simulated callers
    3. Choosing by quality, latency and cost
    4. Voice, faces and consent

  5. Day 5 Capstone project presentations

More about this course

Topics

Vision-language models in applications

  • Visual tokens, resolution and cost
  • Structured extraction from photos
  • Grounding with boxes and points
  • Frames from video
  • Instructions hidden in images

Speech recognition

  • Recogniser families and multilingual encoders
  • Arabic normalisation of diacritics, letter variants and numerals
  • Dialects, code-switching and telephone audio
  • Streaming, voice activity detection, diarisation and text on silence

Synthesis and voice agents

  • Read-back of names, numbers and dates
  • Cascaded, speech-to-speech and full-duplex designs
  • End-of-turn detection and interruption
  • Latency budgets and tool calls during speech

Evaluation

  • Multimodal test sets
  • Error rates with intervals by slice
  • Simulated callers
  • Cost per call

Responsible voice and image handling

  • Voice and faces as personal data
  • Consent, retention and minimisation
  • Disclosure of synthetic voices

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Vision-language model access
an open-weight model on a notebook GPU, a hosted model through a chat-completions style endpoint
Open-weight speech recognition
Whisper-family models, wav2vec-family encoders
Voice activity detection and diarisation
Silero VAD, pyannote.audio
Speech synthesis and speech-to-speech
an open-weight text-to-speech model, any hosted real-time speech model
Voice agent framework
Pipecat, LiveKit Agents
Error-rate scoring
jiwer, a custom scorer with Arabic normalisation
Public data
Mozilla Common Voice, public-domain image collections
Evaluation harness
Inspect, pytest with custom scorers

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: compute error rates under two normalisation rules and diagnose three failing inputs (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a rubric: image pipeline 20, speech evaluation 25, voice agent 25, test set and simulation 20, decision record 10 (60%)
  • Live defence with a call or image the assessor supplies (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the day 2 normalisation lab. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant hints and does not write pipelines or scorers. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
