DS-305

Uncertainty, Interpretability and Model Validation

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

Participants produce the evidence an independent validator needs about a trained model: whether its probabilities can be trusted, how wide its errors are, what drives its predictions, where it stops working and whom it fails. For Mizan Community Lending, a fictional microfinance lender, they repair calibration, wrap models in conformal intervals and prediction sets, test coverage by subgroup and under shift, show where explanations mislead, tabulate subgroup error and write a validation report against a published checklist. Fairness mitigation and documentation are DS-309.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Analysts and data scientists who have completed the level 200 set or can show equivalent work: an analysis or model they built, validated and can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Measure calibration with proper scores and reliability diagrams, recalibrate, and report the effect by subgroup.

  2. Applied

    Implement conformal intervals and prediction sets and test coverage overall, by subgroup and under simulated shift.

  3. Applied

    Apply permutation, accumulated-local-effect and Shapley-value explanations and test their stability across refits.

  4. Performance

    Diagnose why a model degrades on a later batch from slice metrics, coverage loss and changed explanations.

  5. Performance

    Audit a model package for conceptual soundness, outcome performance and monitoring readiness and decide whether it is fit for use.

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on calibration, coverage and explanation limits (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: calibration and coverage 25, explanations 20, subgroup and shift findings 25, decision 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Panel defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and first readings of diagrams and coverage tables. AI-tutor in labs, explaining without writing code or findings. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
