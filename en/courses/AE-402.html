AE-402

AI Solution Architecture and Platform Design

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

Participants practise the architect's loop on AI systems and apply it to the shared platform many teams build on. For a fictional ministry of municipal services with nine product teams and data at three classification levels, they turn requirements into measurable scenarios, draw views as code, cost three options including a non-generative one, keep a decision log, design a model catalogue, gateway, tenancy model, golden paths and showback, enforce a reference design with fitness checks, and review a peer's architecture. Serving optimisation and reliability engineering are left to AE-401 and AE-403.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Engineers who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a production AI system they built, operated and can defend.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare quality-attribute scenarios with measurable targets for answer quality, latency, cost, privacy and human oversight.

  2. Performance

    Design an end-to-end architecture with context, container and data-flow views and a per-request latency and cost budget.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate at least three solution options, including a non-generative baseline, and record the resulting decisions.

  4. Performance

    Design a shared AI platform covering model admission, gateway, tenancy, golden paths and cost attribution.

  5. Performance

    Design a reference architecture with fitness functions that check conformance automatically.

  6. Performance

    Review a peer's architecture with a structured trade-off method and decide on a requested deviation.

  5. Day 5 Capstone project presentations

More about this course

Topics

Requirements to scenarios

  • Drivers, constraints and stakeholders
  • Quality attributes for AI: answer quality, latency, cost, privacy, oversight
  • Classification and transfer rules as constraints

Views, patterns and integration

  • Views as code
  • Retrieval, agentic, fine-tuned, classical, batch and event-driven patterns
  • Integration through APIs and events

Options and decisions

  • Weighted criteria and their pitfalls
  • Three-year total cost and sensitivity
  • Lock-in, exit and procurement requirements
  • Decision records with review triggers

The platform as a product

  • Demand matrix and phasing
  • Model admission on evaluation and licence evidence
  • Gateway: credentials, quotas, fallback, tool mediation, audit
  • Tenancy and showback before chargeback

Reference designs and reviews

  • Golden paths with gates on by default
  • Fitness functions
  • Structured trade-off reviews and deviations

Tools and platforms

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks, AI-off: write a scenario, find the missing consequence in a decision record, place a classified data flow (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: scenarios and views 20, options and decisions 25, platform slice 25, fitness checks 15, review 15 (60%)
  • Panel defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the first scenario draft. AI-tutor in the labs: it questions assumptions and points to missing views but does not write decisions. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended before the panel.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
