AE-490

AI Systems Capstone: Production Readiness Review

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Studio
  • 60 h
  • In development

Each participant takes one existing AI system through a production readiness review and decides whether it may go live. The system is a sanitised copy from the participant's organisation, used with the owner's written approval, or the Academy reference system: the shipment-exceptions agent of the fictional Dune Line Freight company, with retrieval, tools and seeded weaknesses, run in a sandbox. Participants reconstruct the architecture, judge the model and retrieval choices, re-run its evaluations, load-test, fault-inject and cost it, threat-model and probe it, audit its governance evidence, and write a go, conditional-go or no-go recommendation defended before a panel.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Engineers who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a production AI system they built, operated and can defend.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Review the architecture of an existing AI system and the fitness of its model, retrieval and adaptation choices against its requirements.

  2. Performance

    Audit the system's evaluation evidence and re-run its critical evaluations with stated uncertainty.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate reliability, rollback readiness and cost against service objectives by load testing, fault injection and a rehearsed rollback.

  4. Performance

    Assess security by threat modelling, sandboxed probing and checks of supply-chain evidence.

  5. Performance

    Audit governance evidence for classification, personal data flows, risk assessment, human oversight, incidents and retirement.

  6. Performance

    Decide and defend a go, conditional-go or no-go recommendation with conditions, owners and dates.

Format and days

Format
Studio: about eight weeks part-time, an individual production readiness review with four coached review rounds and a panel defence
Total hours
60 h

More about this course

Topics

The readiness review as a practice

  • Scope, independence and evidence standards
  • Checklists versus judgement
  • Findings with severity, owners and dates

Architecture and model fitness

  • Requirements and decision records
  • Model, retrieval and adaptation choices
  • Known model limits and fallbacks

Evaluation, reliability and cost

  • Audit and re-run of evaluation suites
  • Service objectives for latency and quality
  • Load testing to a goodput target and fault injection
  • Cost per successful outcome and capacity assumptions

Security and governance

  • Threat models and sandboxed probes
  • Bills of materials and signing
  • Classification, personal data flows and transfers
  • Risk assessment, cards, oversight, incidents and retirement

The decision

  • Go, conditional go and no-go
  • Conditions with owners and dates
  • Briefing decision makers

Cost modelling
a spreadsheet, a Python notebook

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised written check without AI assistance on review methods: evaluation statistics, threat modelling and risk scoring (15%)
  • Four coached review rounds scored on a rubric (25%)
  • Readiness dossier scored on a rubric covering all seven domains (35%)
  • Panel defence including the response to an unseen change (25%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the dossier and the defence; a well-evidenced no-go recommendation can pass

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised written check and for answers given in coached review rounds. AI-tutor for the guided self-study on review methods. AI-full in the review work, including agents for log analysis and probing, provided every agent transcript is kept and every finding is verified and defended by the participant.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
