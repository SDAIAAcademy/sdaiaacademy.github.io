# USE-EDU-202 · Delivering the Cross-Disciplinary Data and AI Curriculum

- Track: Data & AI Fluency
- Path: [Learners and Educators](../paths/USE-EDU.md)
- Level: Level 200 · Apply
- Format: Workshop: two days with labs, guided pre-work and a micro-teach
- Total hours: 16 h
- Audience: practitioner
- Status: In development

## Description

Faculty prepare to teach the national cross-disciplinary data and AI curriculum: they choose stand-alone, embedded or distributed delivery and map it to the outcomes, adapt the recommended labs, build a discipline track pack, calibrate marking of use-case plans and teach one component. Wider assessment redesign is USE-EDU-201.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Explain the six components, the six intended learning outcomes and their mapping to international frameworks.
- Prepare an alignment matrix from a chosen delivery model to the intended and component outcomes.
- Adapt one recommended lab and build a track pack with a local case and synthetic mini-dataset.
- Apply the curriculum's assessment principles to mark student use-case plans.
- Deliver a ten-minute hands-on micro-teach of one component.

## Prerequisites

- [USE-EDU-101 · Data and AI Fluency for University Students](USE-EDU-101.md)
- Or an AI-off entry check on the six components
- Teaches or will teach a university course

## Topics

### The curriculum and its options

- Intended and component outcomes
- Stand-alone, embedded or distributed delivery
- Optional enrichment topics

### Teaching by doing

- Prediction versus reality, red-team challenge, coverage test
- Workflow map, prompt tournament, data-governance and tool-risk labs
- Effortful labs feel less effective

### Track packs

- The five listed tracks and their rules
- New packs for health, business, law, engineering and education

### Assessment

- Verification, disclosure, data safety, cultural and context sensitivity
- Marking calibration and moderation
