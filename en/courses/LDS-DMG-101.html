LDS-DMG-101

Data Management Foundations

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Intensive
  • 4 days · 24 h
  • Available

Participants learn the knowledge areas of data management by producing the first artefact of each for a fictional public services agency. From synthetic system extracts and interview notes they build an asset register, write glossary entries, trace one dataset's lineage, run simple quality checks and propose provisional classifications, placing each artefact against the domains of a national standard. They finish with a maturity snapshot and a first-year improvement plan.

Who it is for

Audience: beginner

Data management, quality, governance, privacy, compliance, classification and access staff, and law, risk and business graduates moving into these roles; no programming is assumed.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Describe the knowledge areas of data management with the main artefact and accountable role for each.

  2. Awareness

    Distinguish the roles of a governance structure, from the committee to business and technical stewards.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a data asset register and business glossary entries for one business domain.

  4. Applied

    Apply quality dimensions and provisional classification levels to registered assets.

  5. Applied

    Apply a six-level maturity scale to score an organisation's data management and prepare a ranked list of first-year actions.

More about this course

Topics

What data management is

  • Data as an asset: value, cost and risk
  • Knowledge areas and how a national standard groups them into domains
  • Governance versus management
  • The lifecycle from creation to disposal

Roles

  • Data management office and committee
  • Chief data officer and governance, compliance, privacy and open data officers
  • Data owners, business stewards and technical stewards

Core artefacts

  • Asset register and catalogue
  • Business glossary and data dictionary
  • Lineage diagrams
  • Quality rules, issue log and classification register

Protecting and sharing data

  • Classification by impact of disclosure
  • Personal data principles: lawful basis, purpose limitation, minimisation
  • Sharing, open data and information requests
  • What staff may enter into AI tools

Measuring progress

  • Maturity scales and evidence
  • Compliance versus maturity
  • Data readiness for AI use cases

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised scenario test without AI assistance: match 12 situations to knowledge area, artefact and role (30%)
  • Graded lab artefacts (30%)
  • Capstone snapshot and plan (40%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the scenario test

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario test and the first glossary entries. AI-tutor in the other labs, set to question definitions rather than write them. AI-full for drafting the capstone plan, which the participant presents and answers for.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
