## Description

A train-the-trainer workshop for nominated AI champions. Participants plan and run a 45-minute clinic on colleagues' real tasks, teach a short lesson with AI-off practice and give feedback on requests and checks. They curate a shared library of requests and delegation cards, measure use and value beyond self-report, and agree how to handle low-quality AI output passed between colleagues. Leaders' adoption programmes are LDS-EXE-204.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Prepare a 45-minute clinic plan with a short lesson, a worked example, an AI-off task and safe task collection.
- Lead a clinic and give feedback that improves colleagues' requests and checks.
- Configure a shared library of requests, standing context and delegation cards with owner, data rules and review date.
- Measure use and value with indicators, a survey and a time sample rather than self-report alone.
- Deliver a 30-day adoption report on clinics, library use, measured change and a unit agreement on checking AI work before sharing it.

## Topics

### The champion role

- Champion, trainer and manager
- Organisational conditions over individual skill
- Escalation beyond the role

### Teaching adults

- Worked examples, then AI-off practice
- Tutor mode versus answer mode
- Reluctant and over-confident colleagues

### Clinics and libraries

- Collecting tasks without confidential data
- Review dates and retirement

### Measuring and sharing

- Why self-report overstates gains
- Time samples
- Returning low-quality output without blame
