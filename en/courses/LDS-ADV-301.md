# LDS-ADV-301 · Data Products, Valuation and Monetisation

- Track: Executives & Leaders
- Path: [Advisory and Value](../paths/LDS-ADV.md)
- Level: Level 300 · Develop
- Format: Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
- Total hours: 30 h
- Audience: specialist
- Status: In development

## Description

Participants take a fictional transport authority's synthetic ridership, ticketing and fleet-location data from asset to priced product. They score assets for readiness, analyse demand, design a product, value an asset three ways and set licence terms and prices. They test each proposal's lawfulness, including a re-identification test on a supposedly anonymised extract, and model break-even and sensitivity. The capstone is a business case defended before a mock approvals committee.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Classify data assets by expected value and assess their readiness for productisation.
- Analyse demand, beneficiary segments and substitutes to define use cases for a data product.
- Compute the value of a data asset by cost, market and income approaches and reconcile them.
- Design a product, licence and pricing model that balances cost, value, demand and service level.
- Evaluate product proposals against personal data, classification, sharing and intellectual property conditions and decide approvability.
- Build a business case with forecast, costs, break-even, sensitivity and portfolio indicators, and recommend launch, redesign or stop.

## Prerequisites

- [LDS-DMG-101 · Data Management Foundations](LDS-DMG-101.md)
- Builds a spreadsheet financial model with net present value and scenarios
- States what data classification and sharing rules permit

## Topics

### Data as products

- Improve, wrap and sell
- Datasets, interfaces, analytical products and insight services
- Products rather than raw data

### Readiness and demand

- Asset inventory and readiness criteria
- Beneficiary segments, demand evidence and substitutes
- Partners and delivery channels

### Valuation and pricing

- Cost, market and income approaches
- Non-rival goods with near-zero marginal cost
- Subscription, tiered, usage-based and licence pricing

### Licences and regulatory conditions

- Licence terms, including use for AI training
- Anonymisation limits and re-identification risk
- Classification, sharing rules and intellectual property
- Monetisation governance: roles, approval paths and escalation

### Business case and portfolio

- Forecasts, break-even and sensitivity
- Portfolio indicators: revenue, cost, usage, satisfaction
- Repricing and retirement
