## Description

Participants lead the assurance of AI systems that act on an organisation's data. For the fictional Northern Region Benefits Agency (a bilingual benefits assistant and a case-worker agent that reads case files and drafts letters) they plan and run a red-team exercise in Arabic and English, manually and with automated probes, assess the risks with the National AI Risk Management Framework, review the security architecture for agent identity, least privilege, isolation and egress, and assemble an assurance case that ends in a residual-risk briefing. Targets run in a sandbox on synthetic case data. Hardening a single application is AE-308.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Plan a red-team exercise with objectives tied to harms, scope, rules of engagement and stop conditions.
- Conduct manual and automated adversarial testing in Arabic and English and quantify attack success over repeated trials.
- Assess the risks of an agent with the national framework, from context to treatment and residual sign-off.
- Review a security architecture for agent identity, delegated authority, least privilege, isolation and egress control.
- Build an assurance case that links each claim to evidence and maps the evidence to a control framework.
- Present residual risk and a recommendation with conditions to an accountable executive.

## Format and days

### Day 1: Plan the exercise

- Assurance is an argument with evidence
- Scoping from the threat model
- Objectives, scope and rules of engagement
- Choosing techniques from catalogues

### Day 2: Run the red team

- Manual probing in two languages
- Indirect injection and goal hijack
- Automated campaigns over repeated trials
- Findings people can act on

### Day 3: Assess the risk

- The national framework as method
- Scoring and calibration
- Treatment and residual risk
- Supplier and model-change risk

### Day 4: Review and assemble the evidence

- Security architecture review
- Identity and delegated authority
- Assurance evidence packs
- Briefing the accountable executive

### Day 5: Capstone: an assurance pack and its defence


## Topics

### Assurance as argument

- Claims, arguments and evidence
- Independence of testers and reviewers
- Evidence that defeats a claim

### Red-team practice

- Objectives, rules of engagement and stop conditions
- Technique catalogues: OWASP lists and MITRE ATLAS
- Arabic, dialect and transliteration attacks
- Automated probing and success over trials
- Findings turned into regression tests

### Risk assessment with the national framework

- Context and scope
- The seven risk categories
- Likelihood and impact scoring and calibration
- Treatment and residual sign-off
- Supplier and model-change risk

### Security architecture review

- Agent identity and delegated authority
- Least privilege derived from data classification
- Isolation tiers and egress control
- Action-level monitoring and kill switches

### Evidence and reporting

- Crosswalks to ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF and the CSA AI Controls Matrix
- Assurance cases and gap lists
- Briefing accountable executives
