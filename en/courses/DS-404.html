DS-404

Advanced Modelling for Structured Data

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Data Science

Participants decide when pretrained models, ensembles and decision-aware pipelines earn their place in structured-data work. On a fictional food wholesaler's synthetic orders and demand, they test pretrained tabular models and zero-shot forecasters against tuned baselines, stack ensembles, turn forecasts into orders by optimisation, score models by decision cost and validate under the shift of a new depot region. They end with an adoption decision weighing accuracy, licence, cost and data residency.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Data scientists who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a defended analysis with a validated model or a causal estimate that they can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Evaluate a pretrained tabular model against a tuned, ensembled boosted-tree baseline across training-set sizes.

  2. Performance

    Evaluate zero-shot pretrained forecasters, with and without covariates, against statistical and tuned learned benchmarks by rolling-origin backtests.

  3. Applied

    Implement a stacked ensemble from out-of-fold or rolling-origin predictions and measure its improvement over the best single model.

  4. Performance

    Build a predict-then-optimise pipeline and judge forecasters by the decision cost they cause.

  5. Performance

    Diagnose distribution shift between training and deployment data and validate a model under it.

  6. Performance

    Decide whether to adopt a pretrained model for one task, weighing accuracy, cost, licence and data residency.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Pretrained models for tables

    1. Tuned boosted trees first
    2. In-context learning on tables
    3. Learning curves decide
    4. Reading a leaderboard claim

  2. Day 2 Forecasting with pretrained models

    1. Zero-shot against a benchmark floor
    2. Where zero-shot loses
    3. Holidays that move
    4. Ensembles worth their cost

  3. Day 3 From forecast to order

    1. Asymmetric costs
    2. Optimising under capacity
    3. Scoring by regret
    4. Uncertainty in the plan

  4. Day 4 Shift and adoption

    1. Detecting shift
    2. Validating under shift
    3. Correcting for shift
    4. Adoption conditions

  5. Day 5 Capstone project presentations

More about this course

Topics

Pretrained models for tables and time series

  • In-context learning on tables and its size limits
  • Zero-shot forecasting with covariates
  • Intermittent and short series
  • Leaderboard claims and tuning budgets

Ensembling and decisions

  • Out-of-fold and rolling-origin stacking
  • Newsvendor logic
  • Integer programmes for orders
  • Decision regret

Validation under shift

  • Covariate, label and concept shift
  • Adversarial, grouped and temporal validation
  • Weighted conformal intervals

Adoption conditions

  • Licences and permitted use
  • Data residency and classification
  • Subgroup error after shift

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Pretrained tabular models
TabICL, TabPFN family (non-commercial weights)
Pretrained forecasters
Chronos family, TimesFM (non-commercial weights)
Baselines and ensembling
LightGBM, StatsForecast, AutoGluon
Optimisation modelling and solvers
Pyomo or PuLP with HiGHS, OR-Tools
Conformal prediction
MAPIE, course-provided implementations

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised check: choose a validation scheme for a described shift, compute a newsvendor quantity, read a learning curve (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: fair comparison 25, validation under shift 25, ensemble or decision analysis 20, adoption reasoning 15, reproducibility 15 (60%)
  • Defence before two assessors (20%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the capstone rubric and on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and the day 1 baseline. AI-tutor in labs: hints, not finished pipelines. AI-full in the capstone, with a verification note on assisted steps and a live defence.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
