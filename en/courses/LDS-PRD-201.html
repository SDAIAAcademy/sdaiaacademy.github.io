LDS-PRD-201

AI Product Discovery and Specification

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 24 h
  • In development

Participants take a fictional permits service from a vague request to add AI to a specification engineers can build and test against. They mine synthetic interviews and 500 synthetic tickets, choose a problem by frequency, value and cost of error, test feasibility with a prompt-level prototype on 30 cases, and write behaviour rules, pass or fail acceptance criteria, a first evaluation set and a data-needs statement.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Product managers and owners, service and interaction designers, and delivery leads who own AI features or services, and engineers moving into product roles.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish problems suited to generative models, predictive models, rules or no automation.

  2. Applied

    Analyse research material into jobs, pain points and workarounds ranked by frequency and cost of error.

  3. Applied

    Test a candidate feature with a prompt-level prototype on representative cases.

  4. Applied

    Draft a behaviour specification with scope, refusal and escalation rules and pass or fail acceptance criteria.

  5. Applied

    Prepare a first evaluation set covering normal, edge and must-refuse cases.

  6. Performance

    Defend a specification pack and its statement of data sources, access, classification and gaps.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
24 h

Prerequisites

  • Has written user stories or requirements for a digital product or service
  • Can say in plain words why a language model's output can be fluent and wrong

More about this course

Topics

Framing AI product problems

  • Outcome before feature
  • A value metric and its baseline before build
  • What models do well: draft, extract, classify, rank, act
  • When rules or no automation win
  • Cost of a wrong answer

Discovery with users

  • Interviews and observation
  • Mining tickets, searches and logs
  • Synthesis with and without AI

Feasibility with a prototype

  • Prompt-level prototypes
  • Representative cases versus demonstrations
  • Surfacing uneven capability
  • Build approach: prompting, retrieval, adaptation or buying

Evaluation criteria as the specification

  • Behaviour rules, refusals and escalation
  • Acceptance criteria as binary checks
  • Defining success early versus writing checks for observed errors

Data needs

  • Data for grounding, evaluation and adaptation
  • Classification and personal data in sources
  • Access owners and lead times

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Language model access for prototypes
an open-weight model served locally with llama.cpp, a hosted open-weight model, any approved commercial assistant
Qualitative coding and affinity mapping
LibreOffice Calc, any shared whiteboard
Evaluation set store and runner
a CSV file in Git, promptfoo, Inspect

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check: triage eight unseen requests and write criteria for one (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone pack on a five-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the check and the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first synthesis pass. AI-tutor in the labs, set to question specifications rather than write them. AI-full for prototypes and capstone drafting, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
