USE-FLU-104

Safe Use of AI at Work: Data, Privacy and Deception

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Primer
  • 4 days · 4 h
  • Available

Participants practise the safe-use decisions that most often go wrong at work. They classify ten work items and decide which tool tier may receive each, compare provider terms on storage and training use, minimise a request that contains personal data, find hidden instructions planted in an email, a web page and a shared file that an assistant will read, and run a call-back check on a simulated deepfake voice and video request for a payment. They finish with an incident report for a case in which confidential data reached an unapproved tool.

Who it is for

Audience: beginner

Staff in any role and sector, from new hires to managers, who use or will use dashboards, AI assistants and agents in routine work; no technical background is assumed and delivery is Arabic-first in the Kingdom.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Classify workplace material against a data classification scheme and decide which tool tier, if any, may receive it.

  2. Awareness

    Explain how prompts, uploaded files and outputs may be stored, reviewed or used for training under provider and organisational terms.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a minimised version of a request that keeps the task intact while removing or pseudonymising personal data.

  4. Awareness

    Identify signs of prompt injection in content that an assistant or agent reads, and describe the safe response.

  5. Applied

    Apply an out-of-band verification routine to a suspected deepfake request involving money, credentials or data.

  6. Awareness

    Describe the response to a data or AI incident: stop, record, report to the right contact and prevent a repeat.

More about this course

Topics

Classify before you share

  • Four-level data classification schemes
  • Personal and sensitive personal data
  • Tool tiers: public consumer, approved enterprise, local or on-premises
  • Matching data to tool tier

What happens to what you type

  • Storage, logs and retention
  • Use of inputs for model training
  • Who can see shared chats and files
  • Settings that change what is kept
  • Where data are processed and rules on transfer abroad
  • Meeting note-takers, browser extensions and plug-ins that read mail, files or screens

Minimise and pseudonymise

  • Remove, generalise or replace identifiers
  • Keep the task, drop the person
  • When not to use AI at all

Prompt injection for users

  • Hidden instructions in emails, pages, files and images
  • Why assistants and agents follow them
  • Safe responses: stop, do not follow, report
  • Permissions that limit the damage

Deepfakes and AI-enabled fraud

  • Voice clones and video impersonation
  • Urgent requests for money, credentials or data
  • Out-of-band verification and agreed code words
  • Synthetic documents and screenshots

Disclosure and incidents

  • Disclosing AI assistance and labelling synthetic media
  • Provenance labels and watermarks, and why a missing label proves nothing
  • What to do after a mistake
  • Reporting routes

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Identifier detection and pseudonymisation helper
a facilitator-hosted demonstration of an open-source detector such as Presidio, with added rules for local identifier formats, the find, replace and review features of any word processor
Content provenance and image checks
a Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) Content Credentials inspector, reverse image search such as TinEye
Sandboxed assistant for injection examples
a locally served open-weight model behind Open WebUI, an approved enterprise assistant with a test workspace
Simulated fraud request delivery
pre-recorded synthetic voice and video clips with a facilitator role-play script, played in the learning platform, the organisation's security-awareness platform

How the work is assessed

  • Scenario test of 15 items, AI-off (60%)
  • Marked classification exercise (25%)
  • Fraud-response and incident-report exercises (15%)
To pass
70% overall and at least eight of ten items correctly classified

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario test and the classification exercise. AI-full only inside the injection lab's sandboxed assistant, which holds synthetic content and is the object of study; no work data enters any tool.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
