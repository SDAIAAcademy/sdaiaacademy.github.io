DS-407

Leading Analytical Work: Review, Standards and Stakeholders

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 4 days · 21 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Data Science

Participants lead the analytics unit of the fictional Ras Sahil Ports Authority: twelve analysts whose work increasingly starts as an assistant's draft. They write a proportionate quality-assurance standard and review checklist, re-run and review colleagues' analyses, set the unit's rules for AI-assisted work, turn review findings into feedback, and negotiate scope, deadline and evidence with role-played sponsors, one of whom asks for an unfavourable result to be dropped. DS-310 teaches reviewing one analysis; this course sets the standards and runs the system.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Data scientists who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a defended analysis with a validated model or a causal estimate that they can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design a proportionate quality-assurance standard for analytical work, with assurance levels, a review checklist and sign-off rules.

  2. Performance

    Review analyses by others, including assistant-drafted ones, rank findings by their effect on the decision and calibrate severity with a second reviewer.

  3. Performance

    Design rules for AI-assisted analysis covering verification, disclosure and the process evidence each analyst keeps, and audit the unit's work against them.

  4. Applied

    Prepare feedback and a development plan for an analyst from review findings.

  5. Performance

    Lead a negotiation with a sponsor on scope, deadline and evidence threshold and record the agreement.

  6. Performance

    Decide how to respond to pressure to change or suppress a finding, and record the reasons.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
21 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Set the standard

    1. Assurance proportionate to risk
    2. A team review checklist
    3. Reproducibility as a team rule
    4. Rules for AI-assisted analysis

  2. Day 2 Review and develop

    1. Calibrating reviewers
    2. Auditing assisted work
    3. Feedback that builds skill
    4. Review capacity and mentoring

  3. Day 3 Work with sponsors

    1. Scope and deadline
    2. Agreeing the evidence first
    3. Pressure on a finding
    4. Reporting quality and correcting errors

  4. Day 4 Capstone: the staffing pack and the review meeting

More about this course

Topics

Analytical quality assurance

  • Assurance proportionate to risk and use
  • Analyst, reviewer and approver roles
  • Published guides such as the Aqua Book and the REFORMS checklist
  • Correcting errors found after release

Review practice

  • Question fit, data, method, inference, communication
  • Re-running before reading
  • Severity by effect on the decision
  • Calibrating reviewers

AI-assisted work and developing analysts

  • Verification and disclosure rules
  • Auditing assisted work against the rules
  • Feedback that names the reasoning
  • Keeping unaided practice in the work

Sponsors and evidence

  • Scope, deadline and answerability
  • Agreeing the evidence that would change the decision
  • Pressure on findings and escalation
  • Quality statements in board papers

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: review a short seeded-flaw analysis in 40 minutes (20%)
  • Capstone pack rubric: standard 20, review findings 30, analysis agreement 20, quality statement 20, clarity 10 (50%)
  • Chaired review meeting observed against a rubric (30%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on each component

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and first-pass reviews on day 2, so reviewers form their own judgement first. AI-tutor when drafting standards and feedback. AI-full in the capstone pack, defended while chairing the meeting live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
