## Description

Participants make one small build lawful and responsible: a parent-enquiry assistant for a fictional private school group that drafts replies from attendance and fee records with a language model. They map its personal data and minimise it under the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), classify each data set under the National Data Management Office (NDMO) policy, decide what may reach an external model, turn the SDAIA AI Ethics Principles into tests, assess risk with the National AI Risk Management Framework, and write the disclosure and records the build needs.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Explain the PDPL obligations that govern a build, from lawful basis and minimisation to rights, retention, transfer and breach handling.
- Distinguish what the data classification, data sharing and open data policies and the SDAIA Generative AI Guidelines each decide.
- Classify each data set of the build under the four NDMO classification levels and derive the rule for sending it to an external model service.
- Apply minimisation and pseudonymisation to the data the assistant sends, and test whether records can be re-identified.
- Prepare a risk assessment that places the assistant in an ethics risk tier and works through the four phases of the national risk framework.
- Test the assistant against the ethics principles for fairness across Arabic and English enquiries, transparency and human review, and draft the disclosure parents see.

## Topics

### Personal data in a build

- Personal data in prompts, logs and stores
- Lawful basis, purpose and minimisation
- Rights, retention, transfer and breaches
- Records of processing activities

### Classification and sharing

- Four levels and impact assessment
- Handling rules for external services
- Sharing with another entity
- What may be published as open data

### Ethics principles as tests

- The seven SDAIA AI Ethics Principles
- Risk tiers from little or no risk to unacceptable
- Fairness across languages and groups, with the AI Bias Reference Guide
- Disclosure and human review

### Generative AI and risk

- Generative AI guidelines for government and public use
- The four phases of the national risk framework
- Likelihood, impact and treatment
- Licences of models and data
