AE-310

Model Adaptation: Fine-Tuning, Distillation and Small Models

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: AI Engineering

Participants decide when adapting a model is worth it and then do it well. For a fictional chamber of commerce whose bilingual registration requests must stay on its own servers, a prompted small open-weight model fails at field extraction and tool calls. They sort the failures, set a stronger prompted baseline, build a tuning set from traces, staff corrections and filtered teacher outputs, train low-rank adapters, distil a larger model's tool-call behaviour, evaluate against the base and a larger prompted model, and serve the result quantised with several adapters. AE-301 introduces adapters on an encoder; post-training at scale is AE-406.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Engineers who have completed the level 200 set or can show equivalent work: a deployed model or language model application they built and can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Analyse the failures of a prompted model and assign each failure class to prompting, retrieval, tuning or distillation.

  2. Applied

    Prepare a tuning dataset from traces, corrections and teacher outputs, with template rendering, verifier filtering, deduplication, decontamination and personal-data removal.

  3. Applied

    Configure and train low-rank adapters on a 16-bit and a 4-bit base model within the memory of one free-tier GPU.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate the adapted model against the base and a larger prompted model with paired statistics, language slices, regression and safety checks.

  5. Performance

    Deploy a quantised small model with several adapters and measure throughput, latency and cost per thousand requests.

  6. Performance

    Decide whether to ship the adapted model, weighing quality, cost, licence terms and data placement.

Format and days

Format

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Decide whether to adapt

    1. Prompt, retrieve, tune or distil
    2. The baseline tuning must beat

    4. Cost and placement of the options

  2. Day 2 Data for tuning

    1. Tuning data from traces and corrections
    2. Teacher generation and verifier filtering
    3. Chat templates and tool-call formats
    4. Deduplication, decontamination and personal data

  3. Day 3 Train adapters and distil

    1. Low-rank adapters
    2. Quantised adapters and memory
    3. Settings that matter
    4. Distilling a behaviour

  4. Day 4 Evaluate and serve

    1. Paired evaluation against the base
    2. Regressions and safety after tuning
    3. One base, several adapters
    4. Cards and the decision memo

  5. Day 5 Capstone: an adapted small model and its defence

More about this course

Topics

When to adapt

  • Prompt, retrieve, tune or distil
  • Tuning changes behaviour more than knowledge
  • Narrow tasks small models suit and multi-step work they do not
  • Licences and acceptable-use terms
  • Cost and placement of each option

Data for tuning

  • Traces and staff corrections
  • Teacher generation with verifier filtering
  • Chat templates and tool-call formats
  • Deduplication, decontamination and personal data

Adapters and distillation

  • Low-rank and quantised adapters
  • Rank, learning rate and schedule
  • Distilling a behaviour from a larger model

Evaluating adapted models

  • Paired comparison with the base and a larger model
  • Arabic and English slices
  • Forgetting and regressions
  • Safety behaviour after tuning

Serving small models

  • Quantised weight formats
  • Several adapters on one base model
  • Throughput and cost per thousand requests

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks without AI assistance on adaptation choices, data faults and memory arithmetic (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a five-criterion rubric: adaptation decision 15, data quality 25, training 15, evaluation 30, serving and cost 15 (60%)
  • Live defence of the decision memo (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the first failure analysis. AI-tutor in the labs: the assistant explains library calls and errors but does not write the data pipeline. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
