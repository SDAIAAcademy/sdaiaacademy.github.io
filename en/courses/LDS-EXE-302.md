## Description

Learning and talent leaders decide which assessments can still certify skill when every candidate has an AI assistant. Participants watch a hosted model attempt a synthetic version of their own unsupervised test, redesign it into a supervised lane and an open, defended lane, map credentials to what each can honestly claim, and plan how to measure a programme's effect. Classroom assessment is USE-EDU-201.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Test how much of an existing assessment a hosted model can pass without the candidate's own skill.
- Design a two-lane assessment with a supervised check and an open task backed by process evidence and a defence.
- Classify credentials by what they evidence, how they are verified and what they may claim.
- Design an evaluation of a programme's effect on job performance with a comparison group.

## Prerequisites

- Has designed, commissioned or run a training or assessment programme
- Reads a pass-rate table and says what a gap between two groups shows

## Topics

### What AI changed

- AI-written answers in unsupervised exams
- Why detectors fail and whom they harm
- Unguided AI practice and later unaided skill

### Secure where you certify, open where you teach

- Supervised checks, practical exams and oral defences
- Process evidence and AI rules per activity

### Credentials and claims

- Completion, assessed, stacked and proctored credentials
- Verifiable badges with skills alignment and evidence
- AI in marking and proctoring: disclosure and human review
- Cut scores and reliability near the pass mark

### Evaluating training effect

- Levels from reaction to results and return
- Comparison groups and selection effects
