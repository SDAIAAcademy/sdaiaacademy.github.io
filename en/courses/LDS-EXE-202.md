## Description

Participants cost AI the way it is billed and used. From 50 labelled runs of a hosted agent workflow they compute cost per successful task as a distribution, price it under token, capacity and per-outcome schemes, measure which levers change cost and quality, and write a cost governance plan.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Explain why billed tokens exceed user input and why price per token misstates workflow cost.
- Compute cost per successful task, including retries, failed runs and human review time.
- Compare token, capacity and per-seat or per-outcome pricing for one workflow at three volumes.
- Measure how routing, caching, shorter context and fewer retries change cost and success rate.
- Design a cost governance plan with unit-cost indicators, budgets, alerts and decision rights.

## Topics

### How AI is priced

- Input, output, cached and reasoning tokens
- Capacity and utilisation
- Seats, credits, outcomes, commitments (prices in a dated annex)

### Unit economics

- Cost per successful outcome: median and tail
- Hidden costs: review, integration, evaluation, monitoring
- Build versus run budgets

### Why agents cost more

- Loops, tool calls, growing history, retries
- Falling unit prices, rising spend

### Levers and governance

- Routing, smaller models, caching, batching
- Spend limits on runaway runs
- Governance stages; unit costs in leadership reports
