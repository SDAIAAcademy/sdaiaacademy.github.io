# SPC-ANL-301 · Data Storytelling and Decision Briefings

- Track: Data Science
- Path: [Analysis and Business Intelligence](../paths/SPC-ANL.md)
- Level: Level 300 · Develop
- Format: Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
- Total hours: 24 h
- Audience: practitioner
- Status: In development

## Description

Participants turn a completed analysis into a recommendation a decision maker can act on. They identify the decision and audience, structure a briefing that gives the answer first, redesign charts to explain rather than explore, and state uncertainty, assumptions and risks in plain words with ranges and natural frequencies. They write a two-page decision memo and rehearse against hard questions. The course ends with a live briefing to a panel playing decision makers. Framing the question before analysis is SPC-MOD-307; general technical writing is FND-AIC-106.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Analyse a decision context to identify the decision maker, options, criteria and the evidence that would change the choice.
- Draft a storyline that puts the answer first and supports it with grouped, ordered reasons.
- Prepare explanatory charts whose titles state the finding, with annotation matched to the message.
- Draft statements of uncertainty, assumptions and risks in plain words using ranges, natural frequencies and scenarios.
- Deliver a decision memo stating recommendation, evidence, uncertainty, alternatives considered and what to monitor after the decision.
- Defend a recommendation in a live briefing under questioning, stating what is not known.

## Prerequisites

- [SPC-ANL-201 · Exploratory Analysis and Visualisation](SPC-ANL-201.md)
- Reads a confidence interval and states what it does and does not mean
- Brings a completed analysis of public or synthetic data, or uses one provided

## Topics

### From analysis to recommendation

- Decision, options and criteria
- What the audience knows, needs and worries about
- From finding to claim to recommendation
- Counter-evidence and selective emphasis

### Structure and charts

- Answer first, then reasons
- Situation, complication, question, answer
- Titles that state the finding
- Annotation, emphasis and bilingual layouts

### Uncertainty in plain words

- Ranges and natural frequencies
- How readers misread verbal probability terms
- What would change the conclusion

### Delivery

- Spoken structure and timing
- Handling challenge and saying what is not known
- Checking every number in AI-drafted material
