## Description

Participants take the bilingual visitor assistant of a fictional national events authority, too slow at evening peaks and too costly, and make it meet its service objective at the lowest cost they can defend. They build a first-principles cost model, measure goodput curves on replayed traffic, diagnose seeded bottlenecks, then apply batching, caching, quantisation, speculative decoding, distillation, routing, disaggregated serving and autoscaling, re-running an Arabic and English task evaluation after every change.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Compute prefill cost, decode cost and key-value cache memory, and predict the binding resource.
- Evaluate a serving configuration by measuring its goodput curve over concurrency against latency objectives.
- Diagnose why a deployment misses its service objective and confirm the fix.
- Optimise batching, admission, prefix caching and cache precision while holding task quality.
- Evaluate quantised, distilled and speculative-decoding variants against the base model and decide which to serve.
- Design and defend a serving topology covering disaggregation, routing, replica count and scaling policy.

## Topics

### The cost model of inference

- Prefill is compute-bound, decode is memory-bound
- Key-value cache arithmetic
- Roofline and queueing reasoning

### Measuring serving performance

- Time to first token, time per output token, goodput
- Profiling where a request's time goes
- Trace replay and load patterns
- Pareto curves and benchmark validity

### Inside the engine

- Continuous batching, chunked prefill, admission control
- Paged attention, cache precision and tiering
- Prefix and semantic caching; tenant isolation

### Cheaper models at the same quality

- Quantisation formats per accelerator
- Speculative decoding and acceptance rates
- Distillation and multi-adapter serving

### Across replicas and over the day

- Disaggregated prefill and decode
- Cache-aware routing
- Autoscaling, cold starts and scale to zero
- Cost per successful request and energy per token
