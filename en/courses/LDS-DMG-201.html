LDS-DMG-201

Data Governance Operating Model and Stewardship

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 24 h
  • In development

Participants design how governance will run in a fictional organisation of 4,000 staff whose data office exists on paper. They choose an operating model, write charters for the governance bodies, assign decision rights for 20 recurring data decisions, design the steward network and map national requirements to internal controls. They then write a three-year data strategy tied to business objectives and a costed business case for the office, and defend both before a mock executive committee. Policy drafting craft is LDS-DMG-302.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Data management, quality, governance, privacy, compliance, classification and access staff, and law, risk and business graduates moving into these roles; no programming is assumed.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Compare centralised, federated and hybrid governance operating models and when each works.

  2. Applied

    Prepare charters for the committee, the data office and the steward network.

  3. Applied

    Apply a decision-rights matrix to recurring data decisions.

  4. Applied

    Prepare a governance scorecard that traces national requirements to controls, owners and indicators.

  5. Performance

    Build a data strategy and a costed business case for a data office.

More about this course

Topics

Foundations

  • Board-level principles from the international standard on governance of data (ISO/IEC 38505-1)
  • Capability frameworks and benchmarks for data offices
  • Operating models: centralised, federated, hybrid

Structures and stewardship

  • Committee and office charters
  • Owners, business stewards and technical stewards
  • Selecting stewards and protecting their time

Decision rights and controls

  • Recurring decisions and who holds them
  • Responsibility matrices and escalation
  • Policy hierarchy, exceptions and waivers
  • From requirement to control and indicator

Strategy and business case

  • Data objectives tied to business objectives
  • Use cases as the unit of value
  • Costs, benefits, assumptions and sensitivity

Running the programme

  • Key performance and key risk indicators
  • Compliance and maturity cycles
  • Building a governance culture
  • First committee rulings on data used by AI (depth in LDS-DMG-301)

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised case test without AI assistance: assign rights and escalation for eight unseen situations (20%)
  • Graded lab artefacts (30%)
  • Capstone pack and defence (50%)
To pass
70% overall, and at least 60% on the case test and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the case test and the first decision-rights matrix. AI-tutor in the labs, set to challenge assumptions rather than supply them. AI-full for drafting the capstone; the participant answers for every clause and figure.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
