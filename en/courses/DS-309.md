## Description

Participants take the synthetic applicant data and eligibility-screening model of Yusr Housing Support Fund, a fictional public fund, through the checks a release needs: they measure re-identification risk and de-identify the table, publish differentially private counts under a budget, test a synthetic extract for leakage, audit subgroup errors against the bias types in SDAIA's AI Bias Reference Guide, and write the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs. Calibration and explanation are DS-305.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Distinguish pseudonymisation, de-identification, aggregation, synthetic data and differential privacy by the guarantee and cost of each.
- Measure re-identification risk from quasi-identifiers and reduce it while tracking analytic utility.
- Compute differentially private counts and means and state the accuracy cost of a privacy budget.
- Test a synthetic extract for analytic utility and membership-inference leakage.
- Audit a screening model for subgroup disparities, choose fairness criteria that fit the decision and evaluate two mitigations.
- Build the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs to judge and rerun a release.

## Topics

### Personal data in analysis

- Identifiers, quasi-identifiers and sensitive attributes
- Minimisation, purpose limitation and secondary use
- Linkage attacks

### De-identification and synthetic data

- k-anonymity and its limits
- Generalisation, suppression and aggregation
- When de-identified data are still personal data

### Differential privacy basics

- Privacy budget and composition
- Mechanisms for counts and means
- Evaluating a differential privacy claim

### Subgroup fairness audit

- Group metrics and why they conflict
- Intersectional slices and small-group uncertainty
- Bias types across the lifecycle
- Mitigation and its cost

### Documentation and impact assessment

- Datasheets and model cards
- Personal data impact assessment
- Likelihood and impact scoring and residual risk
