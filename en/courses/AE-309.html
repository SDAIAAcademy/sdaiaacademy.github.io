AE-309

Computer Vision Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

Participants build vision systems for a fictional municipal roads and traffic department that must keep working in glare, dust, rain and at night. On public road-damage images and synthetic traffic-camera clips they audit and extend annotations, fine-tune convolutional and transformer backbones, train a pothole detector and a crack segmenter, track and count vehicles, and fit the chosen model to the latency and memory budget of a processor-only roadside unit. Each lab ends with a test under changed lighting, blur or camera position. Vision-language models and multimodal applications are AE-408.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Engineers who have completed the level 200 set or can show equivalent work: a deployed model or language model application they built and can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare an image dataset with annotation guidelines, an agreement check, splits that keep each road segment in one split, and augmentations that preserve label meaning.

  2. Applied

    Implement transfer learning with two pretrained backbones and compare them by condition slice with intervals.

  3. Performance

    Build a detector and a segmenter and diagnose their errors by type to choose the next improvement.

  4. Applied

    Measure tracking and counting accuracy for vehicles crossing a line in video.

  5. Performance

    Optimise a model to a per-frame latency and memory budget on a processor through export, input resolution and quantisation, re-measuring accuracy at each step.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate the model under corruption and camera shift and decide whether to release it.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
26 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Visual data first

    1. How vision systems fail in the field
    2. Annotation guidelines and agreement
    3. Leakage by site and near-duplicates
    4. Augmentation and what it assumes

  2. Day 2 Backbones and transfer

    1. Convolutional networks and vision transformers
    2. How much to fine-tune
    3. Zero-shot baselines
    4. Licences of open-weight backbones

  3. Day 3 Detect, segment and track

    1. Detection and its metrics
    2. Detector error analysis
    3. Segmenting cracks
    4. Tracking and counting

  4. Day 4 Fit the budget and release

    1. Export to an interchange format
    2. Resolution and precision against latency
    3. Corruption and shift tests
    4. Privacy and monitoring in the field

  5. Day 5 Capstone: a service that meets its budget, defended on a failure

Prerequisites

  • AE-201 Machine Learning Foundations and Model Evaluation Level 200
  • AE-202 Deep Learning and Generative AI Fundamentals Level 200

More about this course

Topics

Visual data

  • Boxes, masks and annotation formats
  • Guidelines, agreement and label noise
  • Near-duplicates and leakage by site
  • Augmentation and what it assumes

Backbones and transfer

  • Convolutional networks and vision transformers
  • Linear probe, partial and full fine-tuning
  • Open-vocabulary models as zero-shot baselines
  • Licences of open-weight backbones

Detection, segmentation and video

  • Non-maximum suppression and average precision
  • Semantic and instance segmentation and overlap metrics
  • Tracking by detection and identity switches
  • Counting rules

Deployment constraints

  • Export to an interchange format
  • Resolution, batch size and numeric precision
  • Latency and memory on processors and accelerators

Field reliability and privacy

  • Corruption and camera-shift tests
  • Monitoring camera drift
  • Blurring faces and plates before storage
  • Model cards for vision systems

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks without AI assistance on metrics, splits and error types (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a five-criterion rubric: data and splits 20, model quality and error analysis 25, latency budget 20, field-condition tests and privacy 25, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence on a failure case the assessor picks (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the annotation and error-analysis labs. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains code and errors but does not write the training pipeline. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
