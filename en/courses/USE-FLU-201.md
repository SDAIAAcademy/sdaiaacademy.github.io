# USE-FLU-201 · Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own

- Track: Data & AI Fluency
- Path: [Fluency for Everyone](../paths/USE-FLU.md)
- Level: Level 200 · Apply
- Format: Workshop: one day of six sessions, each with concepts and a lab, plus guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
- Total hours: 8 h
- Audience: practitioner
- Status: In development

## Description

A one-day workshop on the habits that separate useful AI help from polished mistakes. Participants break a realistic task into steps and decide which to delegate; try the assistant on a new task type against a known answer before relying on it; describe goals, context, audience and success criteria; iterate over several turns; and test finished-looking outputs for invented facts, missing context and flattering agreement. They practise deciding when not to use AI, write a disclosure note, and in a tracked follow-up apply a delegation card to one of their own recurring tasks twice. Standing context and custom assistants follow in USE-FLU-202.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Analyse a work task into steps and assign each to the person, the assistant, both or neither, with a reason based on risk, skill and accountability.
- Prepare a request that gives the assistant the goal, context, audience, constraints, success criteria and an example, and refine it over at least three turns.
- Test the assistant on a task type that is new to the participant by comparing its output with a known-good answer before deciding to delegate that task.
- Test a polished AI output for invented facts, missing context, faulty reasoning and agreement with a wrong premise.
- Decide whether to use, limit or refuse AI help for a task and defend the decision, including cases where AI should not be used.
- Draft a disclosure note that states what the assistant did, what the participant checked and who is accountable.

## Topics

### Delegate: what to hand over

- Automation, augmentation and agency
- The uneven frontier: test AI task by task against a known answer
- Tasks to keep: judgement, accountability, skills you must retain
- Confidential and high-impact work

### Describe: context and criteria

- Goal, audience, constraints and success criteria
- Examples and counter-examples
- Asking the assistant to ask questions first
- Giving source material instead of relying on the model's memory

### Iterate

- Critique-and-revise cycles
- Asking for alternatives and for the reasoning
- Structured outputs such as tables and checklists
- When to start again

### Judge: verifying polished output

- Why finished artefacts get checked less
- Invented facts, figures and citations
- Missing context and wrong premises
- Agreement with the user rather than the evidence

### Own: responsibility and disclosure

- Accountability stays with the author
- Disclosure notes
- Low-quality output passed on to colleagues
- Keeping data within approved tools
