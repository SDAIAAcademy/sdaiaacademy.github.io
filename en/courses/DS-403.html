DS-403

Statistical Evaluation of AI Systems

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Data Science

Participants treat every evaluation of an AI system as an experiment with a sampling design. On item-level results from small open-weight models on a fictional permits authority's synthetic bilingual task set, they compute clustered standard errors, compare systems with paired designs, size evaluations within a budget, use exact or Bayesian intervals for small sets, correct for grader error and audit benchmark claims. Graders and harnesses are built in AE-303, not here.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Data scientists who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a defended analysis with a validated model or a causal estimate that they can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute standard errors and intervals for an evaluation score that account for clustered items, repeated samples and small item counts.

  2. Performance

    Decide whether the difference between two systems is real and large enough to matter, using a paired analysis.

  3. Performance

    Design an evaluation's size in items, samples per item and grader calls to detect a stated difference within budget.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate the bias that grader error adds to a pass rate and correct it, carrying calibration uncertainty into the interval.

  5. Performance

    Audit an evaluation claim for statistical validity, contamination risk and unequal tuning or token budgets.

  6. Performance

    Design a reporting standard for evaluation claims, including results by language and subgroup.

    2. Auditing a report

    4. A reporting standard

More about this course

Topics

Evaluations as experiments

  • Items as a sample from a task population
  • Variance from items, samples and graders
  • Pass rates over repeated attempts
  • Pre-registered analysis plans

Comparisons and sizing

  • Clustered standard errors and design effects
  • Paired differences and multiple comparisons
  • Sizing by formula and simulation

Small sets and graders

  • Exact and Bayesian intervals
  • Correcting pass rates for grader error

Claims and standards

  • Contamination, unequal budgets and saturation
  • Reporting by language and subgroup

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Open-weight model serving (capstone only)
llama.cpp or Ollama on a laptop, vLLM on a free notebook GPU
Evaluation harness with item-level logs
Inspect AI, lm-evaluation-harness, promptfoo
Statistics and simulation
SciPy, statsmodels, PyMC
Analysis environment
Python through uv with Jupyter or marimo, Quarto

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised check: compute a clustered standard error by hand, choose paired or unpaired analysis, read a sizing plan (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: plan 20, uncertainty and comparison 25, sizing 15, grader correction and slicing 20, reporting and reproducibility 20 (60%)
  • Live review challenging one statistical choice (20%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the capstone rubric and on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and the first standard error, done by hand. AI-tutor in labs: hints, no finished code. AI-full in the capstone, with relied-on outputs checked in a verification note.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
