USE-EDU-101

Data and AI Fluency for University Students

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Extended
  • 11 days · 40 h
  • Available

Students from any discipline work through the six components of the national cross-disciplinary curriculum, from AI concepts and data as evidence to verification and applied use in their own field. Weekly labs on synthetic or public data end in a tracked artefact. The capstone is a use-case plan with a justified AI or non-AI choice, a risk tier, safeguards and a cost rationale.

Who it is for

Audience: beginner

University students in any discipline, learners in training programmes, and the faculty, school teachers and trainers who teach and assess them; no technical background is assumed.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI and AI agents, and a model from the system around it.

  2. Awareness

    Identify the evidence, assumptions and limitations behind a data-based claim, including correlation presented as cause.

  3. Awareness

    Explain how data quality, representativeness and labelling drive a learned system's errors, using false positives, false negatives and base rates.

  4. Applied

    Use structured requests with audience, constraints and quality criteria, and score generative output against them.

  5. Applied

    Apply a verification and disclosure routine to AI-assisted work, classifying the data and sanitising the prompt first.

  6. Applied

    Prepare a use-case plan for a bounded discipline task that justifies AI, data analysis, both or neither, with risk tier, safeguards, evaluation and cost.

More about this course

Topics

AI concepts and limits

  • Task types: classification, forecasting, recommendation, generation
  • Model versus system: retrieval, tools, people in the loop
  • Assistant to agent: permissions and approval gates
  • Automation bias and the accountability boundary

Data as evidence and how machines learn

  • Provenance, quality dimensions, sampling and measurement bias
  • Data lifecycle, roles and classification
  • Training and testing, overfitting, data shift, leakage
  • Why accuracy misleads

Generative AI, verification and safe use

  • Probabilistic output and the confidence gap
  • Task, audience, constraints, quality criteria, verification request
  • Deepfakes, intellectual property and academic integrity
  • Prompt injection, analysed but never attempted

Applied use in a discipline

  • Avoiding unnecessary automation
  • Risk tiers, including unacceptable uses
  • Cost and sustainability, including the hidden costs of free tools
  • Tracks: the curriculum's five plus health, business, law, engineering and education

How the work is assessed

  • Two supervised AI-off quizzes, weeks 5 and 10 (20%)
  • Weekly artefact portfolio (35%)
  • Capstone plan on a six-criterion rubric (30%)
  • Defence (15%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each quiz

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the quizzes and first attempts at each claim frame. AI-tutor in weekly labs. AI-full in the capstone, disclosed and defended.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
