## Description

Participants practise reading AI-relevant law, guidance and standards the way a governance specialist must: who is in scope, in which role, for which systems, with what duties and from when. The Kingdom's instruments are the reference case; participants then map the same obligations to international management and risk standards and to the main foreign regimes an exporting organisation meets. The product is an obligations register and a one-page multi-jurisdiction map for a fictional company selling three AI products in three markets. The six-monthly briefing LDS-EXE-210 keeps the map current after the course.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Distinguish binding law, implementing regulation, advisory framework, voluntary code and international standard, and state what each requires of an organisation.
- Analyse an instrument to extract scope, roles, risk classes, duties, deadlines and enforcement into a structured obligations register.
- Apply tests of territorial reach, provider or deployer role and risk class to a described AI product.
- Prepare a crosswalk from regulatory duties to clauses of an AI management system standard and a risk management framework.
- Prepare a horizon-scanning log for pending instruments with source, status, expected effect, owner and review date.
- Deliver a one-page multi-jurisdiction map with the controls that close the highest-priority gaps.

## Topics

### How AI is regulated

- Horizontal AI law, sector rules and data protection law
- Risk-based structure: prohibited, high-risk, transparency and minimal classes
- Binding, advisory and voluntary instruments
- How guidance hardens into obligation

### The Kingdom's instruments as the reference case

- No AI-specific statute: how data, cybersecurity and sector law combine with advisory national AI instruments
- Personal data protection law and its regulations applied to training, retrieval and inference
- Data classification, sharing and secondary-use rules
- National AI ethics principles and risk levels
- National AI risk management framework and its advisory status
- Registration, impact assessment and AI service-provider accreditation through the national data governance platform
- Cybersecurity controls and draft AI cybersecurity guidelines
- Sector regulators for health devices, finance, telecoms and cloud

### Reading an instrument

- Scope and definitions
- Roles: developer, provider, deployer, operator
- Duties, deadlines and penalties
- Guidance, answers to frequent questions and enforcement decisions as interpretation

### International standards and frameworks

- AI management systems (ISO/IEC 42001)
- AI risk management (ISO/IEC 23894) and impact assessment (ISO/IEC 42005)
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework and its generative AI profile
- Harmonised standards and presumption of conformity, starting with the first harmonised AI quality management standard

### Foreign regimes for exporters

- The European Union AI Act, its 2026 amendments and staged application dates
- United States federal policy and state laws on automated decisions
- Chinese rules on generative AI, labelling and anthropomorphic AI
- Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI
- OECD and G20 AI principles

### Crosswalks and the multi-jurisdiction map

- One control, many duties
- Gap analysis and prioritisation
- Presenting obligations on one page

### Keeping current

- Horizon scanning and ownership
- Primary sources over commentary
- Dated annexes and review cycles
- Re-verifying assistant summaries of law against the source
