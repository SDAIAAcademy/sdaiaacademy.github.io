USE-PRO-205

AI for Communications, Media and Content Work

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 3 days · 12 h
  • Available

Participants run the content desk for a synthetic public-service launch, drafting in Arabic and English with an assistant, then fact-checking, clearing rights, labelling synthetic media and signing off as separate logged steps. Media generation is USE-FLU-206; translation craft is USE-FLU-207.

Open the coursePractice questions

Under review before its first delivery

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Staff in public administration, human resources, finance, legal, communications, service, projects, research, health, sales, engineering and IT operations who already work with a general assistant and want to apply it safely to their own work.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify editorial risks in AI content: invented quotes and statistics, copy echoing the brief and unlabelled synthetic media.

  2. Applied

    Implement an editorial workflow in which drafting, fact checking, rights review and sign-off are separate, logged steps.

  3. Applied

    Prepare Arabic and English versions of a release and three posts with identical facts, names and figures.

  4. Applied

    Test statistics, quotations and images for source and provenance, and classify synthetic items by required label and consent.

  5. Performance

    Redesign one recurring content task and measure time, errors and post-sign-off corrections.

More about this course

Topics

Editorial workflows with AI

  • Separating drafting from checking and sign-off
  • Facts identical across Arabic and English
  • Brand voice drift

Fact checks and provenance

  • Primary sources for numbers and quotes
  • Reverse image search and Content Credentials
  • Why watermarks and missing credentials prove nothing

Synthetic media, rights and consent

  • Labelling generated and edited content
  • Likeness, voice and consent
  • Licence terms of generated assets

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Writing assistant
an open-weight model in Open WebUI, an organisation-approved commercial assistant
Provenance and reverse image search
a Content Credentials inspector, TinEye, Google Lens

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (40%)
  • Redesign card and live review (40%)
To pass
70% overall, with no unlabelled synthetic item or unchecked quotation in the final release

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and first-pass fact check; AI-tutor for the parity check; AI-full in drafting and the redesign, with every item checked and labelled.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
