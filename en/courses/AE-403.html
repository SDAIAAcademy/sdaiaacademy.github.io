AE-403

Reliability Engineering for AI Services

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: AI Engineering

Participants set and defend reliability standards for AI services that other teams build. For a fictional postal and parcels operator running a bilingual tracking assistant on a self-hosted open-weight model with retrieval, they write service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, set admission limits with headroom from measured goodput, design and chaos-test degradation paths, lead incident drills and blameless reviews, release through canaries with automated rollback, and admit models only after verifying signatures and bills of materials.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Engineers who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a production AI system they built, operated and can defend.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, with error budgets and a release policy.

  2. Performance

    Decide an admission limit and shedding priorities that keep headroom for the busiest hour.

  3. Performance

    Design degradation for overload and dependency failure using load shedding, a smaller fallback model and a cached-answer mode.

  4. Performance

    Lead an incident drill from detection to recovery and write a blameless review.

  5. Performance

    Build a release gate that verifies model signatures, a bill of materials and licences before deployment.

  6. Performance

    Review another team's AI service for production readiness and set launch conditions.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

The days and sessions of the published course.

  2. Day 2 Capacity and failure

    1. Capacity with headroom
    2. Admission control and shedding
    3. Degraded modes
    4. Chaos experiments

  3. Day 3 Incidents and safe releases

    1. Finding silent regressions
    2. Running an incident
    3. Canary and rollback
    4. Learning from incidents

  4. Day 4 Integrity and readiness

    1. The model supply chain
    2. Signing and verification
    3. Bills of materials and admission
    4. Production readiness reviews

  5. Day 5 Readiness review defences

Topics

Objectives for AI services

  • Time to first token, time per output token, availability
  • Answer quality from sampled online grading
  • Error budgets and release policy
  • Objectives for multi-step tasks

Capacity under load

  • Queueing and tail latency
  • Headroom for the busiest hour
  • Admission control and load shedding

Designing for failure

  • Provider, retrieval and tool failures
  • Fallback models and degraded modes
  • Retry storms and chaos experiments

Incidents and releases

  • Burn-rate alerts on latency and quality
  • Incident roles and decision logs
  • Canary releases with automated rollback
  • Reviews turned into regression tests

Integrity and readiness

  • Signing a manifest of hashed model files
  • Machine learning bills of materials and licences
  • Admission policy at deployment
  • Readiness reviews and launch conditions

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks, AI-off: compute error budget burn, set an admission limit from a goodput curve, order first actions in an incident (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: objectives and alerts 20, capacity and degradation 25, incident drill and review 20, release integrity 15, readiness decision 20 (60%)
  • Panel defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and during the game day. AI-tutor in the other labs: it explains telemetry and tool behaviour but does not set objectives or thresholds. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
