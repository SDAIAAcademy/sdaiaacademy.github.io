## Description

Participants run an AI procurement for a fictional organisation from problem statement to agreed terms. They write requirements from the problem rather than the product, build a fixed test set of the buyer's own synthetic cases and test two hosted models on it, draft terms on data use, change notice, rollback, audit, price and exit, and negotiate them in a timed simulation against instructors playing the vendor. The capstone is a solicitation pack and evaluation report.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Prepare requirements and weighted criteria from the business problem, covering quality thresholds, data handling, oversight and exit.
- Test two hosted models or services on a fixed set of the buyer's cases, prepared before vendors are seen.
- Draft terms for data use, no training on buyer data, portability, change notice, rollback, audit, price, fourth parties and exit.
- Decide a negotiation position and defend it in a timed simulation with a vendor team.
- Deliver a solicitation pack and award recommendation that a tender committee could approve.

## Prerequisites

- Has taken part in a procurement as requester, evaluator or approver
- Has used AI assistants and checked their output (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)

## Topics

### AI procurement as a process

- Stages: problem, solicitation, evaluation, negotiation, monitoring, exit
- A cross-functional buying team
- Public rules: life-cycle cost, quality and risk; local content

### Requirements and evaluation

- Requirements from the problem, not the product
- Test sets the buyer holds and the vendor never sees
- Testing a service without access to its code or weights
- Scoring responses and assurance evidence

### Contract terms

- Data rights, no training on buyer data, residency
- Model-change notice, version pinning, rollback
- Service levels, audit and independent testing rights
- Fourth parties, portability, exit and sunset

### Price, negotiation and monitoring

- Total cost over the contract life (economics is LDS-EXE-202)
- Positions, walk-away points and trade-offs
- Re-testing when the vendor ships a new model (licences and supply chain are LDS-AIG-203)
