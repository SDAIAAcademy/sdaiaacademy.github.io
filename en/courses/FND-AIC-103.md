# FND-AIC-103 · Reading AI Evidence: Benchmarks, Claims and Statistics

- Track: Data & AI Foundations
- Path: [AI Concepts and Responsible Practice](../paths/FND-AIC.md)
- Level: Level 100 · Explain
- Format: Workshop: one day with labs and short guided pre-reading
- Total hours: 8 h
- Audience: beginner
- Status: In development

## Description

Participants read the numbers behind AI decisions: benchmark scores, leaderboard ranks, launch claims, adoption figures and model cards. They rescore an agent run as partial credit and as finished tasks, test whether a reported gain exceeds its noise, list what changed between two runs of one benchmark, and reconcile adoption figures that range from about 20% to 90%. The day ends with an evidence brief.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

- Explain what a benchmark measures and name threats to its validity, such as contamination, saturation and invalid items.
- Identify why two reported scores are not comparable: partial credit, attempts, harness, token budget or grader.
- Compute an interval for a reported accuracy difference and decide whether it exceeds sampling noise.
- Explain how adoption and productivity figures differ by denominator, time window and self-reported or measured effect.
- Draft an evidence brief that separates what a published AI claim shows from what it asserts, using its model card.

## Prerequisites

- Reads percentages and chart axes and writes a spreadsheet formula; no programming is required

## Topics

### Benchmarks

- Construct, items and scoring
- Contamination, saturation and audits
- Leaderboards built on pairwise votes

### Comparing results

- Partial credit against completed tasks
- 50% and 80% reliability time horizons
- Same model, different harness
- Cost per completed task
- Intervals and paired comparisons

### Claims about use and value

- Denominators and time windows
- Self-report against randomised trials
- What model and system cards omit
