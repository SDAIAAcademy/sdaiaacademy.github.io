USE-FLU-202

AI Productivity for Knowledge Work

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 30 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Start here

The Academy's published four-day course plus a capstone day, delivered Arabic-first. Day one covers where AI helps and harms, safe use, and structured requests with persistent context. Day two applies them to Arabic and English writing, reading and extraction, spreadsheets and presentations. Day three covers deep research with verification, source-grounded notebooks, custom assistants and workflow redesign. Day four introduces agents with connectors and approvals, and building a small tool from a requirements document. In the capstone each participant redesigns one real recurring task and measures the gain over two trials against a baseline.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Staff in any role and sector, from new hires to managers, who use or will use dashboards, AI assistants and agents in routine work; no technical background is assumed and delivery is Arabic-first in the Kingdom.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Apply a structured request pattern with persistent context to writing, summarising and extraction tasks in Arabic and English.

  2. Applied

    Use an assistant to analyse a spreadsheet and produce a chart and a summary, with every reported figure checked by recalculation.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a research brief with a deep-research tool and a source-grounded notebook, tracing each key claim to a source that exists and supports it.

  4. Applied

    Configure a custom assistant with instructions, reference files and test questions for a recurring team task.

  5. Applied

    Configure an agent run that uses a connector under least-privilege permissions with a human approval step, and identify the prompt-injection risk in its inputs.

  6. Applied

    Draft a requirements document for a small internal tool and produce a working prototype with a generative coding platform, tested against three checks.

  7. Performance

    Design an AI-assisted version of a real recurring task and measure the change in time and quality against a baseline over two trials.

  8. Applied

    Prepare a personal adoption plan over one, seven and thirty days, including tasks kept AI-off to preserve skill and checks that stop unverified work reaching colleagues.

Topics

  • Structured requests, dialogue and persistent context

Everyday tasks

  • Reading, summarising and extraction
  • Spreadsheets and data analysis
  • Presentations and images

Research and trustworthy knowledge

  • Deep research and verification
  • Source-grounded notebooks and projects
  • Custom assistants and reusable skills
  • Workflow redesign

Agents and small tools

  • Agents and scheduled tasks
  • Connectors, permissions and prompt injection
  • From idea to requirements document and agent instruction file
  • Build, test and share a prototype; production deployment belongs to developers

Responsible adoption

  • The cost of unchecked output passed to colleagues
  • Keeping your own skill: deliberate AI-off practice
  • Disclosure and records
  • The one, seven and thirty day plan

Measuring value

  • Baselines before change
  • Time and quality measures
  • Two trials and honest reporting
  • Costs of checking and rework

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check of the participant's own skill: correct a flawed output and write one short paragraph unaided (10%)
  • Daily lab artefacts (25%)
  • Capstone folder scored on the six-criterion rubric (50%)
  • Live capstone defence (15%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the capstone rubric, and at least 60% on the AI-off check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check of the participant's own writing and checking skill. AI-tutor for the guided pre-work. AI-full in the labs and the capstone, where the participant owns, discloses and defends every output.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
