FN-103

Mathematics for Machine Learning: Vectors, Gradients and Probability

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Foundations

Participants learn the minimum mathematics both badges assume and compute every idea twice: by hand on a small case and in array code. The case is a fictional desalination plant with synthetic pump and sensor readings. Vectors and matrices describe the readings and their similarity; least squares and principal components find structure; derivatives, the chain rule and gradient descent fit an energy curve; conditional probability, distributions and seeded simulation explain alarm rates and the uncertainty of an estimate. The capstone ties the three strands into one short analysis.

Who it is for

Audience: beginner

Graduates and career changers entering the AI Engineering or Data Science track; computing and data graduates who pass the placement test skip the courses it covers.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain dot products, norms, matrix products and rank as operations on data, and predict the shape of each result.

  2. Applied

    Compute cosine similarity, a least-squares fit and principal components with array code, checking each against a hand calculation.

  3. Awareness

    Describe derivatives, gradients and the chain rule on a computational graph, and trace one backward pass by hand.

  4. Applied

    Implement gradient descent for a small model and identify step-size problems from the loss curve.

  5. Applied

    Compute the probability that an alarm signals a real fault with Bayes rule, and confirm it by seeded simulation.

  6. Applied

    Use simulation to estimate an expectation and its Monte Carlo error, and show how the error shrinks with sample size.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
26 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Data as vectors

    1. Vectors and similarity
    2. Matrices as transformations
    3. Loops versus vectorised code
    4. Rank and redundant sensors

  2. Day 2 Structure and fit

    1. Projection and least squares
    2. When least squares misbehaves
    3. Covariance and principal components
    4. Language models use the same algebra

  3. Day 3 Gradients

    1. Derivatives as local slopes
    2. The chain rule on a graph
    3. Gradient descent
    4. Penalties and their effect

  4. Day 4 Probability and simulation

    1. Conditional probability and base rates
    2. Distributions for counts and measurements
    3. Expectation and Monte Carlo error
    4. Probability inside models

  5. Day 5 Capstone: a second plant and the oral check

More about this course

Topics

Vectors and matrices as data

  • Dot products, norms and cosine similarity
  • Matrix products as transformations
  • Shapes, broadcasting and vectorised code
  • Rank and linear independence

Structure in data

  • Projection and least squares
  • Covariance and principal component analysis (PCA)
  • Eigenvectors and singular values in brief
  • Floating-point limits and conditioning

Change and optimisation

  • Derivatives and partial derivatives
  • Gradients and the chain rule on a graph
  • Automatic differentiation as the chain rule in code
  • Gradient descent and step size
  • Convexity, local minima and penalties

Probability

  • Conditional probability and Bayes rule
  • Binomial, Poisson, normal and categorical distributions
  • Expectation, variance and covariance
  • Likelihood and cross-entropy as training losses

Simulation

  • Seeds and pseudo-random generators
  • Monte Carlo estimates and their error
  • Law of large numbers and the central limit theorem

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off paper check: hand computations across the three strands (40%)
  • Capstone notebook: correctness 35, hand checks 20, interpretation 25, reproducibility 20 (40%)
  • Oral rework of one step with new numbers (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the paper check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for every hand calculation, which comes first in each lab, and for the paper check. AI-tutor for the array-code half of each lab, with hints tied to the instructor's worked solution. AI-full for the capstone code, but not in the oral.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
