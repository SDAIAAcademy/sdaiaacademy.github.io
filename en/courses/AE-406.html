AE-406

Post-Training Open Models on Domain Data

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: AI Engineering

Participants post-train open-weight models on domain data and prove the result is worth serving. For the fictional Eastern Coast Permits Authority they turn synthetic bilingual permit records, officer edits and agent traces into tuning mixtures, then run full and sharded supervised tuning, preference optimisation, a short reinforcement learning loop with verifiable rewards, distillation from a larger teacher and tuning on filtered agent traces. Each model is compared with its base and a prompting and retrieval baseline, for task gains and for regressions in general ability, safety and Arabic quality. AE-310 covers single-GPU adapters and serving; this course goes beyond them.

Who it is for

Audience: specialist

Engineers who have completed the level 300 core or can show equivalent work: a production AI system they built, operated and can defend.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Decide whether post-training is justified over prompting and retrieval for a task, and which method fits it.

  2. Performance

    Build a validated tuning mixture of domain records, teacher-generated examples and general data with licence, deduplication and leakage checks.

  3. Applied

    Configure supervised tuning at scale, choosing full-parameter or adapter tuning and a sharding strategy that fit a stated accelerator memory budget.

  4. Performance

    Train with preference data or verifiable rewards and diagnose reward hacking in the result.

  5. Performance

    Produce a small model by distillation and by tuning on outcome-filtered agent traces that meets a latency and cost target.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate an adapted model against its base for task improvement and for regressions in general ability, safety and Arabic dialect quality.

Format and days

Format

    1. Full tuning against adapters
    2. Hyperparameters that matter
    3. Forgetting and regressions
    4. Scaling out

  3. Day 3 Preferences and rewards

    1. Preference data from expert edits
    2. Direct preference optimisation
    3. Verifiable rewards
    4. Reward hacking

  4. Day 4 Distillation, traces and the decision

    1. Distilling a teacher
    2. Training on agent traces
    3. Merging task adapters
    4. Evaluating the adapted model

  5. Day 5 Capstone project presentations

More about this course

Topics

Deciding to post-train

  • Prompting, retrieval and tuning as alternatives
  • Cost of training against cost of serving
  • Open-weight licences and acceptable-use terms

Data for post-training

  • Curation, deduplication and mixing against forgetting
  • Synthetic data from teachers and its validation
  • Leakage against evaluation sets

Supervised tuning at scale

  • Full tuning, adapters and quantised adapters
  • Memory accounting for weights, gradients and optimiser state
  • Sharded multi-GPU training
  • Forgetting and safety regressions

Preferences and rewards

  • Preference pairs and annotator agreement
  • Direct preference optimisation and its variants
  • Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards
  • Reward hacking

Distillation, traces and merging

  • Distillation from a teacher
  • Rejection sampling of agent traces
  • Merging methods and interference between tasks

Evaluating adapted models

  • Task, general, safety and Arabic dialect slices
  • Paired comparison with the base
  • Model cards for adapted models

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Post-training library
TRL with PEFT adapters, a plain PyTorch training loop
Sharded training
PyTorch FSDP, DeepSpeed ZeRO
Open-weight models
small open-weight instruction models, a larger open-weight teacher served locally or hosted
Inference engine for generation and evaluation
vLLM, llama.cpp
Model merging
mergekit, task arithmetic in a notebook
Evaluation harness
Inspect, lm-evaluation-harness
Experiment tracking
MLflow, TensorBoard
Compute
a free notebook GPU for labs, Academy-provided multi-GPU hours for the capstone

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: memory accounting, choosing a method for four scenarios and reading loss curves (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a rubric: decision and data 25, training 25, evaluation against base and baseline 35, model card 15 (60%)
  • Live defence in which the assessor picks one regression and the participant explains its cause and a remedy (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the day 1 memory accounting lab. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains errors and does not write training or reward code. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
