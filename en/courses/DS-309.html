DS-309

Responsible Data Science: Privacy, Fairness and Documentation

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

Participants take the synthetic applicant data and eligibility-screening model of Yusr Housing Support Fund, a fictional public fund, through the checks a release needs: they measure re-identification risk and de-identify the table, publish differentially private counts under a budget, test a synthetic extract for leakage, audit subgroup errors against the bias types in SDAIA's AI Bias Reference Guide, and write the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs. Calibration and explanation are DS-305.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Analysts and data scientists who have completed the level 200 set or can show equivalent work: an analysis or model they built, validated and can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish pseudonymisation, de-identification, aggregation, synthetic data and differential privacy by the guarantee and cost of each.

  2. Applied

    Measure re-identification risk from quasi-identifiers and reduce it while tracking analytic utility.

  3. Applied

    Compute differentially private counts and means and state the accuracy cost of a privacy budget.

  4. Applied

    Test a synthetic extract for analytic utility and membership-inference leakage.

  5. Performance

    Audit a screening model for subgroup disparities, choose fairness criteria that fit the decision and evaluate two mitigations.

  6. Performance

    Build the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs to judge and rerun a release.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
26 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Find the personal data

    1. Personal data in an analysis
    2. Classification and permitted use
    3. Linkage attacks
    4. Measuring disclosure risk

  2. Day 2 Release with less risk

    1. Generalisation and suppression
    2. Differential privacy for counts
    3. Budgets and composition
    4. Synthetic data and leakage

  3. Day 3 Audit for fairness

    1. Disaggregating errors
    2. Criteria that conflict
    3. Bias across the lifecycle
    4. Mitigation and its cost

  4. Day 4 Document and assess

    1. Datasheets
    2. Model cards
    3. Impact assessment
    4. Review by someone else

  5. Day 5 Capstone: the release pack

More about this course

Topics

Personal data in analysis

  • Identifiers, quasi-identifiers and sensitive attributes
  • Minimisation, purpose limitation and secondary use
  • Linkage attacks

De-identification and synthetic data

  • k-anonymity and its limits
  • Generalisation, suppression and aggregation
  • When de-identified data are still personal data

Differential privacy basics

  • Privacy budget and composition
  • Mechanisms for counts and means
  • Evaluating a differential privacy claim

Subgroup fairness audit

  • Group metrics and why they conflict
  • Intersectional slices and small-group uncertainty
  • Bias types across the lifecycle
  • Mitigation and its cost

Documentation and impact assessment

  • Datasheets and model cards
  • Personal data impact assessment
  • Likelihood and impact scoring and residual risk

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check on privacy controls, disclosure risk and fairness metrics (20%)
  • Capstone release pack on a rubric: privacy 25, fairness audit 25, documentation 20, impact assessment 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live review recomputing a disparity the assessor selects (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first lab of day 1. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains methods and errors without producing the audit. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
