LDS-AIG-204

AI Risk Management in Practice

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • In development

Participants run the full AI risk cycle on realistic cases: set context and scope, identify risks with structured techniques, score likelihood and impact, choose treatments, sign off residual risk and define the indicators that show when a risk is moving. The national AI risk management framework is the primary method, with crosswalks to international frameworks. The capstone is a complete risk assessment of a fictional government service assistant and an internal procurement agent, presented to a mock risk committee and followed by a tracked monitoring task.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Governance, risk, compliance, audit, privacy and security staff, and technical staff moving into AI assurance roles in public and private organisations.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Describe the phases of the national AI risk management cycle and map them to the functions of two international risk frameworks.

  2. Applied

    Apply structured identification techniques, including stakeholder harm mapping, misuse cases and failure-mode analysis, to an AI use case.

  3. Applied

    Compute likelihood and impact scores on a four-by-four matrix with written justifications, then calibrate them with peers.

  4. Applied

    Draft an AI risk appetite and tolerance statement that sets limits per risk category and the escalation route.

  5. Applied

    Prepare a treatment plan that selects avoid, reduce, transfer or accept for each priority risk, with control owners, cost and expected residual score.

  6. Applied

    Configure key risk indicators and early-warning thresholds with data sources, review frequency and the action each triggers.

  7. Performance

    Deliver a complete AI risk assessment and defend the residual-risk recommendation before a risk committee.

More about this course

Topics

Risk foundations for AI

  • Risk, hazard, harm and uncertainty
  • Enterprise risk management and where AI risk fits
  • Developer, operator and policymaker roles in the national framework
  • Adopting an advisory framework into enterprise policy
  • Lessons from model risk management in financial services: inventory, validation, effective challenge
  • Risk appetite and tolerance

Context and scope

  • System boundaries and intended use
  • Stakeholders and affected people
  • Legal and contractual context
  • Assumptions and dependencies

Identifying AI risks

  • The seven risk categories of the national framework and their mapping to international taxonomies
  • Stakeholder harm mapping
  • Misuse and abuse cases
  • Failure-mode analysis
  • Risks specific to generative systems and agents
  • Third-party and supplier risk
  • Learning from public incident records

Assessing and scoring

  • Likelihood and impact scales
  • Four-by-four matrix and score bands
  • Calibration among assessors
  • Inherent and residual risk
  • Aggregation and its pitfalls

Treatment

  • Treatment options
  • Control selection and design
  • Human oversight as a control, and its limits
  • Cost and proportionality
  • Residual-risk acceptance and sign-off

Monitoring and review

  • Key risk indicators and early warning
  • Control testing
  • Triggers for reassessment: change, incident, drift
  • Reporting to committees and boards

Crosswalks and links

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework and its generative AI profile
  • ISO/IEC 23894 risk management guidance
  • Hand-offs to impact assessment (LDS-AIG-303) and incident response (LDS-AIG-305)

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Risk register
any spreadsheet, an open-source governance, risk and compliance platform, a commercial governance, risk and compliance suite
Structured analysis templates
failure mode and effects analysis worksheet, bow-tie diagram in an open diagram tool such as draw.io
Public AI incident records
AI Incident Database, OECD AI Incidents Monitor
Indicator exercises on monitoring data
a notebook with plotting, any dashboard tool
Language model assistant for challenge and drafting
a locally served open-weight model, any approved commercial assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised scenario test without AI assistance: identify and score risks for an unseen case (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone assessment scored on a six-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Committee defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall, and at least 60% on the scenario test and on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario test and for first independent scoring before calibration. AI-tutor for suggesting misuse cases and challenging scores. AI-full for the capstone report, with every risk an assistant proposed marked and justified by the participant.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
