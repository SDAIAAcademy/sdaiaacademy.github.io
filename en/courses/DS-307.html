DS-307

Metrics, Semantic Models and Dashboards

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 5 days · 26 h
  • Available

On the learning path: Data Science

Participants take over performance reporting for Wadi Noor Transit, a fictional bus operator whose three reports give three different on-time figures. On a synthetic warehouse of trips, boardings and routes they write governed metric definitions, build a semantic model that defines each metric once, design a dashboard for one decision within a load-time budget, trace an anomaly to its source, and test a conversational analytics agent on certified questions with and without the semantic layer. Platform-scale semantic layer design is DS-408.

Who it is for

Audience: practitioner

Analysts and data scientists who have completed the level 200 set or can show equivalent work: an analysis or model they built, validated and can explain.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain why reports built on one warehouse disagree on the same measure.

  2. Applied

    Draft metric definitions stating formula, grain, filters, time logic, owner and limits.

  3. Applied

    Implement a star-schema semantic model with a slowly changing route dimension and each metric defined once.

  4. Applied

    Analyse a metric anomaly by drill-down and segmentation to find its source.

  5. Performance

    Design a dashboard for a named audience and decision within a load-time budget.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate a conversational analytics agent against governed metrics and decide which question types it may answer unreviewed.

Format and days

Format
Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task
Total hours
26 h

The days and sessions of the published course.

  1. Day 1 Define the numbers

    1. Why three reports disagree
    2. Anatomy of a metric definition
    3. Ratios, distinct counts and time logic
    4. Metric trees

  2. Day 2 Model once

    1. Star schemas for analysis
    2. Changing dimensions and calendars
    3. Fan-out and join paths
    4. Metrics in an open specification

  3. Day 3 Show and investigate

    1. Dashboards start from a decision
    2. Comparisons and bilingual layout
    3. Load time is a design constraint
    4. Investigating an anomaly

  4. Day 4 Let an agent answer

    1. From question to query
    2. Certified questions
    3. With and without the semantic layer
    4. What the agent may answer

  5. Day 5 Capstone: fares and concessions

More about this course

Topics

Metrics

  • Rates, ratios, distinct counts and snapshots
  • Grain, time windows, Hijri and Gregorian calendars
  • Metric trees, owners and change control

Semantic models

  • Star schemas and slowly changing dimensions
  • Join paths and fan-out errors
  • Metrics in an open interchange specification

Dashboard design

  • Audience, decision and comparisons
  • Right-to-left layouts and accessibility
  • Pre-aggregation and load-time budgets

Investigating a metric

  • Drill-down and segmentation
  • Mix shift and Simpson's paradox

Conversational analytics

  • Certified questions, including ones to refuse
  • Wrong grain, wrong filter, invented metric
  • Access rules and classification-aware fields

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check on definitions, grain and joins (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a rubric: catalogue 25, semantic model 25, dashboard 20, agent evaluation 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live review reconciling a figure the assessor selects (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and each day's first lab. AI-tutor in the other labs, explaining errors without writing definitions. AI-full in the capstone, with a verification note for each AI-drafted definition; the day 4 agent is under test, not an aid.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
