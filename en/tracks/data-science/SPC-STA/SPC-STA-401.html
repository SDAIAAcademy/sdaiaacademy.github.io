SPC-STA-401 Bayesian Modelling and Decisions under Uncertainty

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-STA Statistics and Inference

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 10 weeks part-time, with weekly labs, graded assignments and a defended capstone

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-STA-301 Regression and Generalised Linear Modelling
  • Works with conditional probability and likelihoods at the depth of FND-MTH-103
  • Writes simulation code in Python or R

Description

Participants build Bayesian models for real decisions and judge models built by others. They set priors and check them by prior predictive simulation, fit models with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, diagnose the computation, and test fit with posterior predictive checks and cross-validation. They build hierarchical models for many small groups and verify their code by simulation-based calibration. The final weeks turn posteriors into decisions through expected utility and the value of further data.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement a Bayesian workflow from prior predictive simulation through fitting to sampler diagnosis.

    Given a model specification and public data.

    Evidence a notebook with prior checks, diagnostics and a repaired fit.

  2. Performance

    Design a hierarchical model for many small groups and defend the pooling it implies.

    Given synthetic outcomes for clinics, schools or benchmark tasks.

    Evidence shrinkage plots against no-pooling and complete-pooling fits.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate competing models with posterior predictive checks and approximate leave-one-out cross-validation.

    Given three candidate models of the same data.

    Evidence a comparison report with scores and their uncertainty.

  4. Performance

    Validate a model implementation by parameter recovery and simulation-based calibration.

    Given a model with a seeded coding error.

    Evidence a report locating the error, and the corrected model.

  5. Performance

    Decide between actions by expected utility under the posterior, including the value of collecting more data.

    Given a brief with costs, benefits and an option to sample further.

    Evidence a decision memo with sensitivity to prior and utility.

  6. Performance

    Review another analyst's Bayesian analysis for priors, computation, checking and reporting.

    Given an analysis with planted flaws.

    Evidence a written review with findings ranked by consequence.

Topics

Priors and posteriors

  • The model as a data-generating story
  • Eliciting priors from domain experts
  • Prior predictive simulation
  • Bayesian and frequentist answers compared

Computation

  • Grid approximation for intuition
  • Hamiltonian Monte Carlo
  • Convergence, effective sample size and divergences
  • Reparameterisation; limits of variational approximation

Hierarchical models

  • Partial pooling and shrinkage
  • Varying intercepts and slopes
  • Measurement error and missing data

Checking models

  • Posterior predictive checks
  • Approximate leave-one-out cross-validation and stacking
  • Simulation-based calibration
  • Prior sensitivity
  • Reporting a Bayesian analysis

Decisions

  • Utility and loss
  • Expected value of information
  • Small experiments and benchmarks
  • Bandits and Thompson sampling

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Probabilistic programming
Stan through CmdStanPy or CmdStanR, PyMC, NumPyro
Formula-based Bayesian regression
brms for R, Bambi for Python
Diagnostics and model comparison
ArviZ, loo and bayesplot for R

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off written examination (20%)
  • Weekly graded assignments (30%)
  • Peer review of a model (10%)
  • Capstone repository and memo (30%)
  • Oral defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the examination and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised examination and first attempts at derivations. AI-tutor in weekly labs and assignments. AI-full in the capstone, where every modelling choice is defended.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
