SPC-STA-301 Regression and Generalised Linear Modelling

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 36 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-STA Statistics and Inference

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

36 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-STA-201 Statistical Inference by Simulation
  • Reads matrix notation for a linear model
  • Writes analysis notebooks in Python or R

Description

Participants build regression models that answer stated questions and defend what the coefficients mean. They fit linear, logistic and count models with interactions and spline terms, diagnose misfit with residuals and fake-data simulation, compare cluster-adjusted errors with a first mixed-effects model for grouped data, and apply penalised regression when predictors are many. Results are reported as predicted quantities and marginal effects with intervals. The capstone is a modelling report on public data, defended live.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish descriptive, predictive and causal uses of a regression and the assumption each requires.

    Given five published regression claims.

    Evidence a classification matching the reference on four of five.

  2. Applied

    Implement linear, logistic and count models with interactions and splines, interpreted as predicted values and average marginal effects.

    Given continuous, binary and count outcomes.

    Evidence effect plots and intervals that match the reference.

  3. Performance

    Diagnose misfit with residuals, influence measures and fake-data simulation, and correct the model.

    Given a model with five seeded problems such as overdispersion and separation.

    Evidence a diagnosis log finding at least four, and a corrected model.

  4. Applied

    Analyse grouped data with cluster-adjusted standard errors and a random-intercept model, compared with a naive fit.

    Given repeated measurements nested in sites.

    Evidence a comparison table explaining the change in intervals.

  5. Applied

    Apply ridge and lasso penalties with cross-validated tuning and measure the stability of the selected predictors.

    Given many correlated predictors.

    Evidence a coefficient path, the chosen penalty and a resampling stability check.

  6. Performance

    Build and defend a regression model that answers a stated question, justifying terms, family and uncertainty.

    Given a public dataset and a policy or operations brief.

    Evidence a report and notebook defended in a live review.

Topics

Linear models as answers

  • Description, prediction and causal claims
  • Adjustment sets and the Table 2 fallacy (identification in SPC-EXP-302)
  • Contrasts, interactions and transformed outcomes
  • Splines for non-linear relationships

Generalised linear models

  • Links and outcome families
  • Logistic regression and separation
  • Poisson, negative binomial and offsets
  • Average marginal effects

Diagnostics

  • Residuals, hat values and influence
  • Overdispersion and calibration
  • Recovering known parameters from fake data

Grouped data

  • Heteroskedasticity-consistent and cluster-adjusted standard errors
  • Random intercepts and slopes
  • No, complete and partial pooling

Regularisation and reporting

  • Ridge, lasso and elastic net
  • Why intervals after selection mislead
  • Effect plots on the outcome scale

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analysis language and notebooks
Python with Jupyter or marimo, R with Quarto
Regression and penalised model fitting
statsmodels, R stats, glmnet, scikit-learn
Mixed-effects models
lme4 for R, glmmTMB for R, statsmodels
Marginal effects and diagnostics
marginaleffects for R and Python, DHARMa for R

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off practical: interpret model outputs and predict the effect of a specification change (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone on a five-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Live review (10%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the practical and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised practical and the first fit in each lab. AI-tutor for diagnostics and reporting. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
