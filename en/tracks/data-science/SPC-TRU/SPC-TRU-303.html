SPC-TRU-303 AI Safety and Alignment Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-TRU Trustworthy AI Science

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 10 weeks part-time, a weekly lecture and lab, four graded labs and a safety case

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-TRU-301 Evaluation Science for AI Models
  • Fine-tunes a small open-weight language model with adapters, as in SPC-DLR-306 or DEV-LLM-301
  • Has built a simple tool-using agent loop and read its transcripts

Description

Participants judge whether an AI system could cause serious harm and design controls that do not depend on the model being trustworthy. They write threat models, build dangerous-capability evaluations from safe proxy tasks in a sandbox, measure propensities such as sycophancy, reward hacking and sandbagging, red-team an open model by hand and with an automated attacker, and design protocols in which a weaker trusted model monitors a stronger untrusted one. Alignment training methods are in SPC-DLR-306 and SPC-DLR-401; application red teaming is DEV-SEC-302; governance controls are LDS-AIG-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Compare misuse, misalignment and structural threat models, and describe how safety frameworks tie capability thresholds to safeguards.

    Given two published frontier safety frameworks.

    Evidence written answers in the supervised short-answer paper.

  2. Applied

    Draft a threat model naming actors, capabilities, harm pathways and the evidence that would rule each pathway in or out.

    Given a fictional coding agent with repository and network access.

    Evidence a threat model scored against a rubric.

  3. Performance

    Build a dangerous-capability evaluation from safe proxy tasks, with elicitation effort and thresholds fixed in advance.

    Given sandboxed capture-the-flag and autonomy tasks and two open-weight models.

    Evidence pre-registered thresholds, an elicitation log, repeated-trial results and a capability statement.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate sycophancy, reward hacking and sandbagging propensities, and test whether results change when the model can tell it is being evaluated.

    Given hand-written and model-written test sets, coding tasks with exploitable checks and an open model.

    Evidence a propensity report with rates, intervals and a with-cue and without-cue comparison.

  5. Performance

    Design and stress-test a control protocol in which a trusted monitor reviews an untrusted model's actions.

    Given an agent environment, an attack policy and a fixed human audit budget.

    Evidence safety-usefulness curves for two protocols and a recommendation naming the attacks that beat each.

  6. Performance

    Defend a safety case that links each claim about a deployment to evaluation, red-team and monitoring evidence.

    Given the participant's course results and a fictional deployment.

    Evidence a structured safety case and a panel defence.

Topics

Threat models and safety frameworks

  • Misuse, misalignment and structural risks
  • Capability thresholds and safeguards
  • Safety cases: claims, arguments and evidence

Dangerous-capability evaluations

  • Cyber and autonomy proxies in sandboxes
  • Elicitation by scaffolding, fine-tuning and repeated attempts
  • Handling hazardous information

Propensity evaluations

  • Sycophancy, deception and sandbagging
  • Reward hacking and specification gaming
  • Emergent misalignment after narrow fine-tuning
  • Evaluation awareness

Red teaming

  • Manual and automated red teaming
  • Limits of refusal training
  • Turning findings into regression evaluations

Monitoring and control

  • Trusted and untrusted models
  • Monitoring reasoning versus monitoring actions
  • Audit budgets, resampling and deferral

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Evaluation and agent-task framework
Inspect, a custom pytest harness
Automated red teaming
garak, PyRIT
Isolated sandbox with egress disabled
Docker, gVisor
Open-weight model serving (GPU hours provided)
vLLM, llama.cpp

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off short-answer paper (15%)
  • Four graded labs (35%)
  • Safety case (35%)
  • Panel defence (15%)
To pass
60% on the supervised paper, 70% overall and at least 60% on the safety case

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised paper and the first threat model. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the safety case, where models may act as attackers, monitors and graders once the participant has validated them.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
