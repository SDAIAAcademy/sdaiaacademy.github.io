SPC-TRU-301 Evaluation Science for AI Models

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 40 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-TRU Trustworthy AI Science

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

40 h

Prerequisites

  • Runs and scores open-weight language models from Python
  • Computes bootstrap intervals and standard errors, as in SPC-STA-201
  • Runs containers from the command line

Description

Participants treat a benchmark score as a measurement and state what it measures, how precisely and at what cost. They audit a public benchmark for label errors and construct validity, test models for contamination, validate a model-based judge against human labels, report results with uncertainty and compute budgets, and build a small agent evaluation in a sandbox. Statistical depth is in SPC-EXP-402; evaluation of applications and agents for developers is in DEV-EVL-301 and DEV-EVL-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the main threats to evaluation validity: construct mismatch, contamination, saturation, test awareness and grader error.

    Given five published evaluation claims.

    Evidence a critique naming each claim's main threat and the check that would expose it.

  2. Applied

    Analyse a public benchmark for label errors and ambiguous items, and recompute model scores on the corrected set.

    Given a 300-item benchmark sample and outputs from three open-weight models.

    Evidence an audit log, an error rate with an interval and the resulting rank changes.

  3. Applied

    Test models for training-data contamination with overlap search, perturbed item variants and completion probes.

    Given a benchmark, an open corpus sample and two models with documented training data.

    Evidence a contamination report per model with the score gap on perturbed variants.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate a model-based judge against human labels, measure its position, length and self-preference biases, and decide where it may replace human graders.

    Given 200 pairwise comparisons labelled blind by the cohort before any judge output is seen.

    Evidence a validation report with agreement and bias estimates, each with an interval, and a reasoned decision.

  5. Performance

    Design an agent evaluation that reports success over repeated trials, cost per solved task and failure categories.

    Given ten sandboxed multi-step tasks with programmatic checkers and two token budgets.

    Evidence transcripts, success at one and at five trials by budget, and the environment bugs found.

  6. Performance

    Build a new evaluation for a stated capability and report results with uncertainty, compute budget and known limits.

    Given a capability chosen by the participant and three open-weight models.

    Evidence an evaluation package with datasheet and harness, defended in a viva.

Topics

Measurement and benchmark design

  • Constructs and what a score can claim
  • Item writing, keys and adjudication
  • Item response theory in brief
  • Saturation; private and refreshed test sets
  • Datasheets with Arabic and dialect coverage

Contamination and test awareness

  • Overlap search against training corpora
  • Perturbed variants, canaries and dated test sets
  • Models that detect tests

Model-based judges

  • Pointwise, pairwise and reference-guided judging
  • Position, length and self-preference bias
  • When a judge may replace people

Reporting under a budget

  • Clustered and paired comparisons
  • Prompts, settings and seeds as conditions
  • Rankings that shift with test-time compute

Agent evaluations

  • Sandboxes and programmatic checkers
  • Repeated trials, transcripts and environment bugs

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Evaluation harness
Inspect, lm-evaluation-harness
Open-weight model inference (GPU hours provided)
vLLM, llama.cpp
Agent sandbox
Docker, Podman
Corpus overlap search
OpenSearch, a custom n-gram index
Blind human labelling
Label Studio, a shuffled-pair form in a notebook

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check: critique a claim and compute an interval by hand (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone package on a six-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Viva (10%)
To pass
60% on the supervised check, 70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and for labelling, so the judge is validated against unassisted labels. AI-tutor in other labs. AI-full in the capstone, with every result defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
