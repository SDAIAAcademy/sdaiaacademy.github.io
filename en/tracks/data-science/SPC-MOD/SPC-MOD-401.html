SPC-MOD-401 Foundation Models for Structured Data

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-MOD Machine Learning and Modelling

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Workshop: two days with labs, guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-MOD-201 Machine Learning for Tabular Data: Baselines and Validation
  • SPC-MOD-302 Forecasting and Time Series
  • Tunes boosted trees under a fixed budget and runs rolling-origin backtests

Description

Participants decide whether a pretrained model for tables, time series or relational data should replace or join a tuned baseline. They test tabular in-context learners against tuned boosted trees across sample sizes and zero-shot forecasters against statistical benchmarks, survey relational and graph pretraining, audit leaderboard claims and weigh licences, cost and data residency. Labs run on a free notebook GPU or a provided accelerator.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Evaluate a tabular foundation model against a tuned, ensembled boosted-tree baseline across training-set sizes.

    Given three public datasets and an equal tuning budget per method.

    Evidence learning curves with intervals showing where, if anywhere, it wins.

  2. Performance

    Evaluate zero-shot and fine-tuned forecasters against seasonal naive, exponential smoothing and a tuned global boosted-tree model.

    Given a public multi-series dataset with covariates.

    Evidence a backtest report of point and probabilistic scores by horizon.

  3. Performance

    Audit a published claim for pretraining overlap, unequal tuning budgets and leaderboard overfitting.

    Given a technical report and its benchmark results.

    Evidence an audit note classing each claim as supported, unsupported or untestable.

  4. Performance

    Design a reusable evaluation protocol and decide on adoption for one structured-data task, including licence, hosting and data-flow conditions.

    Given a synthetic use case with stated requirements and a data classification.

    Evidence a protocol, a permitted-use table and a decision memo defended before a panel.

Topics

How the models work

  • In-context learning on tables
  • Pretrained forecasters and covariates
  • Relational deep learning and graph foundation models

When they win

  • Small-data and many-series regimes
  • Tuned, ensembled baselines
  • Cost and latency per prediction

Evaluation caveats

  • Pretraining and test overlap
  • Leaderboard overfitting
  • Uncertainty in rankings

Adoption conditions

  • Non-commercial and research-only weights
  • Hosted against local inference
  • Version pinning and hand-over

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Tabular foundation models
TabICL, TabPFN family, under its licence
Pretrained forecasters
Chronos family, TimesFM, checking its weight licence
Tuned baselines and ensembling
LightGBM or XGBoost, StatsForecast, AutoGluon
Public benchmarks to audit
TabArena, GIFT-Eval, fev-bench

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check on evaluation design and leakage (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone with panel defence (50%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor in the curve and backtest labs. AI-full in the audit lab and capstone.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
