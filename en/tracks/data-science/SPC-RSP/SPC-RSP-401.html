SPC-RSP-401 Reproduction Capstone: Reproduce and Extend a Published Result

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Studio
  • 100 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-RSP Research Practice and Frontier

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Studio: twelve weeks part-time, individual and coached, with proposal, mid-point and panel reviews

Total hours

100 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-RSP-301 Research Practice Studio
  • Has completed a model development course at level 300, such as SPC-DLR-302, SPC-DLR-303 or SPC-GEN-301, or can show equivalent work
  • Writes research-grade training code and profiles a training run

Description

Each participant chooses a result from a recent peer-reviewed paper, reproduces it within an agreed compute budget, extends it with one well-designed experiment, and reports both against a reproducibility checklist. A mid-point review checks the reproduction before extension starts. Credit goes to measurement quality, not agreement with the paper: a carefully measured failure to reproduce, with its likely causes tested, can earn full marks.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Defend the choice of a target result on feasibility, licence position and compute budget.

    Given a shortlist of recent papers and a fixed allocation of accelerator hours.

    Evidence an approved proposal with a compute estimate and a risk list.

  2. Performance

    Reproduce the target result, measuring variation across seeds and logging every deviation from the paper.

    Given released code where it exists, otherwise the paper's description.

    Evidence a reproduction table with intervals, a deviation log and a rerunnable repository.

  3. Performance

    Diagnose the causes of any gap between the reproduced and the published result.

    Given the reproduction runs and the original artefacts.

    Evidence a gap analysis that tests at least two candidate causes with controlled runs.

  4. Performance

    Design and run one extension that tests the claim beyond its original conditions, such as a new dataset, scale, language or ablation.

    Given the reproduced baseline and the remaining compute budget.

    Evidence an extension plan dated before its runs, and results with uncertainty.

  5. Performance

    Deliver a reproducibility report that meets a published checklist for code, data, compute, statistics and ethics.

    Given a workshop-paper template and the checklist.

    Evidence the report and completed checklist, scored by two reviewers.

  6. Performance

    Defend the findings, including any negative result, under questioning from a panel.

    Given a 20-minute defence that includes a reduced-scale rerun.

    Evidence panel scores for rigour, honesty and judgement.

Topics

Choosing a target

  • What makes a result reproducible within budget
  • Licences of code, data and model weights
  • Estimating compute from the paper

Reproduction method

  • Environment capture and the limits of determinism
  • Seeds, variance and how many runs are enough
  • Deviation logs and contacting authors

Diagnosing gaps

  • Unreported hyperparameters and preprocessing
  • Evaluation mismatches and contamination
  • Hardware and numerical precision effects

Extension design

  • Out-of-distribution and cross-lingual tests, including Arabic
  • Ablations and scaling probes
  • Plans and stopping rules fixed before the runs

Reporting

  • Reproducibility checklists
  • Negative results that others can use
  • Releasing artefacts responsibly

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep learning framework
PyTorch, JAX
Experiment tracking
MLflow, TensorBoard, Weights & Biases
Environment capture
containers with lock files, conda or Nix environments
Paper and artefact discovery
OpenReview, arXiv with linked code repositories
Statistics
SciPy, statsmodels
Compute
an Academy-provided accelerator allocation, a laptop CPU for debugging

How the work is assessed

  • Proposal and mid-point reviews (20%)
  • Reproducibility report and repository, scored by two reviewers on a rubric weighted to measurement quality (50%)
  • Panel defence with live rerun (30%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the defence, and every reported number rerunnable; agreement with the original paper is not a criterion

Use of AI in the course

AI-tutor in the proposal clinic. AI-full, coding and research agents included, for the reproduction and extension, with all use disclosed in the report and every number owned by the participant. AI-off in the defence.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
