DS-205 Designing and Reading Experiments

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-200 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Assistant level

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-201 Statistical Inference by Simulation
  • Computes a proportion, a difference in means and a confidence interval
  • Writes a simulation loop in Python or R

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants design randomised experiments for a fictional municipal permits office and read the results without fooling themselves. They turn a decision question into a pre-registration with a hypothesis, primary and guardrail metrics, a stop rule and a unit of randomisation, compute sample size by formula and by simulation, analyse a synthetic experiment log, check that randomisation worked, and measure how peeking, many metrics and a mismatched unit inflate false wins. Variance reduction and sequential tests are left to DS-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish a randomised experiment, a quasi-experiment and an observational comparison, and explain the causal claim each supports.

    Given six study descriptions from public services.

    Evidence a classification with the supported claim, at least five of six correct.

  2. Applied

    Draft a pre-registration stating the decision, hypothesis, unit of randomisation, primary and guardrail metrics, stop rule and analysis plan.

    Given the office's question about renewal reminders.

    Evidence a one-page pre-registration that passes a reviewer's checklist.

  3. Applied

    Compute the sample size and run time needed to detect a minimum effect of interest and check it by simulation.

    Given baseline renewal rates, daily traffic and the office's minimum effect.

    Evidence a notebook with the formula result, a simulated power curve and the chosen duration.

  4. Applied

    Analyse a completed two-arm experiment with a difference in proportions, a confidence interval and a sample ratio mismatch test.

    Given a synthetic log of 60,000 renewal notices.

    Evidence a result note with estimate, interval, checks and a recommendation.

  5. Applied

    Measure by simulation the false-positive rate caused by peeking, many metrics and analysis at the wrong unit.

    Given simulated A/A tests with no true effect.

    Evidence a table of false-win rates for each trap, with and without the correct procedure.

  6. Performance

    Review a reported experiment and decide whether it supports the decision it claims to support.

    Given write-ups with seeded flaws, one in class and one in the capstone.

    Evidence a review memo per write-up naming each flaw, its consequence and the decision the evidence does support, defended live.

Topics

From decision to design

  • Decision, hypothesis and estimand
  • Primary, secondary and guardrail metrics
  • Pre-registration and stop rules

Randomisation

  • Simple, stratified and cluster assignment
  • Balance and sample ratio checks
  • Interference between units

Sample size and power

  • Minimum effect of interest
  • Formula and simulation
  • Run time and weekly cycles

Reading a result

  • Differences, intervals and effect sizes
  • Practical against statistical significance
  • Subgroups read with care

Common traps

  • Peeking
  • Many metrics
  • Wrong unit of analysis
  • Novelty effects and attrition

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analysis environment and notebooks
Python with uv and JupyterLab or marimo, R with Quarto
Statistics and power calculation
SciPy and statsmodels, R with the pwr package
Dataframes and analytical SQL engine
DuckDB, Polars, pandas

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised entry quiz and exit check with items in the badge-exam style (20%)
  • Capstone pack on a rubric: design and pre-registration 25, power and sample size 25, analysis and checks 20, review memo 20, writing and reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence of one design choice (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and the first pass of each sample-size and analysis step. AI-tutor for simulation code, hinting only. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
