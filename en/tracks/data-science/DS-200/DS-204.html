Description

Participants predict which trainees of a fictional vocational training fund are likely not to complete, so that advisers can offer support early. On synthetic enrolment data with repeated trainees and cohorts, they climb a baseline ladder to tuned gradient-boosted trees, test an automated or pretrained challenger, choose validation that mirrors use, audit for leakage, set a threshold, break errors down by subgroup and write a model card. Deployment is left to AE-205 and AE-305.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify the leakage type and the fitting validation scheme in short tabular modelling cases.

    Given six described problems and their data-collection process.

    Evidence a table naming the leak and the scheme for each, at least five of six correct.

  2. Applied

    Apply a baseline ladder from a constant predictor to tuned gradient-boosted trees, logging every run.

    Given identical folds and a fixed tuning budget.

    Evidence a run log with score and fold spread per rung.

  3. Applied

    Implement validation that matches the data structure and measure how far a random split overstates performance.

    Given repeated trainees across five yearly cohorts.

    Evidence random, grouped and time-ordered scores with the gap explained.

  4. Applied

    Test an automated or pretrained challenger against the tuned model under identical folds and time budget.

    Given the champion model and a challenger configuration.

    Evidence a paired comparison over folds with an interval and a one-line decision.

  5. Applied

    Analyse calibration, the decision threshold and held-out errors by subgroup, with intervals that reflect slice size.

    Given predictions joined to region, gender, age band and provider, and adviser capacity.

    Evidence a calibration plot, a threshold with its precision and recall, and a subgroup error table.

  6. Performance

    Build a validated tabular model from framing to model card and defend its validation scheme and limits.

    Given a synthetic case or an approved public tabular problem.

    Evidence a public repository with run log, leakage audit, subgroup results and model card, defended live.

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Frame and baseline
1.1A prediction serves a decisionWrite target, unit, prediction time and adviser action for the question
1.2The baseline ladderScore a constant predictor and a three-rule baseline; log both
1.3Linear models firstFit a regularised logistic model in a pipeline; log score and spread
1.4Features from historyAdd two history features from past records only; pass a time-travel test
Day 2 Trees and challengers
2.1Gradient-boosted treesTrain boosted trees with defaults; compare training and validation scores
2.2Tuning under a budgetRun a 20-trial search in nested folds and record the gain
2.3Automated and pretrained challengersRun a challenger on the same folds and time budget
2.4Is the challenger betterCompute the paired fold difference with an interval and decide
Day 3 Validation and leakage
3.1The split decides the scoreScore one model under random, grouped and time-ordered splits
3.2Kinds of leakageClassify six leaks from other projects by type
3.3Hunting planted leaksFind four planted leaks with timestamps, duplicates and importance scores
3.4Reading someone else's notebookList what makes a colleague's notebook score untrustworthy
Day 4 Errors, groups and documentation
4.1Thresholds for rare outcomesPick a threshold on the precision-recall curve that fits adviser capacity
4.2CalibrationCalibrate the boosted model; compare expected and observed by decile
4.3Errors by subgroupBreak errors down by region, gender and age band with intervals
4.4The model cardDraft the model card and choose the capstone problem

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analysis environment and notebooks
Python with uv and JupyterLab or marimo, R with Quarto
Machine learning library with splitters and pipelines
scikit-learn, tidymodels
Gradient-boosted trees
LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost
Automated or pretrained challenger
AutoGluon, a tabular foundation model used within its licence
Subgroup metrics
Fairlearn, grouped metrics written in pandas

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised entry quiz and exit check with items in the badge-exam style (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a rubric: framing and baseline 15, validation and leakage 25, models and comparison 20, threshold, calibration and subgroup errors 25, model card and reproducibility 15 (60%)
  • Live defence of the validation choice (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and the first attempt at each split, leakage hunt and subgroup table. AI-tutor for library syntax. AI-full in the capstone, with prompts and agent transcripts committed and every choice defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
