DS-203 Exploratory Analysis and Visualisation

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-101 Python for Data and AI Work
  • FN-104 SQL and Data Wrangling
  • DS-201 Statistical Inference by Simulation
  • Makes a basic chart in any plotting library

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants answer a fictional regional tourism board's questions by exploring its data, starting every analysis from written questions. They profile tables, examine distributions, relationships and change over time, build charts with a grammar of graphics and scales that do not mislead, and test by resampling whether a pattern could be chance. They repair misleading charts and write findings that state what is and is not shown, in a notebook that reruns from raw data.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish exploratory from confirmatory analysis and explain why a pattern found by exploring needs a fresh test.

    Given five findings from a past board report.

    Evidence each finding marked exploratory or confirmed, with the check it would need.

  2. Applied

    Prepare an analysis plan listing questions, units of observation, variables and the comparison that answers each.

    Given the director's brief and the data dictionary.

    Evidence a one-page plan checked against a checklist before any chart is drawn.

  3. Applied

    Analyse distributions, relationships and change over time with summaries and charts suited to each variable type.

    Given three years of visitor, occupancy and survey tables.

    Evidence a chart, a summary statistic and a two-sentence reading per question.

  4. Applied

    Use a grammar of graphics to map variables to position, colour and facets with honest scales, baselines and labels.

    Given six misleading charts from earlier reports.

    Evidence six repaired charts, each with the fault and the fix named.

  5. Applied

    Test whether an apparent difference or correlation could arise by chance, using permutation or bootstrap resampling.

    Given three patterns the board wants to announce.

    Evidence each pattern's resampling result and a decision to report, qualify or drop it.

  6. Performance

    Deliver a reproducible exploratory report that states findings, uncertainty, open questions and limits for a non-specialist reader.

    Given an unseen dataset and a named audience.

    Evidence a rendered report and notebook that rerun from raw data, defended live.

Topics

Questions before charts

  • Analysis plans and units of observation
  • Exploratory versus confirmatory work
  • Profiling a new table

Distributions and relationships

  • Histograms, density and box plots
  • Scatter plots and overplotting
  • Rates and denominators
  • Change over time and seasonality

Grammar of graphics

  • Marks, encodings and scales
  • Small multiples
  • Colour-blind-safe palettes
  • Arabic and bilingual labels

Honest charts

  • Baselines and axis ranges
  • Aggregation and Simpson's paradox
  • Showing uncertainty

Checking and reporting

  • Chance patterns and resampling
  • Counting the comparisons made
  • Findings, limits and next questions

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analysis environment and notebooks
Python with uv and JupyterLab or marimo, R with Quarto
Dataframes and analytical SQL engine
DuckDB, Polars, pandas
Plotting with a grammar of graphics
Vega-Altair, plotnine, ggplot2
Resampling
NumPy and SciPy, R with the infer package

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised entry quiz and exit check with items in the badge-exam style (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a rubric: plan and questions 15, analysis and charts 30, honesty and uncertainty 20, chance checks 15, reproducibility and writing 20 (60%)
  • Live defence with one chart redrawn on request (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and the first draft of every chart reading. AI-tutor for plotting syntax, hinting only. AI-full in the capstone, with assisted charts and sentences marked in the notebook and defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
