Prerequisites

  • FN-101 Python for Data and AI Work
  • FN-103 Mathematics for Machine Learning: Vectors, Gradients and Probability

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants learn inference by computing it. On synthetic data from a fictional water utility whose true population is known, they draw repeated samples, build bootstrap intervals, run permutation tests beside classical ones, and report effect sizes with intervals instead of bare p-values. They see how testing 30 districts at once produces false findings and correct for it, then fit and read a first linear regression.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain what a sampling distribution, a standard error, a confidence interval and a p-value describe, and identify common misreadings.

    Given eight statements from utility reports.

    Evidence a marked sheet with reasons, at least six of eight correct.

  2. Applied

    Compute bootstrap intervals for a mean, a median and a group difference, resampling the correct unit.

    Given skewed repair times with the population known.

    Evidence each interval with its coverage over 1,000 simulated samples.

  3. Applied

    Implement a permutation test, compare it with the matching classical test and report an effect size with an interval.

    Given satisfaction scores for two contractors.

    Evidence a script reporting both p-values and the effect size.

  4. Applied

    Apply family-wise and false discovery rate corrections to many tests and measure the false findings without them.

    Given 30 districts of unequal size, five with a planted difference.

    Evidence true and false discoveries under no correction, Holm and Benjamini-Hochberg.

  5. Applied

    Analyse a linear regression of water consumption on household size and temperature, with slope intervals and residual checks.

    Given 2,000 synthetic metered households.

    Evidence a regression summary, a residual plot and a two-sentence interpretation.

  6. Performance

    Deliver an inference brief that answers a manager's question with an estimate, an interval, a test, an effect size and stated limits.

    Given a question and a dataset not seen in class.

    Evidence a two-page brief and a notebook that reruns from raw data, defended live.

Topics

Variation and estimation

  • Population, sample, parameter and statistic
  • Sampling distributions and standard error
  • Skew, outliers and the median

Bootstrap intervals

  • Resampling the right unit
  • Percentile intervals and coverage
  • When the bootstrap fails

Testing

  • The null hypothesis as a shuffling rule
  • Classical tests as approximations
  • What a p-value is and is not
  • Effect size, Type I and Type II errors

Many comparisons

  • Forking paths
  • Family-wise error and false discovery rate
  • Analysis plans written before the data

A first regression

  • Least squares and slope intervals
  • Residual checks
  • Confounding and a second variable

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Variation
1.1A sample is one draw of manyDraw 1,000 samples of 50 households and plot their means
1.2Standard error by simulation and by formulaCompare simulated and formula standard errors at three sample sizes
1.3Skew, outliers and the medianMeasure how one extreme repair time moves mean and median
1.4Describing a sample honestlySummarise repair times in five numbers and a histogram
Day 2 Intervals
2.1The bootstrap ideaBootstrap the median repair time and compare with the truth
2.2Coverage is a testable claimCount how often two interval methods cover the truth in 500 studies
2.3Resample the right unitBootstrap satisfaction by household and by district; explain the difference
2.4Intervals for differences and ratiosBuild intervals for the difference and ratio of contractor medians
Day 3 Tests
3.1A test is a shuffling ruleRun a permutation test and compare its p-value with a t-test
3.2p-values and their misreadingsMark eight report sentences about p-values right or wrong, with reasons
3.3Effect size and practical significanceReport the contractor effect with an interval against the utility's threshold
3.4Thirty districts, many false alarmsTest 30 districts with and without correction; note where small districts land
Day 4 Regression and reporting
4.1Least squares as a summaryFit consumption on household size; state the slope in a manager's units
4.2Slopes with intervalsBootstrap the slope and compare with the reported standard error
4.3Residuals and confoundingAdd temperature, plot residuals and explain why the slope moved
4.4Writing an inference briefDraft an estimate, interval and limits paragraph; choose the capstone question

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised entry quiz and exit check with items in the badge-exam style (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a rubric: question and estimand 15, intervals and coverage 25, tests and effect sizes 25, regression and limits 20, reproducibility 15 (60%)
  • Live defence of one result (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for quizzes and each day's first core computation. AI-tutor in lab extensions, hinting one step at a time without writing the simulation. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
