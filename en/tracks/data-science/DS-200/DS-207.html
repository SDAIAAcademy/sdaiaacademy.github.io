DS-207 Responsible Data Practice and Conduct in Analytical Work

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-200 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Assistant level

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-106 Responsible Data and AI Practice in the Kingdom
  • Computes rates and a confidence interval for a proportion by group in SQL or a dataframe
  • Has written a short analytical report for a non-specialist reader

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants handle an analytical engagement for a fictional university's student-success unit. They test requests against the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), classify the data and decide what may be shared with a government partner, suppress small cells in aggregate outputs, compare outcome and error rates across student groups, keep an analysis log and a datasheet, and handle three stakeholder conversations: scoping, pushing back on an unsafe request and explaining limits. Deeper de-identification and fairness audits are in DS-309.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the PDPL principles and the classification and sharing rules an analysis engages, from lawful basis to data subjects' rights and transfer.

    Given five analysis requests from the case.

    Evidence a note per request naming the rules engaged, at least four of five right.

  2. Applied

    Classify the datasets and outputs of an analysis and decide what may be shared, with whom and in what form.

    Given the student records, a partner's sharing request and the classification policy.

    Evidence a classification and sharing table with the control each decision requires.

  3. Applied

    Apply aggregation and small-cell suppression to results tables and check that no student can be singled out.

    Given draft tables by college, nationality and disability status.

    Evidence release-ready tables and a log of each suppressed cell and the reason.

  4. Applied

    Analyse referral and error rates across student groups and report differences with their uncertainty and possible causes.

    Given a support-referral rule and its results by group.

    Evidence rates with intervals and two possible bias types with the check that separates them.

  5. Applied

    Prepare a datasheet and an analysis log that let a reviewer trace each result to its data, decisions and approvals.

    Given the case analysis and a template.

    Evidence a datasheet and log a peer uses to answer five audit questions.

  6. Performance

    Lead a stakeholder conversation that rescopes an unsafe or unclear request into a lawful, answerable question, and record the agreement.

    Given a role-played sponsor asking for an individual-level risk list.

    Evidence an observed role-play scored on a rubric and a written scope note.

Topics

The law in an analysis

  • Lawful basis, purpose and minimisation
  • Data subjects' rights and retention
  • Secondary use, transfers and breaches

Classification and sharing

  • The four classification levels
  • Sharing requests and conditions
  • Small cells and what may go into AI tools

Bias and fairness basics

  • Where bias enters
  • Rates and errors across groups
  • What fairness metrics cannot settle

Documentation

  • Datasheets and analysis logs
  • Decision records and versioned releases

Working with stakeholders

  • Scoping and success criteria
  • Saying no with an alternative
  • Escalating to the data protection officer

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

1.1What the law asks of an analystMap five requests to the PDPL principles they engage
1.2Purpose, minimisation and secondary useCut an extract to the fields the purpose needs, recording why

1.4Sharing requestsAssess a partner's sharing request and draft approval conditions

2.4Naming the biasMatch two observed gaps to bias types in the national guide

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

DuckDB, Polars, pandas
Personal data detection
Presidio, regular-expression rules
Group metrics
Fairlearn, grouped metrics written in SQL or pandas
Documentation templates
Markdown datasheet templates in Git, Quarto documents

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check, AI-off, with scenario items in the style of the badge exam (20%)
  • Capstone pack scored on a rubric: legal and classification decisions 25, release tables and suppression 20, group comparison 20, documentation 20, scope note 15 (60%)
  • Observed role-play and live defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and role-plays. AI-tutor when reading regulations, quoting and explaining the text without making the decision. AI-full for drafting capstone documents, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
