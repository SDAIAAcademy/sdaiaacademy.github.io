Description

Participants build the data an analysis depends on for Bayyin Consumer Protection Office, a fictional regulator holding 30,000 synthetic complaint records in Arabic and English. They write a labelling guideline, pilot it, measure agreement and adjudicate, run golden-question checks and review queues across a rater team, turn analysts' requirements into quality rules and a data contract, and package the preparation as a reproducible pipeline that stops on those rules. Measuring model-made labels is DS-306; platform-scale data engineering is AE-306.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Draft a labelling guideline with definitions, decision rules and edge cases, and revise it after a pilot.

    Given 300 synthetic complaints labelled by three raters.

    Evidence a versioned guideline whose change log ties each revision to a measured disagreement.

  2. Applied

    Compute chance-corrected agreement by category and rater, and adjudicate the disagreements.

    Given two rounds of pilot labels.

    Evidence an agreement report with intervals and an adjudicated set.

  3. Applied

    Configure a labelling workflow with golden-question checks, review queues and rater-drift monitoring.

    Given a simulated round of 2,000 items from six raters, one of whom drifts.

    Evidence a quality-control report that flags the drifting rater and states cost per accepted label.

  4. Applied

    Implement quality rules with thresholds and severities, and a data contract for the prepared table.

    Given four source tables with seeded defects.

    Evidence rules and a contract that catch each defect and reject a breaking schema change.

  5. Performance

    Build a reproducible preparation pipeline from raw extracts to an analysis-ready table that stops on a blocking rule.

    Given a repository with continuous integration.

    Evidence run logs that pass on clean data and fail on a seeded defect from a clean checkout.

  6. Performance

    Diagnose why an analytical figure changed after a data refresh and decide on the correction.

    Given two runs, their lineage records and a stakeholder asking which figure is right.

    Evidence an incident note with root cause, affected outputs and correction.

Topics

Labelling as measurement

  • Label schemas, decision rules and guideline versions
  • Arabic dialects and code-switching
  • Rater training and workload

Agreement and labelling operations

  • Kappa and Krippendorff's alpha
  • Adjudication methods
  • Golden questions, review queues and rater drift
  • Cost per accepted label

Quality rules and contracts

  • Quality dimensions as rules with owners
  • Duplicates and merchant matching
  • Contract clauses in an open standard

Pipelines for analysis

  • Lock files and deterministic outputs
  • Warn, block or quarantine
  • Lineage for tracing a changed figure

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Annotation tool
Label Studio, Argilla, a spreadsheet with locked validation rules
Validation framework
Pandera, Great Expectations, Soda Core
Data contract specification
Open Data Contract Standard, contract checks compiled to SQL tests
Data processing and pipeline runner
DuckDB or Polars with a task runner, Dagster with OpenLineage events
Language model for pre-labels, through a chat-completions style endpoint
a locally served open-weight model, a free hosted open-weight model

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check on agreement, quality dimensions and rules (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a rubric: guideline and agreement 25, rules and contract 25, pipeline 25, incident handling 15, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live review of a defect the assessor seeds (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first lab of days 1 and 3. AI-tutor in the other labs, explaining errors without writing the guideline. AI-full in the capstone, defended live; model pre-labels are checked by people, never accepted unseen.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
