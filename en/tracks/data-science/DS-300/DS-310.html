DS-310 Problem Framing and Analytical Review

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-300 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Associate level

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-204 Machine Learning for Tabular Data: Baselines and Validation
  • DS-205 Designing and Reading Experiments
  • Has completed at least one analysis for a stakeholder
  • Reads a notebook in Python or R and reruns it

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Associate level.

Description

Participants practise the work before and after an analysis, using requests from Ghadeer Water Company, a fictional water utility. They turn vague requests into decision questions with an estimand, a metric and a decision rule, interview a role-played manager, use domain knowledge of metering, billing and repair to see where the data mislead, and test whether data and deadline make the question answerable. They then review analyses by peers and by an assistant and write review notes ranked by consequence. Metric definition is DS-307; team review standards are DS-407.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a decision question with estimand, population, metric and decision rule from a vague request.

    Given five requests from the utility's managers.

    Evidence framing cards a peer can plan from without further questions.

  2. Applied

    Analyse a metric set for proxy failure and gaming, and propose guardrail metrics.

    Given the utility's metric tree for water loss and customer service.

    Evidence an annotated tree with two guardrails and the behaviour each prevents.

  3. Applied

    Use domain documents and an expert interview to map how the data behind a metric are produced.

    Given a briefing pack on metering, billing cycles and leak repair, and a role-played engineer.

    Evidence a process map marking three points where the data can mislead.

  4. Performance

    Design an analysis plan stating data, method, threats to validity and the result that would change the decision.

    Given a framed question, a data inventory and a two-week deadline.

    Evidence a two-page plan scored against a rubric.

  5. Performance

    Review an analysis for errors in question fit, data handling, method, inference and communication.

    Given three seeded-flaw notebooks, one generated by an assistant.

    Evidence review notes ranking the critical flaws by consequence, each with a fix.

  6. Performance

    Decide whether a request needs description, an experiment, a causal analysis, a predictive model or no analysis.

    Given a role-played sponsor whose constraints change during the meeting.

    Evidence a recommendation defended against the assessor's challenge.

Topics

From request to question

  • Decision, estimand and population
  • Descriptive, predictive and causal questions
  • Process models such as CRISP-DM and their limits

Stakeholders and domain knowledge

  • Decision makers, users and affected groups
  • Interviewing for constraints and evidence
  • How operational processes produce data

Metrics and plans

  • Metric trees, proxies and Goodhart's law
  • Plans fixed before outcomes are seen
  • Threats to validity

Analytical review

  • A checklist from question to conclusion
  • Ranking flaws by consequence
  • Reviewing assistant-generated analyses

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 From request to question
1.1Requests are not questionsReframe two vague requests as decision questions with an estimand (AI-off)
1.2Question typesClassify eight requests as descriptive, predictive, causal or unanswerable
1.3Interviewing a stakeholderInterview a role-played operations manager and record constraints and acceptable evidence
1.4Feasibility and scopeCheck one question against the data inventory and the deadline and cut its scope
Day 2 Know the domain, plan the work
2.1How the data are madeMap the meter-to-bill process and mark where the data can mislead
2.2Metrics and proxiesStress-test the water-loss metric tree for gaming and add guardrails
2.3Analysis plansWrite a plan for a framed question, including the result that would change the decision
2.4Threats to validitySwap plans with a peer and list each other's three main threats
Day 3 Review others' work
3.1A review checklistApply the checklist to a short notebook and score it
3.2Finding the flaws that matterReview a seeded-flaw notebook and rank its flaws by consequence
3.3Reviewing assistant-generated analysisRerun an assistant-generated analysis, find where it fails and write a verification note
3.4Review notes people act onRewrite review notes so each states the problem, its consequence and a fix

a free hosted open-weight model, any commercial coding assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised framing check without AI assistance: three requests framed in 40 minutes (20%)
  • Capstone framing card, plan and review notes on a rubric (50%)
  • Live defence with an unseen change of constraint (30%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the live defence

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first framing lab. AI-tutor in the other labs, asking questions rather than supplying framings. AI-full in the capstone, with an assistant-generated analysis also used as an object of review.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
