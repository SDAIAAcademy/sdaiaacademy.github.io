Description

Participants run the statistics behind an experimentation programme that includes tests of AI features. On a synthetic event stream from Sufrah, a fictional food-delivery marketplace, they check tests for trust problems, analyse triggered exposure, reduce variance with pre-experiment data, compute intervals for ratio metrics, design sequential tests for daily monitoring, set guardrails for latency, cost and harm, and measure interference through shared couriers, ending on a mock review board that decides launch, iterate or stop.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Apply regression adjustment with pre-experiment data and report the sample-size saving.

    Given a test with 28 days of pre-period data per customer.

    Evidence unadjusted and adjusted intervals with the effective sample-size gain.

  2. Applied

    Compute intervals for ratio metrics with the delta method and with a bootstrap over the randomisation unit.

    Given order-level logs from a customer-randomised test.

    Evidence two agreeing intervals and a note on why the naive one is too narrow.

  3. Applied

    Implement group sequential and always-valid tests and measure their false-positive rates under daily monitoring by simulation.

    Given a simulator with and without a true effect.

    Evidence error rates at or below the nominal level, with expected stopping times.

  4. Performance

    Design the metric set, power and decision rule for launching an AI feature.

    Given a proposed AI-ranked search change with goals and expected traffic.

    Evidence a pre-registration with guardrail thresholds, sample size and a written decision rule.

  5. Performance

    Diagnose interference in a marketplace test and design a cluster or switchback alternative with its power.

    Given a simulator in which treated customers take couriers from control customers.

    Evidence a memo quantifying the naive bias and the power of the new design.

  6. Performance

    Review experiment write-ups and decide launch, iterate or stop for each.

    Given five write-ups with seeded faults such as sample ratio mismatch, peeking and novelty decay.

    Evidence a review log scored against reference decisions.

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Trustworthy experiments
1.1Assignment, exposure and triggeringTrace assignment and exposure in SQL and compare the triggered effect with the diluted overall effect
1.2Sample ratio mismatch and A/A testsRun A/A and sample ratio checks on three tests and name the faulty one
1.3Metrics for AI featuresDefine a primary metric and latency, cost and harm guardrails for an AI-ranked search test
1.4Power for heavy-tailed metricsSize the search test by simulation on revenue per user and compare with the textbook formula
Day 2 Precision
2.1Variance and heavy tailsMeasure how capping order value changes variance and bias
2.2Regression adjustmentAdjust for pre-period behaviour and report the reduction in interval width
2.3Ratio metricsCompute a delta-method interval for orders per session and check it by bootstrap
2.4StratificationEstimate a city-stratified test with and without post-stratification
Day 3 Monitoring and interference
3.1Group sequential designsAdd group sequential boundaries to the daily-peeking simulator and measure the false-positive rate
3.2Always-valid testsRun an always-valid sequential test on the same simulator and compare stopping times
3.3Interference in marketplacesMeasure the bias of a customer-randomised dispatch test
3.4Cluster and switchback designsDesign a city-by-hour switchback and simulate its power
Day 4 Decide and review
4.1Testing AI featuresAnalyse a chat-assistant test with latency, cost and complaint guardrails
4.2Many segmentsCorrect 20 segment results for false discoveries and report what survives
4.3Writing the recommendationDraft a one-page launch recommendation with effect, interval and guardrails
4.4The review boardDecide two seeded write-ups and choose the capstone change

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on power, intervals and trust problems (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: design and power 25, analysis 25, monitoring and guardrails 20, recommendation 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Review-board defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and first attempts at power and interval calculations. AI-tutor in labs, hinting without writing the analysis. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
