DS-304 Forecasting and Time Series

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-300 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Associate level

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-201 Statistical Inference by Simulation
  • DS-204 Machine Learning for Tabular Data: Baselines and Validation
  • Resamples and reshapes time-indexed data in a dataframe

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Associate level.

Description

Participants forecast with the discipline of a backtest. On synthetic daily electricity demand from Shams Power, a fictional distributor with 12 regions and 400 feeders, they decompose series, set a seasonal-naive benchmark floor, fit exponential smoothing and ARIMA-family models with weather and Hijri holidays, train one boosted model across many series, reconcile feeder and regional forecasts and run pretrained zero-shot forecasters as baselines. Every method is scored by rolling-origin cross-validation with point and probabilistic measures and reaches the planner as a fan chart.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Apply decomposition and a seasonal-naive benchmark floor scored by rolling-origin cross-validation.

    Given regional daily demand with weekly and annual seasonality.

    Evidence a backtest table of scaled errors by horizon.

  2. Applied

    Implement exponential smoothing, ARIMA-family and dynamic regression models with temperature and moving-holiday terms.

    Given the regional series and a Hijri and Gregorian calendar.

    Evidence residual diagnostics and scores against the floor.

  3. Applied

    Measure point and quantile accuracy of a global boosted model and a pretrained zero-shot forecaster against statistical models.

    Given 400 feeder series with eight weeks held out.

    Evidence a scorecard of scaled error, pinball loss, coverage, run time and licence terms.

  4. Performance

    Review a forecast evaluation for leakage across time and selection on the test window, and correct it.

    Given a notebook with three seeded faults.

    Evidence a review memo and a corrected backtest.

  5. Performance

    Build a forecasting analysis for new series and defend method, horizon and uncertainty to a planner.

    Given a supplied dataset and a planner's request.

    Evidence a repository and a two-page note with fan charts, defended live.

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Look, frame and set the floor
1.1What is forecast, and whyWrite horizon, error measure and decision for three planner requests
1.2Seeing structurePlot one region at three time scales and mark Ramadan and Eid
1.3DecompositionDecompose regional demand and quantify trend and seasonal strength
1.4The benchmark floorBacktest naive and seasonal-naive forecasts over 28-day horizons
Day 2 Statistical forecasters
2.1Exponential smoothingFit exponential smoothing models and score them against the floor
2.2ARIMA-family modelsFit a seasonal ARIMA model, check residuals and score it
2.3Weather and moving holidaysAdd temperature and Hijri holiday regressors and measure the gain around Eid
2.4Intervals that holdMeasure 80% and 95% interval coverage in the backtest
Day 3 Many series and pretrained models
3.1Many series and hierarchiesFit per-feeder models, reconcile them with regional totals and compare errors at both levels
3.2Global boosted forecastersBacktest one boosted model on lag and calendar features across feeders
3.3Pretrained zero-shot forecastersRun a pretrained forecaster zero-shot and record accuracy, run time and licence
3.4Choosing among familiesFill a scorecard of accuracy, cost and licence per method family
Day 4 Review and communicate
4.1Leakage across timeFind three seeded leaks in a notebook and correct the backtest
4.2New and short seriesForecast a feeder with eight weeks of history using pooled and pretrained methods
4.3Combining forecastsTest whether an average of three methods beats the best single one
4.4Fan charts and forecast notesDraw a fan chart, write a planner's note and choose the capstone dataset

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analysis language and notebooks
Python with Jupyter or marimo, R with Quarto
Statistical forecasting
statsforecast, fable for R, statsmodels
Machine learning forecasting
LightGBM with lag features, sktime, Darts
Pretrained forecaster as a baseline
the Chronos family of open-weight forecasters, TimesFM (non-commercial weights), AutoGluon time series

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on decomposition, error measures and leakage (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: framing and floor 15, methods 25, backtest design 25, uncertainty and communication 25, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and for setting up the first backtest. AI-tutor in labs, explaining output without writing the backtest. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
