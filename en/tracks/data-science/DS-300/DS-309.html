DS-309 Responsible Data Science: Privacy, Fairness and Documentation

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-300 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Associate level

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-204 Machine Learning for Tabular Data: Baselines and Validation
  • DS-207 Responsible Data Practice and Conduct in Analytical Work
  • Trains and evaluates a classifier in Python
  • Computes error rates and confidence intervals for proportions

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Associate level.

Description

Participants take the synthetic applicant data and eligibility-screening model of Yusr Housing Support Fund, a fictional public fund, through the checks a release needs: they measure re-identification risk and de-identify the table, publish differentially private counts under a budget, test a synthetic extract for leakage, audit subgroup errors against the bias types in SDAIA's AI Bias Reference Guide, and write the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs. Calibration and explanation are DS-305.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish pseudonymisation, de-identification, aggregation, synthetic data and differential privacy by the guarantee and cost of each.

    Given five described data releases.

    Evidence a decision table naming the fitting control and the residual risk for each.

  2. Applied

    Measure re-identification risk from quasi-identifiers and reduce it while tracking analytic utility.

    Given 60,000 synthetic applicant records and a linkable external file.

    Evidence unique records, linkage success and three key statistics before and after treatment.

  3. Applied

    Compute differentially private counts and means and state the accuracy cost of a privacy budget.

    Given three candidate budgets.

    Evidence error-against-budget curves and a recommended setting.

  4. Applied

    Test a synthetic extract for analytic utility and membership-inference leakage.

    Given a generator trained on the applicant table.

    Evidence a utility and leakage report with a release recommendation.

  5. Performance

    Audit a screening model for subgroup disparities, choose fairness criteria that fit the decision and evaluate two mitigations.

    Given a scored test set with protected and intersectional attributes.

    Evidence an audit report with disaggregated error rates, intervals and a recommendation.

  6. Performance

    Build the datasheet, model card and impact assessment a reviewer needs to judge and rerun a release.

    Given the participant's capstone release.

    Evidence a pack that a peer reruns and challenges, with a likelihood and impact score for each risk.

Topics

Personal data in analysis

  • Identifiers, quasi-identifiers and sensitive attributes
  • Minimisation, purpose limitation and secondary use
  • Linkage attacks

De-identification and synthetic data

  • k-anonymity and its limits
  • Generalisation, suppression and aggregation
  • When de-identified data are still personal data

Differential privacy basics

  • Privacy budget and composition
  • Mechanisms for counts and means
  • Evaluating a differential privacy claim

Subgroup fairness audit

  • Group metrics and why they conflict
  • Intersectional slices and small-group uncertainty
  • Bias types across the lifecycle
  • Mitigation and its cost

Documentation and impact assessment

  • Datasheets and model cards
  • Personal data impact assessment
  • Likelihood and impact scoring and residual risk

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Find the personal data
1.1Personal data in an analysisTag each applicant field by identifiability and decide what to drop (AI-off)
1.2Classification and permitted useAssign classification levels to three extracts and decide which may leave the fund
1.3Linkage attacksRe-identify applicants by linking a release to a synthetic public register
1.4Measuring disclosure riskCompute unique quasi-identifier combinations and k for three candidate releases
Day 2 Release with less risk
2.1Generalisation and suppressionReach k of at least five and measure the change in three key statistics
2.2Differential privacy for countsRelease district counts at three budgets and plot the error
2.3Budgets and compositionSplit one budget across four queries and justify the allocation
2.4Synthetic data and leakageGenerate a synthetic extract and test it with distance-to-closest-record and membership-inference checks
Day 3 Audit for fairness
3.1Disaggregating errorsCompute error rates by gender, region and nationality with intervals
3.2Criteria that conflictShow two fairness criteria conflicting on the test set and choose one
3.3Bias across the lifecycleMap five findings to bias types in the national guide
3.4Mitigation and its costApply reweighting and a threshold change and compare accuracy and disparity
Day 4 Document and assess
4.1DatasheetsWrite the datasheet for the de-identified release
4.2Model cardsWrite a model card with disaggregated results and intended use
4.3Impact assessmentScore four risks on the national framework's likelihood and impact matrix and propose treatments
4.4Review by someone elseRerun one result from a peer's day 2 or day 3 notebook and record the documentation gaps

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

ARX, sdcMicro
Differential privacy library
OpenDP, diffprivlib
Synthetic tabular data
Synthetic Data Vault, synthpop

Notebooks with pinned environments
Jupyter or marimo with uv, R with Quarto and renv

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check on privacy controls, disclosure risk and fairness metrics (20%)
  • Capstone release pack on a rubric: privacy 25, fairness audit 25, documentation 20, impact assessment 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live review recomputing a disparity the assessor selects (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first lab of day 1. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains methods and errors without producing the audit. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
