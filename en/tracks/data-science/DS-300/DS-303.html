Description

Participants answer causal questions where no experiment was run. For the Rimal Employment Fund, a fictional agency whose wage subsidy reached regions in different years, they write each question as an estimand, draw a causal graph to decide what to adjust for, and estimate effects with matching and weighting, staggered difference-in-differences, synthetic control and regression discontinuity. The synthetic data carry known true effects, and each analysis ends with a sensitivity analysis and a plain list of assumptions.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Draft a causal graph (directed acyclic graph, DAG) and derive a valid adjustment set, naming colliders and bad controls.

    Given three programme questions with candidate variables.

    Evidence adjustment sets matching the reference, each exclusion justified.

  2. Applied

    Apply propensity-score matching and inverse probability weighting with overlap and balance checks.

    Given job-seeker records with a known true training effect.

    Evidence balance tables, overlap plots and estimates set against the truth.

  3. Applied

    Apply difference-in-differences with a leads-and-lags plot and an estimator suited to staggered adoption.

    Given a regional panel with adoption in four years and a growing effect.

    Evidence two-way fixed-effects and staggered estimates with an explanation of the gap.

  4. Applied

    Implement synthetic control and regression discontinuity, each with its standard diagnostic.

    Given a single-adopter region and an eligibility score with a cut-off.

    Evidence placebo tests, bandwidth checks and a manipulation test.

  5. Performance

    Defend an identification strategy for a new causal question against at least two alternatives.

    Given a policy question, a data inventory and access limits.

    Evidence a design memo with estimand, strategy, key assumption and falsification tests.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate an estimate's sensitivity to unmeasured confounding and state how strong a confounder would need to be to overturn it.

    Given an estimate from the participant's own analysis.

    Evidence a sensitivity section with bounds in plain language.

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Questions, estimands and graphs
1.1From a question to an estimandRewrite three requests from the fund as estimands
1.2Causal graphsDraw the graph for the training track and list every path to the outcome
1.3What to adjust forDerive adjustment sets and find the seeded collider and bad control
1.4Why naive comparisons failSimulate confounded data and set naive and adjusted estimates against the truth
Day 2 Selection on observables
2.1Propensity scoresFit a propensity model and plot overlap
2.2MatchingMatch participants to non-participants and report balance
2.3WeightingEstimate the effect with inverse probability weights and trim extremes
2.4Combining outcome and treatment modelsEstimate with augmented weighting and rank three estimates against the truth
Day 3 Panels and timing
3.1Difference-in-differencesEstimate the effect for the first adopters and plot pre-trends
3.2Event studiesRead event-study leads as a check on parallel trends
3.3Staggered adoptionEstimate the rollout with two-way fixed effects and a staggered estimator
3.4Synthetic controlBuild a synthetic control for the single-adopter region and run placebos
Day 4 Thresholds and credibility
4.1Regression discontinuityEstimate sharp and fuzzy effects at the eligibility cut-off with two bandwidths
4.2Checking a discontinuityRun density and covariate balance tests at the cut-off
4.3Sensitivity to hidden confoundingFind the confounder strength that erases a supplied matching estimate
4.4Choosing a designDraft a design memo for a new question and choose the capstone question

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on adjustment sets, event studies and design choice (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: estimand and design 25, estimation and diagnostics 25, sensitivity 20, interpretation 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Panel defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks, graphs and adjustment sets. AI-tutor in estimation labs, explaining without writing the analysis. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
