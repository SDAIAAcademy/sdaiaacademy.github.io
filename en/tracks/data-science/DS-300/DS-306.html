Description

Participants turn text into measurements they can defend. For the Sada Citizen Voice Centre, a fictional agency receiving complaints in Arabic and English, they normalise Arabic script and dialect spellings, redact personal data, build sparse and embedding-based classifiers, classify and extract fields with a prompted open-weight language model, and treat every model-made label as a measurement: agreement with a human gold set, stability across runs and prompts, gaps between dialects and the bias it carries into estimates. Writing labelling guidelines and adjudication are in DS-311.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a bilingual corpus by normalising script, removing near-duplicates and redacting personal data.

    Given 20,000 raw complaints with mixed scripts and embedded phone numbers.

    Evidence a cleaning script and a profile report with the redaction miss rate.

  2. Applied

    Implement sparse, embedding-based and prompted classifiers and score them on one held-out gold set.

    Given a training pool and a fixed budget of model calls.

    Evidence results with intervals by language and cost per thousand documents.

  3. Applied

    Implement schema-constrained field extraction and measure field-level precision and recall.

    Given 500 synthetic inspection reports with reference extractions.

    Evidence an extraction script and a per-field validation report.

  4. Applied

    Compute a district complaint rate from model labels corrected with a human-labelled sample and compare it with the uncorrected rate.

    Given model labels for all complaints and a 300-document human gold set with measured agreement.

    Evidence a table of naive and corrected rates with intervals.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate a language model as an annotator and decide whether its labels can feed a downstream estimate.

    Given labels from two prompts and three runs over the gold set.

    Evidence a reliability memo on agreement, stability, dialect gaps and the decision.

  6. Performance

    Design a text-measurement pipeline for a stakeholder question and defend each measurement choice.

    Given a supplied corpus and a written brief.

    Evidence a repository and report defended live.

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Text into clean data
1.1Text as a data sourceProfile the corpus by language, length, channel and duplicate rate
1.2Preparing Arabic textNormalise letter variants and diacritics and measure the vocabulary change
1.3Near-duplicatesFind near-duplicate complaints with character shingles and decide which to keep
1.4Redacting personal dataMask phone numbers, identity numbers and names and measure misses
Day 2 Labels you can trust
2.1Gold sets and agreementLabel 40 complaints with a partner from the supplied codebook and compute chance-corrected agreement
2.2Sparse baselinesTrain a character n-gram classifier and score it on the gold set
2.3Embeddings as featuresTrain an embedding classifier and score it per language
2.4Comparing classifiers fairlyCompare the two classifiers on the gold set with paired bootstrap intervals by language
Day 3 Language models as instruments
3.1Prompted classificationClassify the gold set with a prompted open-weight model
3.2Ask it twiceRepeat one prompt three times, reword it once and measure label stability
3.3Dialect gapsMeasure agreement on Modern Standard Arabic, Gulf dialect and English subsets
3.4Is the model a good annotatorDecide whether model labels can replace a second human coder
Day 4 From documents to tables
4.1Schema-constrained extractionExtract six fields from inspection reports into a validated schema
4.2Field-level validationCompute per-field precision and recall and list the commonest errors
4.3Structure in textCluster complaint embeddings and label five clusters
4.4Errors downstreamCorrect model-label complaint rates with the human sample and choose the capstone corpus

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on agreement, representation choice and field validation (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: preparation and redaction 25, gold set and agreement 20, models and extraction 20, reliability memo 25, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and human gold labels. AI-tutor for cleaning and classifier code, hinting without solutions. Language models are measured in labs; AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
