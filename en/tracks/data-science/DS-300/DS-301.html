DS-301 Regression and Generalised Linear Modelling

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Stream

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-201 Statistical Inference by Simulation
  • Fits a simple linear regression and reads its coefficient table

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Associate level.

Description

Participants build regression models that answer a stated question and defend what each term means. On synthetic data from Sahil Water, a fictional utility with 30 districts, they fit linear models with splines and interactions, logistic models for late payment and count models for leak reports, diagnose misfit by simulating from the fit, handle grouped data with clustered errors and random intercepts, and penalise many predictors. Results are reported as marginal effects with intervals.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement linear models with interactions and splines and report average marginal effects with intervals.

    Given household consumption with tariff band, household size and temperature.

    Evidence effect plots and an interval table matching the reference.

  2. Applied

    Apply logistic and count models, choosing between Poisson and negative binomial from a dispersion check.

    Given late-payment flags and weekly leak reports with pipe length as exposure.

    Evidence a notebook stating effects on the probability and count scales.

  3. Performance

    Diagnose misfit with residuals, influence measures and simulation from the fitted model, then correct the model.

    Given a model with five seeded problems such as overdispersion and separation.

    Evidence a diagnosis log naming at least four problems, and the corrected model.

  4. Applied

    Analyse grouped data with clustered standard errors and a random-intercept model and explain how the intervals change.

    Given readings nested in 30 districts of very different sizes.

    Evidence a table of naive, clustered and mixed-effects estimates with a short explanation.

  5. Applied

    Apply ridge and lasso with cross-validated tuning and measure the stability of the selected predictors.

    Given 120 correlated candidate predictors.

    Evidence a coefficient path, the chosen penalty and a bootstrap selection-frequency table.

  6. Performance

    Build and defend a regression model for a planner's question, justifying terms, family, uncertainty and whether the use is descriptive, predictive or causal.

    Given a supplied dataset and a written brief.

    Evidence a report and notebook defended live.

Topics

Purpose and set-up

  • Descriptive, predictive and causal uses
  • Estimands before models
  • Fake-data simulation

Linear and generalised linear models

  • Splines and interactions
  • Links and families
  • Logistic, Poisson and negative binomial models
  • Offsets and exposure

Checking models

  • Residuals and influence
  • Simulation-based checks
  • Separation and collinearity

Grouped data and many predictors

  • Clustered errors
  • Random intercepts and partial pooling
  • Ridge and lasso
  • Selection stability and inference after selection

Reporting

  • Predicted values and marginal effects
  • Intervals people can read
  • Stating limits

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Linear models that answer a question
1.1A regression answers a questionLabel five published regression claims descriptive, predictive or causal
1.2Fake data firstSimulate consumption from a known model and recover its coefficients
1.3Transformations and splinesFit log consumption with a temperature spline and plot its band
1.4Interactions and marginal effectsAdd a tariff-by-household-size interaction and report marginal effects
Day 2 Generalised linear models
2.1Links and familiesFit late payment with linear and logistic models and show where the first breaks
2.2Reading a logistic modelTurn coefficients into probability differences for three household profiles
2.3Counts and exposureModel leak reports with a Poisson model and a pipe-length offset
2.4OverdispersionTest dispersion and refit with a negative binomial model
Day 3 Check and repair
3.1Residuals that tell you somethingFind a missing non-linear term from binned-residual plots
3.2Influential observationsFind the three most influential districts and refit without them
3.3Simulation-based checksTest whether the count model reproduces the observed share of zeros
3.4Separation and error structureRepair a separated logistic fit and add heteroskedasticity-consistent errors
Day 4 Grouped data and many predictors
4.1Clustered dataSet naive and district-clustered standard errors side by side and explain the change
4.2Partial poolingFit a random-intercept model and plot how small districts shrink
4.3Penalised regressionTune ridge and lasso on 120 predictors and plot coefficient paths
4.4Reporting a modelDraft a one-page model report and choose the capstone brief

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off entry and exit checks on reading and specifying models (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: specification 20, diagnostics 30, uncertainty 20, interpretation 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and each lab's first fit and interpretation. AI-tutor in lab extensions, hinting without writing model code. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
