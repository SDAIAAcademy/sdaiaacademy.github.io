DS-406 Measuring the Impact of Data and AI Programmes

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-400 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Expert level

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-302 Online Experiments and A/B Testing
  • DS-303 Causal Inference from Observational Data
  • Estimates a difference-in-differences model and reads an event-study plot
  • Runs a power calculation by simulation

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants evaluate three programmes of the fictional Al-Rawdah Municipal Services Authority: a drafting assistant rolled out in phases to 24 permit offices, a citizen service chatbot and a data-sharing platform. They turn claims into outcome measures, design a trial for a rollout still to come, estimate the effect of one that has happened while correcting for the keenest offices adopting first, carry the uncertainty into a value case, and brief a panel playing the executive committee on whether to stop, continue or scale. DS-302 and DS-303 teach the methods; this course applies them to programmes.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare an outcome framework that separates activity, speed, quality and value and links each measure to a programme claim.

    Given the programme charter and the operational data available.

    Evidence a logic model and a measure table with source and owner.

  2. Performance

    Design an evaluation for a programme before rollout, choosing among randomised, stepped-wedge and encouragement designs and setting unit, duration and power.

    Given the constraint that every office receives the tool within a year.

    Evidence a measurement plan with a power simulation, fixed before the capstone data are released.

  3. Performance

    Estimate the effect of a completed rollout while correcting for selection into adoption, and test the result's sensitivity.

    Given a weekly panel of 24 offices in which the keenest adopted first.

    Evidence a notebook with an event-study plot, the naive comparison for contrast, effects by office and citizen group, and a sensitivity table.

  4. Performance

    Build a value case that carries the effect's uncertainty through costs, benefits and break-even.

    Given an effect interval, licence and review costs and adoption rates.

    Evidence a simulated return distribution with the probability of breaking even.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate an impact report from a vendor or internal team and decide whether it supports scaling.

    Given a report with seeded flaws such as a self-selected pilot and a survey-only outcome.

    Evidence a graded review and a scaling decision memo.

  6. Performance

    Present programme impact to leadership with uncertainty in plain words and one clear recommendation.

    Given a panel playing the executive committee.

    Evidence a two-page brief and a ten-minute briefing scored on a rubric.

Topics

What programmes claim

  • Logic models and estimands
  • Activity, speed, quality and value
  • Blind review of sampled work
  • Perceived versus measured gains

Designs before rollout

  • Cluster randomisation and design effects
  • Stepped-wedge and encouragement designs
  • Measurement plans fixed in advance

After the rollout

  • Selection into adoption
  • Staggered rollouts as natural experiments
  • Who gains and who loses
  • Spillover, novelty and fading effects

From estimate to decision

  • Value cases as distributions
  • Programme views for leadership
  • Stop, continue or scale

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 What would show it worked
1.1Claims, logic models and estimandsTurn the programme charter into a logic model with one estimand per claim
1.2Outcomes that matterReplace five activity counts with outcome measures, including rework and error cost
1.3Measuring quality by blind reviewDesign a blind-rating plan for permit decisions and compute rater agreement on 40 rated cases
1.4Perceived versus measured gainsCompare self-reported time savings with logged handling times for the same offices
Day 2 Design before the rollout
2.1Units, clusters and design effectsChoose the randomisation unit and estimate the design effect from the intraclass correlation
2.2Stepped-wedge rolloutsSimulate the power of a six-step stepped-wedge plan across 24 offices
2.3When use cannot be forcedEstimate the chatbot's effect among compliers from a randomised encouragement
2.4The measurement planWrite the primary outcome, analysis, stopping rule, the result that would change the decision and the notice to measured staff
Day 3 After the rollout
3.1Selection into adoptionShow how comparing adopters with non-adopters overstates the effect
3.2The phased rollout as evidenceEstimate the effect with a staggered-adoption estimator, using offices not yet treated as controls, and plot the event study
3.3Who gains and who losesEstimate effects by office size, region and citizen group and check whether the average hides harm to one group
3.4Effects that spill or fadeTest whether the effect decays after eight weeks or spills to neighbouring offices
Day 4 From estimate to decision
4.1Value with uncertaintyPropagate the effect interval through costs and benefits and find the break-even adoption rate
4.2A programme view for leadershipDesign a one-screen view with targets, intervals and a counterfactual line
4.3Reviewing an impact reportMark the seeded flaws in a vendor impact report and grade each claim
4.4Stop, continue or scaleDraft the two-page brief and rehearse a three-minute version with a partner

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

linearmodels, statsmodels, did for R, fixest for R

NumPy, R, a spreadsheet with scenario tables
Programme views and briefs
Quarto, Apache Superset, Metabase

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check of design and inference fundamentals without AI assistance (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: design 25, analysis 25, value case 15, communication 25, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live briefing before a panel playing the executive committee (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the day 1 logic model. AI-tutor in the labs: it explains estimators but does not choose the design. AI-full in the capstone, presented and defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
