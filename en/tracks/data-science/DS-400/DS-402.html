Prerequisites

  • DS-302 Online Experiments and A/B Testing

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants use machine learning to estimate who benefits from a programme and to choose the rule that allocates it. On a fictional employment agency's synthetic coaching pilot and logged caseworker decisions, they estimate average effects by double machine learning, estimate and validate heterogeneous effects, evaluate allocation rules offline and learn a rule under a budget and a fairness constraint. Every rule ends with the trial that would confirm it.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement double machine learning with cross-fitting for an average effect and compare it with regression adjustment.

    Given logged data with 60 confounders and a known effect.

    Evidence an estimate table with intervals and each method's bias.

  2. Performance

    Build conditional average treatment effect (CATE) estimators by three methods and validate them on held-out randomised data.

    Given the randomised coaching pilot.

    Evidence calibration and ranking checks per estimator and a justified choice.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate allocation rules offline with inverse propensity and augmented estimators and diagnose untrustworthy estimates.

    Given logged decisions with recorded propensities and poor overlap in places.

    Evidence a value table with intervals, overlap diagnostics and effective sample sizes.

  4. Performance

    Design an allocation rule under a budget and a fairness constraint and compare it with treat-all, risk targeting and current practice.

    Given unit-level effect estimates and one place per five job seekers.

    Evidence gains and outcomes by region, gender and disability status, with the attributes the rule may use.

  5. Performance

    Review a heterogeneous-effect claim for overfitting, unstable subgroups and reuse of data for discovery and evaluation.

    Given an analysis with seeded faults.

    Evidence a review memo naming each fault and its effect.

  6. Performance

    Design a confirmatory trial for a learned rule, powered for the comparison with current practice.

    Given the capstone rule and its offline value.

    Evidence a protocol with randomisation unit, primary metric, sample size and stopping rule.

Topics

Estimands and double machine learning

  • Estimands, and why risk is not benefit
  • Overlap and positivity
  • Orthogonal scores and cross-fitting
  • Sensitivity to unobserved confounding

Heterogeneous effects and uplift

  • Meta-learners and causal forests
  • Validation on randomised data
  • Uplift and Qini curves

Off-policy evaluation

  • Inverse propensity and augmented estimators
  • Effective sample size

Policy learning and fairness

  • Policy trees under a budget
  • Fairness constraints and their cost
  • Confirmatory trials

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

DoubleML, EconML, CausalML, grf for R
Off-policy evaluation and policy learning
course-provided NumPy estimators, policytree for R
Subgroup fairness metrics
Fairlearn, course-provided pandas checks
Analysis environment
Python through uv with Jupyter or marimo, R with Quarto

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off supervised check: identify an estimand, read an overlap plot, compute an inverse-propensity value by hand (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: identification 15, effects and validation 25, offline evaluation 25, rule and fairness audit 20, trial plan and reproducibility 15 (60%)
  • Live defence of one design choice (20%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the capstone rubric and on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for supervised checks and each day's first estimator. AI-tutor in other labs: hints, no finished code. AI-full in the capstone, with assisted steps in a verification note, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
