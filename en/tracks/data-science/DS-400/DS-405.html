DS-405 Data Preparation at Scale: Contracts, Synthetic Data and Privacy

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-309 Responsible Data Science: Privacy, Fairness and Documentation
  • Writes SQL with window functions and cleans data with a data-frame library
  • Has written a data contract and blocking quality rules for one table (DS-311 or equivalent)

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants run data preparation for the fictional Rimah Health Data Consortium: three hospital groups send encounter, laboratory and pharmacy extracts (about 30 million synthetic rows) for a readmission study, and a university partner wants a copy. They contract and link the producers, rebuild preparation as an incremental pipeline over partitioned columnar tables, generate and attack synthetic versions, compare privacy-enhancing methods for four requests, and write a release decision a privacy officer could sign. Single-table contracts come from DS-311; de-identification and basic differential privacy from DS-309.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design shared data contracts, a pseudonymous linkage key and a breaking-change policy for a feed with several producers.

    Given three producers whose extracts disagree on schema, codes and units.

    Evidence contracts checked against all three producers, a linkage match rate measured against the planted truth, and a one-page policy.

  2. Applied

    Implement an incremental, idempotent preparation pipeline over partitioned columnar tables with quality gates.

    Given 30 million synthetic rows on a laptop.

    Evidence identical output when a day is re-run, with time and memory measured.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate a synthetic dataset for fidelity, analytical utility and disclosure risk.

    Given two generators fitted to related patient and encounter tables.

    Evidence a report with train-on-synthetic, test-on-real scores and attack results.

  4. Applied

    Compute differentially private statistics under a budget shared across repeated releases.

    Given twelve monthly statistics.

    Evidence a budget ledger and a plot of error against budget.

  5. Performance

    Select a privacy-enhancing method for each data request by comparing protection, utility and cost.

    Given four requests, from an internal analyst to an external university.

    Evidence a decision table stating the residual risk of each option.

  6. Performance

    Decide whether to release a dataset and on what conditions, and defend the decision.

    Given the university request and the measured results.

    Evidence a release decision record defended live.

Topics

Contracts across producers

  • Producer and consumer duties
  • Code and unit harmonisation as contract clauses
  • Keyed pseudonyms for record linkage
  • Versions, deprecation and breaking changes

Pipelines for large data

  • Lazy columnar execution and partitions
  • Idempotent, incremental loads
  • Snapshots in open table formats
  • Quality gates and lineage events

Synthetic data

  • What it is for and what it cannot do
  • Relational generators
  • Fidelity and fitness for the analysis
  • Distance and membership-inference tests

Privacy-enhancing methods and release

  • Disclosure risk in longitudinal and linked records
  • Privacy budgets across releases
  • Federated analysis, secure aggregation and clean rooms
  • Lawful basis, secondary use and release conditions

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Contract the producers
1.1Preparation breaks where producers disagreeProfile three producers' encounter extracts and list where schema, codes and units disagree
1.2One contract, three producersWrite a shared contract in an open standard with code mappings, unit rules and freshness, and run it against all three extracts
1.3Linking patients across producersGenerate a keyed-hash linkage identifier at each producer and measure the match rate against the planted truth
1.4Versions and breaking changesDraft the policy (what counts as breaking, notice periods, who approves) and test it on a seeded producer change
Day 2 Prepare at scale
2.1Columnar files and lazy executionRewrite an eager cleaning script as a lazy query over partitioned files and compare time and memory
2.2Incremental and idempotent loadsMake the daily load idempotent and prove it by loading one day twice
2.3Tables with historyRebuild last month's analysis set from a table snapshot
2.4Gates and lineage at scaleRun blocking rules per partition and trace a seeded defect to its producer and partition through lineage events
Day 3 Synthesise and attack
3.1When synthetic data fitsDecide for four requests between synthetic data, a de-identified extract and a query service
3.2Generating related tablesFit two generators to the patient and encounter tables, keeping the parent-child relation
3.3Utility is measured against the analysisTrain the readmission model on synthetic data, test on real data and compare
3.4Privacy is measured by attackRun distance-to-closest-record and membership-inference tests on each synthetic table
Day 4 Protect and decide
4.1Disclosure risk in longitudinal recordsMeasure re-identification risk when encounter sequences act as quasi-identifiers and compare it with the single-table estimate
4.2Privacy budgets over many releasesAllocate a budget across twelve monthly statistics and plot error against budget
4.3Analysis without pooling recordsCompute a cross-site readmission rate with a simulated secure-aggregation protocol
4.4The release decisionDraft the decision for the university request: method, residual risk, conditions, approver

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks of fundamentals without AI assistance (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: contracts and pipeline 25, synthetic data validation 25, privacy method and risk 25, release decision 15, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence of the release decision (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the first contract on day 1. AI-tutor in the labs: it explains errors but does not write generator or attack code. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
