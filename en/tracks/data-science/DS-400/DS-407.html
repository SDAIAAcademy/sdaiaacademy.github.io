DS-407 Leading Analytical Work: Review, Standards and Stakeholders

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

DS-400 Data Science: courses that help prepare for the Expert level

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • DS-310 Problem Framing and Analytical Review
  • Has reviewed an analysis written by someone else and written comments the author acted on
  • Uses version control for analysis code

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA Data Science badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants lead the analytics unit of the fictional Ras Sahil Ports Authority: twelve analysts whose work increasingly starts as an assistant's draft. They write a proportionate quality-assurance standard and review checklist, re-run and review colleagues' analyses, set the unit's rules for AI-assisted work, turn review findings into feedback, and negotiate scope, deadline and evidence with role-played sponsors, one of whom asks for an unfavourable result to be dropped. DS-310 teaches reviewing one analysis; this course sets the standards and runs the system.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design a proportionate quality-assurance standard for analytical work, with assurance levels, a review checklist and sign-off rules.

    Given the unit's log of 30 requests with their uses and risks.

    Evidence a standard with which a peer, working independently, assigns the same assurance levels to six new requests.

  2. Performance

    Review analyses by others, including assistant-drafted ones, rank findings by their effect on the decision and calibrate severity with a second reviewer.

    Given three seeded-flaw analysis repositories.

    Evidence review notes that find the seeded critical flaws, and a severity agreement table with the second reviewer.

  3. Performance

    Design rules for AI-assisted analysis covering verification, disclosure and the process evidence each analyst keeps, and audit the unit's work against them.

    Given the national generative AI guidelines and a log of the unit's assisted analyses.

    Evidence a one-page rule set and an audit of five assisted analyses against it.

  4. Applied

    Prepare feedback and a development plan for an analyst from review findings.

    Given a reviewed notebook and the analyst's skills profile.

    Evidence a feedback note and three-month plan that a peer judges specific.

  5. Performance

    Lead a negotiation with a sponsor on scope, deadline and evidence threshold and record the agreement.

    Given a role-played sponsor whose constraints change during the meeting.

    Evidence an analysis agreement and an observer's rubric score.

  6. Performance

    Decide how to respond to pressure to change or suppress a finding, and record the reasons.

    Given a scripted request to remove an unfavourable subgroup result.

    Evidence a decision record citing the unit's standard and the ethics principles.

Topics

Analytical quality assurance

  • Assurance proportionate to risk and use
  • Analyst, reviewer and approver roles
  • Published guides such as the Aqua Book and the REFORMS checklist
  • Correcting errors found after release

Review practice

  • Question fit, data, method, inference, communication
  • Re-running before reading
  • Severity by effect on the decision
  • Calibrating reviewers

AI-assisted work and developing analysts

  • Verification and disclosure rules
  • Auditing assisted work against the rules
  • Feedback that names the reasoning
  • Keeping unaided practice in the work

Sponsors and evidence

  • Scope, deadline and answerability
  • Agreeing the evidence that would change the decision
  • Pressure on findings and escalation
  • Quality statements in board papers

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Set the standard
1.1Assurance proportionate to riskGrade six requests by risk and complexity and assign an assurance level to each
1.2A team review checklistDraft a checklist from question to conclusion by adapting a published reproducibility checklist
1.3Reproducibility as a team ruleRe-run a colleague's analysis from its repository and record every step that failed
1.4Rules for AI-assisted analysisWrite what analysts must verify, disclose and keep as evidence when an assistant drafts work
Day 2 Review and develop
2.1Calibrating reviewersReview a seeded-flaw berth-delay analysis independently with a partner, compare severity ratings and revise the checklist where you disagree
2.2Auditing assisted workCheck five assistant-drafted analyses from the unit's log against the rules for AI-assisted work and record each breach
2.3Feedback that builds skillTurn review findings into feedback and a three-month plan for a new analyst
2.4Review capacity and mentoringPlan a quarter's review load and pairings for a team of twelve
Day 3 Work with sponsors
3.1Scope and deadlineRole-play a sponsor who wants a result in a week and agree what can be answered
3.2Agreeing the evidence firstWrite an analysis agreement: question, decision rule and evidence threshold
3.3Pressure on a findingHandle a scripted request to drop an unfavourable subgroup and record the decision
3.4Reporting quality and correcting errorsDraft a board-paper quality statement (what was checked, by whom, what remains uncertain) and a correction notice for an error found after release

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Notebooks and reports
Jupyter, marimo, Quarto
Code and document review
pull or merge requests on GitHub or GitLab, comments in a shared document
Reproducible environments
uv with a lock file, containers
Assistant whose output is reviewed
a free hosted open-weight model, the assistant the participant's unit already uses

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: review a short seeded-flaw analysis in 40 minutes (20%)
  • Capstone pack rubric: standard 20, review findings 30, analysis agreement 20, quality statement 20, clarity 10 (50%)
  • Chaired review meeting observed against a rubric (30%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on each component

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and first-pass reviews on day 2, so reviewers form their own judgement first. AI-tutor when drafting standards and feedback. AI-full in the capstone pack, defended while chairing the meeting live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
