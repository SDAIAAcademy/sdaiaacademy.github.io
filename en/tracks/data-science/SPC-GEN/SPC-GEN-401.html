SPC-GEN-401 Robot Learning and Vision-Language-Action Models

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-GEN Generative, Multimodal and Embodied Models

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 10 weeks part-time, one two-hour session and one lab a week, with robot-arm lab days and a team capstone

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-GEN-304 Reinforcement Learning and Decision Making
  • Has trained a diffusion or flow model, as in SPC-GEN-301, or passes an entry exercise
  • Fine-tunes a transformer model with adapters, as in SPC-DLR-306

Description

Participants train and evaluate learned robot policies in simulation and on low-cost open robot arms. They collect teleoperated demonstrations, train behaviour cloning, action-chunking and diffusion policies, fine-tune an open-weight vision-language-action (VLA) model, and measure success with intervals on held-out objects, positions and instructions. They narrow the simulation-to-hardware gap with domain randomisation and system identification. Engineering of deployed robot learning systems is DEV-PHY-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement behaviour cloning with action chunking and a diffusion policy, and compare them on the same demonstrations.

    Given a simulated manipulation task with 50 to 200 demonstrations.

    Evidence success rates over 50 episodes per policy, with intervals.

  2. Performance

    Design a demonstration protocol and diagnose how number, diversity and operator quality of demonstrations affect success.

    Given teleoperation in simulation and on a desktop arm.

    Evidence a data card and an ablation of success against data.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate a fine-tuned open-weight VLA model against a policy trained from scratch on held-out objects, positions and instructions.

    Given a pretrained action model and a fixed GPU budget.

    Evidence a report with generalisation splits, intervals and failures annotated by cause.

  4. Performance

    Diagnose the gap between simulation and a real arm and measure how far randomisation and system identification close it.

    Given one task set up in both.

    Evidence a transfer report with success before and after each technique.

  5. Performance

    Review a published robot learning result for evaluation design and claimed generalisation.

    Given a recent paper with released code or data.

    Evidence a written review proposing a stronger protocol.

  6. Applied

    Configure workspace limits, speed limits and an emergency stop for a learned policy on a physical arm.

    Given the arm's workspace and the policy's failure modes.

    Evidence an approved safety sheet and logs showing the limits enforced.

Topics

Robot data

  • Observation and action spaces, control rate and latency
  • Teleoperation, public datasets and data cards

Imitation learning

  • Behaviour cloning and compounding error
  • Action chunking
  • Diffusion and flow policies for multimodal actions

Vision-language-action models

  • Backbones with action heads or action tokens
  • Pretraining on data from many robot types
  • Adapter fine-tuning, inference speed and action rate

Simulation to reality

  • Where simulators differ from hardware
  • Domain randomisation and system identification

Evaluation and safe operation

  • Success rates, intervals and trial counts
  • Generalisation splits; simulated evaluation as a proxy for hardware
  • Workspace limits, emergency stop and supervision

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Robot learning library and dataset format
LeRobot, robomimic
Physics simulation
MuJoCo, PyBullet, Isaac Lab
Low-cost arms (provided per team)
SO-101 open-source arms with a leader arm, other open desktop arms with teleoperation
Open-weight action models
VLA checkpoints on public model hubs, OpenVLA-family models
Accelerator access (provided)
about 40 GPU hours per participant, a lab workstation per team

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off test (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Team capstone (40%)
  • Individual oral defence (10%)
To pass
65% overall and at least 60% on the capstone; no hardware run before the safety sheet is approved

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the test and the first behaviour cloning implementation. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, defended live; safety settings on hardware are set and checked by people.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
