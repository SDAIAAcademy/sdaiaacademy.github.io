SPC-GEN-303 Speech and Arabic Language Modelling

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-GEN Generative, Multimodal and Embodied Models

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 10 weeks part-time, one two-hour session and one lab a week, weekly graded assignments and a capstone

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-DLR-302 Deep Learning from First Principles
  • Has trained a small transformer language model end to end, as in SPC-DLR-303
  • Reads Arabic script, or pairs with someone who does for error analysis

Description

Participants train and adapt the models behind spoken and written Arabic. They fine-tune a self-supervised speech encoder and an encoder-decoder recogniser on public Arabic speech, report error rates by dialect, and fine-tune a text-to-speech model judged by listening tests. For text, they design an Arabic-aware tokeniser, continue pretraining and align a small open-weight model, and evaluate it against audited Arabic and cultural benchmarks. Applications are DEV-LLM-302 and DEV-MLE-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain how Arabic morphology, optional diacritics, dialects and code-switching affect tokenisation, recognition and evaluation.

    Given annotated examples, in a supervised test.

    Evidence a written analysis scored on a rubric.

  2. Applied

    Fine-tune a speech encoder and an encoder-decoder recogniser for Arabic and report error rates by dialect.

    Given public Arabic speech split by dialect.

    Evidence an error-rate table with bootstrap intervals and an analysis of 50 errors.

  3. Applied

    Fine-tune a pretrained text-to-speech model for Arabic and measure its intelligibility and naturalness.

    Given a licensed single-speaker dataset.

    Evidence samples, a recognition round-trip error rate and a blind listening test.

  4. Performance

    Design an Arabic and English tokeniser and justify its vocabulary by fertility and downstream loss.

    Given a mixed corpus and three existing tokenisers.

    Evidence a report with fertility by dialect and a short pretraining comparison.

  5. Performance

    Build a continued-pretraining and alignment run that improves a small open-weight model in Arabic without losing English ability.

    Given a filtered Arabic corpus, a small preference set and a GPU budget.

    Evidence data cards, logs and before-and-after scores in both languages.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate an Arabic model for knowledge, instruction-following, dialect and cultural appropriateness, and audit the benchmarks used.

    Given public benchmarks and prompts written by the cohort.

    Evidence a report with per-dialect results, a contamination check and invalid items listed.

Topics

Speech recognition

  • Self-supervised speech encoders
  • Connectionist temporal classification and encoder-decoder models
  • Error rates, normalisation and diacritics

Speech synthesis

  • Acoustic models, vocoders, codec tokens and speech language models
  • Listening tests and objective proxies
  • Voice cloning and consent

Tokenisation and multilinguality

  • Tokeniser fertility on Arabic
  • Morphological segmentation
  • Cross-lingual transfer and vocabulary extension

Arabic pretraining and alignment

  • Filtering, deduplication and dialect identification
  • Mixing ratios and forgetting
  • Preference data for cultural norms

Arabic evaluation

  • Knowledge, exam and instruction-following benchmarks
  • Cultural and dialect evaluation sets
  • Contamination and translated-benchmark artefacts

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep learning framework
PyTorch, JAX
Speech toolkits
ESPnet, SpeechBrain
Open-weight speech and language models
HuBERT-family speech encoders, Whisper-family recognisers, small open Arabic-capable language models, with ALLaM 7B as an evaluation case
Tokenisers and Arabic text processing
SentencePiece, CAMeL Tools
Arabic speech data
Mozilla Common Voice, multi-dialect corpora from Arabic speech shared tasks
Arabic text evaluation
ArabicMMLU, Ar-IFEval, Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard tasks
Accelerator access (provided)
about 40 GPU hours per participant, free notebook GPUs for evaluation

How the work is assessed

  • Two supervised AI-off tests (30%)
  • Weekly graded assignments (30%)
  • Capstone (30%)
  • Oral defence (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each test and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the tests and the first error-rate computation. AI-tutor in later assignments. AI-full in the capstone, defended live; native-speaker judgements are never delegated to a model.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
