SPC-GEN-302 Vision and Multimodal Foundation Models

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-GEN Generative, Multimodal and Embodied Models

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 10 weeks part-time, one two-hour session and one lab a week, weekly graded assignments and a capstone

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-DLR-302 Deep Learning from First Principles
  • Has trained an image classifier and uses linear algebra for rotations and projections

Description

Participants pretrain and probe the visual representations that multimodal models are built on. They train a small vision transformer with two of the contrastive, masked-image and self-distillation objectives on public images, then a contrastive image-text model, and assemble a small vision-language model from a frozen encoder, a trained projector and an open-weight language model, followed by instruction tuning. A final block fits neural radiance fields and Gaussian splatting to captured scenes. Application use of vision-language models is DEV-MLE-304; video and 3D generation is SPC-GEN-402.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Compare contrastive, masked and self-distillation objectives and explain the invariances each teaches an encoder.

    Given three pretrained encoders, in a supervised test.

    Evidence a written comparison scored on a rubric.

  2. Applied

    Implement two self-supervised objectives for a small vision transformer and measure them with linear probes and retrieval.

    Given about 100,000 unlabelled public images and a labelled subset.

    Evidence a probe table against a supervised baseline.

  3. Performance

    Build a small vision-language model from a frozen encoder, a trained projector and an open-weight language model, then instruction-tune it.

    Given public caption and visual-question data and a fixed GPU budget.

    Evidence a repository, a model card and held-out scores.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate a vision-language model for hallucinated objects, Arabic and English text reading, and prompt sensitivity.

    Given the participant's model and a larger open-weight model.

    Evidence a report with per-slice intervals and annotated failure cases.

  5. Applied

    Fit a neural radiance field and a Gaussian splatting model to a captured scene and compare quality and speed.

    Given about 100 photographs posed by structure from motion.

    Evidence novel views and a metrics table.

  6. Performance

    Design a pretraining and adaptation plan for a vision encoder in a new domain with few labels.

    Given a satellite or industrial-inspection scenario.

    Evidence a defended plan with objective, data, compute and ablations.

Topics

Self-supervised vision

  • Vision transformers and patch embeddings
  • Contrastive, masked and self-distillation objectives
  • Probing, retrieval, dense-feature and fine-tuning evaluation

Image-text models

  • Contrastive image-text pretraining and zero-shot use
  • Caption noise, filtering and inherited bias

Vision-language models

  • Encoder-projector designs against early-fusion native multimodal models
  • Alignment pretraining and visual instruction tuning
  • Resolution, tiling and visual token budgets

Evaluating multimodal models

  • Benchmarks and their limits
  • Object hallucination and grounding
  • Contamination and prompt sensitivity

Three-dimensional representations

  • Camera models and structure from motion
  • Neural radiance fields and Gaussian splatting
  • Depth and point clouds, in brief

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep learning framework
PyTorch, JAX
Open-weight encoders and small language models
CLIP-family and DINO-family encoders, small open-weight language models
Accelerator access (provided)
about 40 GPU hours per participant, free notebook GPUs for probing labs
Multimodal evaluation
lmms-eval, VLMEvalKit
3D reconstruction
COLMAP, nerfstudio, gsplat

How the work is assessed

  • Two supervised AI-off tests (30%)
  • Weekly graded assignments (30%)
  • Capstone (30%)
  • Oral defence (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each test and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the tests and the first contrastive loss implementation. AI-tutor in later assignments. AI-full in the capstone, with a verification note, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
