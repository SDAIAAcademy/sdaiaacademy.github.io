SPC-GEN-402 World Models, Video and 3D Generation

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Seminar
  • 30 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-GEN Generative, Multimodal and Embodied Models

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Seminar: 8 weekly sessions of paper reading and discussion, a replication sprint and a closing review; rebuilt each term from current conference tutorials and workshops

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-GEN-301 Diffusion and Flow Matching from Scratch
  • Reads current machine learning papers and has trained a model on a GPU
  • Reinforcement learning basics (SPC-GEN-304) and radiance fields (SPC-GEN-302) help in the world-model and 3D sessions

Description

A research seminar on models that generate or simulate the world: video generators, interactive world models that predict what happens next given an action, and generators of 3D objects and scenes. Each week participants read and present papers, place their claims on a shared taxonomy and judge the evidence behind them. In a replication sprint each pair reproduces one small published result, such as a latent dynamics model in a classic control task or a small video prediction model, within a fixed GPU budget.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Describe the main families of video, world-model and 3D generative methods and the evidence each offers for physical consistency.

    Given the term's reading list.

    Evidence a taxonomy map with one entry per paper, reviewed in seminar.

  2. Performance

    Review a paper's claims about controllability, physical consistency and generalisation, and judge whether its evaluation supports them.

    Given assigned papers each week.

    Evidence written reviews in a conference format, scored on a rubric.

  3. Applied

    Measure a pretrained video or world model's consistency under controlled prompts or actions with a small probe set.

    Given open-weight models and a probe template.

    Evidence a probe report with failure categories and examples.

  4. Performance

    Reproduce one small published result and report where the reproduction agrees and differs.

    Given a paper with released code or enough detail, a partner and a fixed GPU budget.

    Evidence a repository, run logs and a report against a reproducibility checklist.

  5. Performance

    Defend a position on an open problem, such as how to evaluate interactive world models.

    Given the seminar readings and the replication results.

    Evidence a 15-minute presentation and a two-page position note.

Topics

Video generation

  • Video tokenisers, latent video diffusion and autoregressive generation
  • Temporal consistency and long-horizon generation
  • Measuring video quality and physical plausibility

World models for agents

  • Latent dynamics for planning and control
  • Interactive, action-conditioned video world models
  • World models as training environments for agents and robots

3D and 4D generation

  • Objects and scenes as radiance fields, Gaussians or meshes
  • Lifting 2D generators into 3D
  • Multi-view consistency and its measurement

Evidence and responsibility

  • Benchmarks of physical understanding and their limits
  • Compute, provenance and licences of training video
  • Synthetic media labelling and deepfake risk

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep learning framework
PyTorch, JAX
Open-weight video and 3D generators
models released with weights on public model hubs, reference implementations from paper repositories
Environments for world-model replication
Gymnasium, MuJoCo
Accelerator access (provided)
about 20 GPU hours per pair, free notebook GPUs for small probes

How the work is assessed

  • Weekly written reviews (30%)
  • Reproduction repository and report (40%)
  • Position presentation and note (20%)
  • Seminar contribution scored on a rubric (10%)
To pass
60% overall and a reproduction report that meets the checklist

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the in-session review and for first readings of assigned papers. AI-tutor for background reading. AI-full in the replication sprint, with prompts and agent transcripts kept as process evidence and the result defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
