SPC-GEN-304 Reinforcement Learning and Decision Making

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Extended
  • 80 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-GEN Generative, Multimodal and Embodied Models

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Extended: 12 weeks part-time, one two-hour session and one lab a week, graded assignments and a capstone

Total hours

80 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-DLR-302 Deep Learning from First Principles
  • Uses probability, expectations and gradient-based optimisation, at the depth of SPC-DLR-301

Description

Participants implement the main families of reinforcement learning from scratch and report results that hold across random seeds. They solve small Markov decision processes (MDPs) exactly, then build value-based, policy-gradient and actor-critic agents, a model-based agent that plans with a learned dynamics model, and an offline agent trained on logged data. They compare exploration strategies and close by setting reinforcement learning against optimisation and heuristic baselines on operations problems. Reinforcement learning for language models is SPC-DLR-401, robot learning SPC-GEN-401 and optimisation methods SPC-MOD-304.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the Bellman equations, the policy gradient theorem and the bias-variance trade-off in return estimates.

    Given derivation items in a supervised test.

    Evidence a scored test with worked derivations.

  2. Applied

    Implement value iteration, bandit exploration strategies, Q-learning and a deep Q-network with replay and target networks.

    Given bandit problems, classic control and small arcade environments.

    Evidence regret curves and learning curves over five seeds that reach a stated return.

  3. Applied

    Implement a policy-gradient agent, proximal policy optimisation and an off-policy actor-critic for continuous control.

    Given simulated locomotion tasks.

    Evidence runs that match reference curves within their reported intervals.

  4. Performance

    Build a model-based agent that plans with a learned dynamics model, and diagnose where model error limits it.

    Given a tenth of the model-free sample budget.

    Evidence a comparison of sample efficiency and return with a model-error analysis.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate offline methods against behaviour cloning on datasets of different quality and recommend one per regime.

    Given public logged datasets from expert, medium and random policies.

    Evidence a results table with intervals and a recommendation.

  6. Performance

    Design a reinforcement learning experiment for a decision problem, with the reward, optimisation baselines and a sound comparison over seeds.

    Given a simulated inventory, battery-dispatch or traffic-signal scenario.

    Evidence a report with interquartile means and intervals over at least ten seeds, and a decision memo.

Topics

Foundations

  • MDPs, value functions and dynamic programming
  • Bandits, regret and exploration

Value-based methods

  • Temporal-difference learning and Q-learning
  • Deep Q-networks and overestimation

Policy and actor-critic methods

  • Policy gradients, baselines and advantage estimation
  • Trust regions and proximal policy optimisation
  • Off-policy actor-critic with entropy regularisation

Model-based and offline learning

  • Learned dynamics, model predictive control and tree search
  • Behaviour cloning, distribution shift and conservative value methods
  • Off-policy evaluation, in brief (full treatment in SPC-EXP-401)

Experiments and applications

  • Seeds, interquartile means and bootstrap intervals
  • Reward design, reward hacking and constraints
  • Comparison with optimisation and heuristics
  • Multi-agent settings, in brief

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep learning framework
PyTorch, JAX
Environments and physics simulation
Gymnasium, MuJoCo, PettingZoo
Reference implementations
CleanRL, Stable-Baselines3
Offline datasets
Minari, datasets released with offline-learning papers
Multi-seed statistics
rliable, SciPy bootstrap routines
Compute (provided where needed)
laptop CPU for early labs, about 30 GPU hours per participant for continuous control

How the work is assessed

  • Two supervised AI-off tests of derivations (30%)
  • Graded assignments (30%)
  • Capstone (30%)
  • Oral defence (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each test and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the tests and the first Q-learning and policy-gradient implementations. AI-tutor in later assignments. AI-full in the capstone, with a verification note, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
