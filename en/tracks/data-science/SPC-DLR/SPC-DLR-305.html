SPC-DLR-305 Data and Scaling for Foundation Models

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 40 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-DLR Deep Learning and Model Development

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

40 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-DLR-303 Building a Language Model from Scratch
  • Processes multi-gigabyte files with a dataframe or SQL engine
  • Fits and reads a regression on log-log axes

Description

Participants turn raw web crawl archives into pretraining data and decide how to spend a compute budget on it. They extract text, identify languages including Arabic varieties, filter for quality, remove personal data and near-duplicates, record licences and provenance, and set a mixture that may include filtered synthetic data. Each filtering decision is tested by training small models on the data it produces. Finally they fit scaling laws from small runs to choose a model size and token count for a stated budget.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a text pipeline from raw web archives covering extraction, language identification, quality filters and personal-data removal.

    Given public crawl archive samples in Arabic and English.

    Evidence stage-by-stage counts and a hand-checked sample of removed text.

  2. Applied

    Implement near-duplicate detection with MinHash and decontaminate the corpus against evaluation sets.

    Given the filtered corpus and three public benchmarks.

    Evidence duplicate and overlap rates with precision measured on hand-checked pairs.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate a filter, synthetic dataset or mixture by training small models on its output at equal compute.

    Given two candidate datasets and a fixed budget.

    Evidence a comparison across seeds with intervals and a decision.

  4. Performance

    Build a scaling-law fit from small runs and use it to decide model size and token count for a budget.

    Given a provided grid of runs plus the participant's own.

    Evidence a fit with uncertainty, an extrapolation check on a larger held-out run and a recommendation.

  5. Applied

    Draft a data card recording sources, licences, opt-out handling, filters and known gaps.

    Given the capstone dataset.

    Evidence a card that passes a checklist review.

Topics

Sources and rights

  • Web crawls, books, code and open government data
  • Licences and opt-out signals
  • Personal data in web text

Cleaning and filtering

  • Text extraction and language identification for Arabic varieties
  • Rule-based and classifier quality filters
  • What filters remove from dialect and minority text

Deduplication and decontamination

  • Exact and near-duplicate detection
  • MinHash and locality-sensitive hashing
  • Removing benchmark overlap

Mixtures and synthetic data

  • Domain weights and multilingual balance
  • Generating and filtering synthetic data
  • Degradation from training on model output

Scaling laws and budgets

  • Compute-optimal allocation
  • Checking extrapolation
  • Data-constrained regimes and repetition

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Web archive reading and text extraction
warcio, trafilatura
Language identification
GlotLID, fastText language identification models
Large-file processing
DuckDB, Polars
Near-duplicate detection
datasketch, a MinHash implementation written in class
Small-model training and curve fitting
the participant's model from SPC-DLR-303, SciPy
Compute
Academy GPU hours with a per-participant budget, a laptop CPU for pipeline work and debugging

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance on scaling arithmetic and deduplication (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone recipe and review (50%)
To pass
60% on the supervised check, 60% on the capstone and 70% overall

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, with every filtering decision backed by an ablation the participant defends.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
