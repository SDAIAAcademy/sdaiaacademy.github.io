SPC-ANL-302 Geospatial Analytics

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data Science

Stream

SPC-ANL Analysis and Business Intelligence

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days with labs, guided pre-work and a short defended project

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • SPC-ANL-201 Exploratory Analysis and Visualisation
  • Writes dataframe or SQL analyses with joins and grouping

Description

Participants answer location questions with open vector data on a laptop. They correct coordinate reference systems, geocode addresses, use spatial joins and network distance to measure access to services, map rates rather than counts, and test whether a cluster is stronger than chance. The workshop ends with a service-access analysis on open geodata, defended live. Imagery and learned models are DEV-MLE-305.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain how projections and the modifiable areal unit problem can change a spatial result.

    Given three worked examples containing errors.

    Evidence a written diagnosis and correction of each.

  2. Applied

    Apply spatial joins, buffers and distance measures to compute access to services.

    Given open boundaries, facilities and a population grid.

    Evidence a notebook with checked counts and distances.

  3. Applied

    Prepare maps with normalised measures, suitable class breaks and colour schemes.

    Given counts by district and population data.

    Evidence two maps with a design note.

  4. Performance

    Diagnose spatial clustering with global and local autocorrelation and judge whether a hot spot is meaningful.

    Given synthetic incidents with a known background population.

    Evidence a report with permutation-based significance adjusted for multiple local tests, and an interpretive caution.

  5. Performance

    Deliver a service-access analysis with stated assumptions and limits.

    Given open geodata for one city.

    Evidence a notebook, two maps and a ten-minute defence.

Topics

Spatial data

  • Reference systems and projections
  • GeoJSON, GeoPackage and GeoParquet
  • Geocoding addresses and match errors
  • Open geodata and licences

Spatial operations

  • Joins, overlays and buffers
  • Network distance and travel-time areas
  • Hexagonal and square grids

Map design

  • Rates by population or area
  • Unstable rates in small areas
  • Class breaks, colour and Arabic labels

Spatial statistics and privacy

  • Global and local autocorrelation
  • Modifiable areal unit and edge effects
  • Re-identification and minimum-count aggregation

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Spatial dataframes and geometry
GeoPandas, DuckDB spatial extension, PostGIS
Map inspection
QGIS, Folium
Spatial statistics and networks
PySAL, spdep, OSMnx
Open geodata
OpenStreetMap, official boundaries from open-data portals, open population grids

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI on projections and map reading (20%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone and defence (45%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, with a verification note on assisted spatial operations.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
