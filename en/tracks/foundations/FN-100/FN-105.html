FN-105 How Machine Learning and Language Models Work

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FN-100 Shared Foundations: the entry courses for both badge tracks

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-101 Python for Data and AI Work
  • FN-103 Mathematics for Machine Learning: Vectors, Gradients and Probability
  • Loads a table into a dataframe and computes a proportion

Description

Participants find out how models are made and where they fail by doing the work in small steps. The case is the after-sales service of a fictional home-appliance retailer. They fit a nearest-neighbour rule and a line by hand, then train and validate a classifier that predicts which repair tickets need a second visit, overfit it on purpose and find planted leakage. They then count tokens in Arabic and English, sample from a small open-weight model, test recall across a long warranty document, and check a published model claim against its evidence.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain how a supervised model fits labelled examples by reducing a loss, and why its score on training data overstates its score on new data.

    Given a nearest-neighbour rule and a line fitted by hand to twelve tickets.

    Evidence a worked sheet and a two-sentence explanation checked against a rubric.

  2. Applied

    Use a train, validation and test split and cross-validation to compare a baseline with two models.

    Given 5,000 synthetic repair tickets.

    Evidence a results table with the baseline, the spread over folds and the chosen model.

  3. Applied

    Analyse a pipeline for target, duplicate and temporal leakage and measure the score before and after each fix.

    Given a working pipeline with three planted leaks.

    Evidence a leakage note naming each leak and its effect on the score.

  4. Applied

    Compute precision, recall and the cost of errors at several thresholds and choose one for a stated cost.

    Given validation predictions and the service's cost of a missed second visit.

    Evidence a threshold table and the chosen threshold with its expected cost.

  5. Awareness

    Describe how a language model splits text into tokens, predicts and samples the next token, and uses its context window.

    Given logged runs of a small open-weight model on Arabic and English text.

    Evidence a behaviour sheet in which every claim cites a logged run.

  6. Awareness

    Identify the limits of a model claim, including fabrication, knowledge cut-off, benchmark contamination and partial versus complete credit.

    Given a published model card and a launch claim.

    Evidence a one-page evidence brief stating what the number supports and what it does not.

Topics

Learning from examples

  • Features, labels and loss
  • Nearest neighbours and linear models by hand
  • Trees and small neural networks in brief

Generalisation and evaluation

  • Train, validation and test sets
  • Overfitting and learning curves
  • Cross-validation
  • Confusion matrix, precision, recall and thresholds

Leakage and data

  • Target, duplicate and temporal leakage
  • Pipelines fitted on training data only
  • Distribution shift and subgroup error

How language models work

  • Tokens and Arabic script
  • Next-token probabilities and sampling
  • Pretraining and post-training
  • Context windows, tools and retrieval in brief

Limits and evidence

  • Fabrication and sycophancy
  • Knowledge cut-off and model cards
  • Benchmarks, contamination and harness effects
  • Partial credit against completed tasks

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Models from examples
1.1Learning by handClassify twelve tickets with a nearest-neighbour rule and fit a line on paper, then reproduce both in code
1.2Training and testingTrain a classifier on the tickets and compare its training and test scores with a majority baseline
1.3Overfitting on purposeRaise tree depth step by step and plot training and validation curves
1.4Thresholds and the cost of errorsChoose a threshold for second-visit predictions from the service's cost table
Day 2 Evaluation and leakage
2.1Cross-validationCompare three models over five folds and report the spread
2.2Finding leakageWith AI off, find three planted leaks and record the score before and after each fix
2.3Error by subgroupCompare error rates by appliance type and region and note one possible cause
2.4Tokens and next-token predictionCount tokens on parallel Arabic and English texts and read next-token probabilities from a small open-weight model
Day 3 Language models and their limits
3.1Sampling and variabilitySample one prompt twenty times at three temperatures and measure how often the answers agree
3.2Context and recallPlace one warranty fact at five positions in a long document and test recall
3.3Fabrication and groundingAsk twenty warranty questions with and without the policy in context and count unsupported answers
3.4Reading AI evidenceRecompute a published agent result from its per-task logs as partial credit and as completed tasks, and draft the evidence brief

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check: scenario items on splits, leakage, metrics, tokens and model limits (30%)
  • Capstone repository: modelling and validation 30, leakage and threshold 25, language model behaviour sheet 25, honesty of claims 20 (50%)
  • Defence of one result (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the AI-off check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the hand-fitting lab, the leakage hunt and the supervised check. AI-tutor in the other labs, where language models are objects of measurement, not sources of answers. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
