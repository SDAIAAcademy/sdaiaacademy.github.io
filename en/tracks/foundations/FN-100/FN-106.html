FN-106 Responsible Data and AI Practice in the Kingdom

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FN-100 Shared Foundations: the entry courses for both badge tracks

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-101 Python for Data and AI Work
  • FN-105 How Machine Learning and Language Models Work
  • Reads a short Python program that calls a web API

Description

Participants make one small build lawful and responsible: a parent-enquiry assistant for a fictional private school group that drafts replies from attendance and fee records with a language model. They map its personal data and minimise it under the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), classify each data set under the National Data Management Office (NDMO) policy, decide what may reach an external model, turn the SDAIA AI Ethics Principles into tests, assess risk with the National AI Risk Management Framework, and write the disclosure and records the build needs.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the PDPL obligations that govern a build, from lawful basis and minimisation to rights, retention, transfer and breach handling.

    Given the assistant's data flow diagram.

    Evidence an annotated diagram naming the obligation at each step, checked against the reference.

  2. Awareness

    Distinguish what the data classification, data sharing and open data policies and the SDAIA Generative AI Guidelines each decide.

    Given six questions about the build.

    Evidence a decision table naming the governing instrument, matching the reference on at least five of six.

  3. Applied

    Classify each data set of the build under the four NDMO classification levels and derive the rule for sending it to an external model service.

    Given the school group's data inventory and the policy's impact categories.

    Evidence a classification register with level, reason and permitted destinations per data set.

  4. Applied

    Apply minimisation and pseudonymisation to the data the assistant sends, and test whether records can be re-identified.

    Given synthetic student and guardian records.

    Evidence a before-and-after field list, a pseudonymisation script and a re-identification test result.

  5. Applied

    Prepare a risk assessment that places the assistant in an ethics risk tier and works through the four phases of the national risk framework.

    Given the build description and five candidate harms.

    Evidence a risk register with tier, likelihood and impact ratings, an owner, a treatment and a review date per risk.

  6. Applied

    Test the assistant against the ethics principles for fairness across Arabic and English enquiries, transparency and human review, and draft the disclosure parents see.

    Given forty scripted enquiries.

    Evidence a test report with results per check, the changes made and the disclosure text.

Topics

Personal data in a build

  • Personal data in prompts, logs and stores
  • Lawful basis, purpose and minimisation
  • Rights, retention, transfer and breaches
  • Records of processing activities

Classification and sharing

  • Four levels and impact assessment
  • Handling rules for external services
  • Sharing with another entity
  • What may be published as open data

Ethics principles as tests

  • The seven SDAIA AI Ethics Principles
  • Risk tiers from little or no risk to unacceptable
  • Fairness across languages and groups, with the AI Bias Reference Guide
  • Disclosure and human review

Generative AI and risk

  • Generative AI guidelines for government and public use
  • The four phases of the national risk framework
  • Likelihood, impact and treatment
  • Licences of models and data

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Personal data and the law
1.1Where personal data flows in a buildMap every field the assistant reads, sends, logs and stores on a data flow diagram
1.2Lawful basis and minimisationSet a lawful basis and purpose for each flow and remove the fields the purpose does not need
1.3Pseudonymisation and its limitsPseudonymise the guardian and student records and attempt to re-identify them by linkage
1.4Rights, retention and breachesWrite a retention rule per store and walk a seeded breach through the PDPL breach guideline
Day 2 Classify, share and send
2.1Data classification levelsClassify the six data sets with the policy's impact categories and record the reasons
2.2What may reach an external modelDecide for each data set whether it may go to a hosted model, a locally served model or neither
2.3Sharing with another entityAssess a partner entity's request for attendance data against the Data Sharing Policy and draft the conditions
2.4Licences and provenanceCheck the licences of two candidate open-weight models and one dataset and record the use restrictions
Day 3 Ethics and risk in practice
3.1Principles into testsWrite three checks that turn fairness, transparency and accountability into pass or fail results
3.2Testing the assistantRun forty scripted enquiries in Arabic and English, compare reply quality and escalation rates, and name the bias types from the national guide that explain any gap
3.3Risk tier and risk registerPlace the assistant in a risk tier and rate five harms by likelihood and impact
3.4Disclosure and human reviewDraft the notice parents see and the rule that sends sensitive replies to staff

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Language model for the build
a small open-weight model served locally, a free hosted endpoint used only with synthetic data
Personal data detection
Presidio, regular-expression rules
De-identification and linkage testing
ARX, dataframe joins on quasi-identifiers
Licence references
the SPDX licence list, model and dataset cards
Diagramming
draw.io, Mermaid

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off scenario check on the national instruments (30%)
  • Capstone build pack on a five-criterion rubric: data protection 25, classification and sharing 20, risk assessment 20, ethics tests 20, disclosure and records 15 (50%)
  • Ten-minute review in which the assessor changes one fact of the case (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the scenario check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check and the first classification of each data set. AI-tutor for reading the instruments, with every answer checked against the official text. AI-full for drafting the build pack, provided no real personal data enters any assistant.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
