FN-103 Mathematics for Machine Learning: Vectors, Gradients and Probability

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FN-100 Shared Foundations: the entry courses for both badge tracks

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-101 Python for Data and AI Work
  • Solves a linear equation and reads a function graph at secondary-school level

Description

Participants learn the minimum mathematics both badges assume and compute every idea twice: by hand on a small case and in array code. The case is a fictional desalination plant with synthetic pump and sensor readings. Vectors and matrices describe the readings and their similarity; least squares and principal components find structure; derivatives, the chain rule and gradient descent fit an energy curve; conditional probability, distributions and seeded simulation explain alarm rates and the uncertainty of an estimate. The capstone ties the three strands into one short analysis.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain dot products, norms, matrix products and rank as operations on data, and predict the shape of each result.

    Given six small hand problems and their array-code versions.

    Evidence worked answers and shapes, at least five of six correct, confirmed by running the code.

  2. Applied

    Compute cosine similarity, a least-squares fit and principal components with array code, checking each against a hand calculation.

    Given synthetic readings from 40 pumps.

    Evidence a notebook in which every computed result agrees with its hand check within a stated tolerance.

  3. Awareness

    Describe derivatives, gradients and the chain rule on a computational graph, and trace one backward pass by hand.

    Given a two-parameter model of energy use.

    Evidence a hand-drawn graph with each partial derivative, matching a finite-difference check.

  4. Applied

    Implement gradient descent for a small model and identify step-size problems from the loss curve.

    Given the plant's energy and flow data.

    Evidence loss curves for three step sizes with a short reading of each.

  5. Applied

    Compute the probability that an alarm signals a real fault with Bayes rule, and confirm it by seeded simulation.

    Given the alarm's stated detection rate, false-alarm rate and fault rate.

    Evidence a hand calculation and a simulation that agree within the simulation's error.

  6. Applied

    Use simulation to estimate an expectation and its Monte Carlo error, and show how the error shrinks with sample size.

    Given a model of daily pump failures.

    Evidence estimates with error bars at three sample sizes beside the analytic value.

Topics

Vectors and matrices as data

  • Dot products, norms and cosine similarity
  • Matrix products as transformations
  • Shapes, broadcasting and vectorised code
  • Rank and linear independence

Structure in data

  • Projection and least squares
  • Covariance and principal component analysis (PCA)
  • Eigenvectors and singular values in brief
  • Floating-point limits and conditioning

Change and optimisation

  • Derivatives and partial derivatives
  • Gradients and the chain rule on a graph
  • Automatic differentiation as the chain rule in code
  • Gradient descent and step size
  • Convexity, local minima and penalties

Probability

  • Conditional probability and Bayes rule
  • Binomial, Poisson, normal and categorical distributions
  • Expectation, variance and covariance
  • Likelihood and cross-entropy as training losses

Simulation

  • Seeds and pseudo-random generators
  • Monte Carlo estimates and their error
  • Law of large numbers and the central limit theorem

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Data as vectors
1.1Vectors and similarityCompute cosine similarity between three pump profiles by hand, then for all 40 in array code
1.2Matrices as transformationsApply scaling and rotation matrices to readings, predicting each output shape before running
1.3Loops versus vectorised codeTime a loop and a vectorised matrix product and count the floating-point operations of each
1.4Rank and redundant sensorsFind the two sensors whose readings are linear combinations of others
Day 2 Structure and fit
2.1Projection and least squaresFit energy against flow by the normal equations and check that the residuals are orthogonal to the inputs
2.2When least squares misbehavesRefit with two nearly collinear sensors and record how far the coefficients swing
2.3Covariance and principal componentsReduce eight sensor channels to two components and plot the pumps
2.4Language models use the same algebraRank short Arabic and English maintenance notes against a query with supplied embeddings
Day 3 Gradients
3.1Derivatives as local slopesCompare analytic and finite-difference derivatives across five step sizes
3.2The chain rule on a graphBackpropagate by hand through a two-parameter model and check the result with automatic differentiation
3.3Gradient descentFit the energy curve with three learning rates and read each loss curve
3.4Penalties and their effectAdd a penalty on coefficient size and measure its effect on fit and coefficients
Day 4 Probability and simulation
4.1Conditional probability and base ratesCompute the chance that an alarm means a real fault, by hand and by simulation
4.2Distributions for counts and measurementsMatch failure counts and sensor noise to candidate distributions and check by simulation
4.3Expectation and Monte Carlo errorEstimate expected monthly downtime with error bars at three sample sizes
4.4Probability inside modelsCompute cross-entropy for three sets of predicted probabilities and sample tokens at two temperatures from a given distribution

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off paper check: hand computations across the three strands (40%)
  • Capstone notebook: correctness 35, hand checks 20, interpretation 25, reproducibility 20 (40%)
  • Oral rework of one step with new numbers (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the paper check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for every hand calculation, which comes first in each lab, and for the paper check. AI-tutor for the array-code half of each lab, with hints tied to the instructor's worked solution. AI-full for the capstone code, but not in the oral.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
