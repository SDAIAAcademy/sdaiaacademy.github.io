FND-AIC-103 Reading AI Evidence: Benchmarks, Claims and Statistics

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Workshop
  • 8 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FND-AIC AI Concepts and Responsible Practice

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Workshop: one day with labs and short guided pre-reading

Total hours

8 h

Prerequisites

  • Reads percentages and chart axes and writes a spreadsheet formula; no programming is required

Description

Participants read the numbers behind AI decisions: benchmark scores, leaderboard ranks, launch claims, adoption figures and model cards. They rescore an agent run as partial credit and as finished tasks, test whether a reported gain exceeds its noise, list what changed between two runs of one benchmark, and reconcile adoption figures that range from about 20% to 90%. The day ends with an evidence brief.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain what a benchmark measures and name threats to its validity, such as contamination, saturation and invalid items.

    Given three public benchmarks and their audit notes.

    Evidence a validity note per benchmark naming two threats.

  2. Awareness

    Identify why two reported scores are not comparable: partial credit, attempts, harness, token budget or grader.

    Given two published results on one agent benchmark.

    Evidence a completed comparability checklist.

  3. Applied

    Compute an interval for a reported accuracy difference and decide whether it exceeds sampling noise.

    Given per-item scores for two models and a calculation template.

    Evidence the interval with a one-line verdict.

  4. Awareness

    Explain how adoption and productivity figures differ by denominator, time window and self-reported or measured effect.

    Given three adoption statistics and one controlled study.

    Evidence a reconciliation stating what each figure counts.

  5. Applied

    Draft an evidence brief that separates what a published AI claim shows from what it asserts, using its model card.

    Given a claim assigned on the day.

    Evidence a one-page brief naming the test that would settle the claim.

Topics

Benchmarks

  • Construct, items and scoring
  • Contamination, saturation and audits
  • Leaderboards built on pairwise votes

Comparing results

  • Partial credit against completed tasks
  • 50% and 80% reliability time horizons
  • Same model, different harness
  • Cost per completed task
  • Intervals and paired comparisons

Claims about use and value

  • Denominators and time windows
  • Self-report against randomised trials
  • What model and system cards omit

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Calculation
a spreadsheet such as LibreOffice Calc, Python with SciPy
Public evidence sources, read critically
the AI Index report, Epoch AI benchmark data, METR time-horizon measurements

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off scenario check on eight claims (40%)
  • Evidence brief (60%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 50% on the scenario check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check and the first two labs. AI-tutor in the other labs. AI-full for finding sources for the brief, with every number traced to its original.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
