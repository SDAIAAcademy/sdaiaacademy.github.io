FND-MTH-102 Calculus and Optimisation for Machine Learning

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Extended
  • 45 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FND-MTH Mathematics and Statistics

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Extended: 8 weeks part-time, one session and one lab a week, weekly graded problem sets and a capstone

Total hours

45 h

Prerequisites

  • FND-MTH-101 Linear Algebra for Machine Learning
  • School calculus: derivatives of polynomials and exponentials, or the pre-work refresher
  • Writes short Python programs with functions and loops (FND-PRG-101 or equivalent)

Description

Participants work through the calculus that trains models: derivatives as local linear approximations, gradients, Jacobians and the chain rule on computational graphs. They backpropagate by hand, then write a small reverse-mode automatic differentiation engine and check it against a framework. They run batch, stochastic and momentum gradient descent, read loss curves, and handle convexity and constraints. The capstone trains a small network with their own engine; tensor-level differentiation and modern optimisers follow in SPC-DLR-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain derivatives, partial derivatives, gradients and Jacobians as local linear approximations.

    Given functions of one and several variables.

    Evidence supervised AI-off test, at least 70% correct.

  2. Awareness

    Distinguish convex from non-convex objectives and describe how saddle points and step size shape gradient descent.

    Given six loss surfaces with optimisation traces.

    Evidence annotated plots marked against a reference.

  3. Awareness

    Explain how a constraint moves the optimum of a small problem, using the Lagrangian and the meaning of its multiplier.

    Given three problems, including a budget allocation.

    Evidence written solutions marked against a rubric, with each multiplier read as the price of the constraint.

  4. Applied

    Compute gradients of a composite function by applying the chain rule backwards through a computational graph.

    Given a two-example logistic-regression loss.

    Evidence a hand-worked backward pass matching a numerical gradient check.

  5. Applied

    Implement reverse-mode automatic differentiation for scalar operations and verify it with finite-difference checks.

    Given a starter interface and test suite.

    Evidence an engine that passes the tests and agrees with a framework's gradients.

  6. Applied

    Apply batch, stochastic and momentum gradient descent and diagnose divergence or slow convergence from loss curves.

    Given losses on public data.

    Evidence an experiment log with learning-rate sweeps and a diagnosis of each failed run.

Topics

Change and approximation

  • Derivatives and the tangent-line approximation
  • Partial derivatives, gradients and Jacobians
  • Taylor expansions and finite differences

The chain rule on graphs

  • Computational graphs
  • Forward and reverse mode
  • Backpropagation by hand
  • The chain rule with vectors and matrices

Gradient-based optimisation

  • Gradient descent and step size
  • Stochastic and mini-batch gradients
  • Momentum and adaptive step methods
  • Learning-rate warm-up and decay
  • Reading loss curves

Shape and constraints

  • Convexity, local minima and saddle points
  • Curvature, the Hessian and the largest stable step
  • Numerically stable softmax and log-sum-exp
  • Regularisation as a penalty
  • Lagrange multipliers and projected gradients

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Array computing
NumPy, JAX
Symbolic algebra
SymPy, Maxima
Reference automatic differentiation
PyTorch autograd, JAX
Notebook environment
JupyterLab, marimo, a free hosted notebook service
Hint-only tutor
an open-weight model in tutor mode, an approved assistant with a tutor instruction

How the work is assessed

  • Weekly problem sets, auto-checked (20%)
  • Two supervised AI-off tests, on paper and at the keyboard (40%)
  • Capstone engine and report, rubric-scored (30%)
  • Oral check (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each supervised test

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for first attempts at problem sets, the hand backpropagation lab and both tests. AI-tutor in the other labs. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended orally.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
