FND-MTH-105 Discrete Mathematics and Logic for Computing

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Extended
  • 40 h
  • Audience: beginner

Stream

FND-MTH Mathematics and Statistics

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Extended: 7 weeks part-time, one session and one lab a week, weekly graded problem sets and a capstone portfolio

Total hours

40 h

Prerequisites

  • School algebra
  • Writes short Python programs with lists, loops, functions and recursion (FND-PRG-101 or equivalent)

Description

Participants practise the discrete mathematics and proof that computing degrees assume: sets, relations, logic, proof, counting and graphs. Code tests each claim on generated cases before it is proved, so the gap between testing and proof is visible. Labs link relations to joins, logic to rule checking, counting to collision and re-identification risk, and graphs to pipeline dependencies. Algorithms on graphs follow in FND-PRG-103.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify whether a relation is a function, an equivalence or an order, and which properties it has.

    Given twelve relations from data and software.

    Evidence supervised AI-off test, at least 70% correct.

  2. Awareness

    Distinguish valid from invalid arguments and translate plain-language rules into propositional and predicate logic.

    Given five access and data-quality rules.

    Evidence rules checked by truth table or solver, four of five correct.

  3. Awareness

    Describe direct, contradiction and induction proofs and identify the gap in a flawed proof.

    Given six short proofs, two with planted errors.

    Evidence marked answers in the supervised test.

  4. Applied

    Apply induction and loop invariants to prove simple claims about integers, sets and recursive code.

    Given claims first tested in code.

    Evidence rubric-marked proofs, each with its test code.

  5. Applied

    Compute counts of arrangements, selections and collisions and verify them by enumeration.

    Given hashing and record-linkage problems.

    Evidence a notebook in which formula and brute-force counts agree.

  6. Applied

    Use a graph model of dependencies to find cycles and compute a topological order.

    Given a pipeline graph with a planted cycle.

    Evidence output naming the cycle and a valid order once it is removed.

Topics

Sets, functions and relations

  • Sets and Cartesian products
  • Injective, surjective and bijective functions
  • Equivalence relations and orders
  • Joins as relation composition

Logic

  • Propositional logic and truth tables
  • Normal forms and Boolean satisfiability (SAT)
  • Predicates and quantifiers

Proof

  • Direct proof, counterexample and contradiction
  • Mathematical and structural induction
  • Loop invariants
  • Testing a claim versus proving it

Counting

  • Sum and product rules
  • Permutations and combinations
  • Pigeonhole and inclusion-exclusion

Graphs and growth

  • Graphs, trees and directed acyclic graphs
  • Paths, cycles and topological order
  • Recurrences and growth of functions

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Satisfiability solver
PySAT, Z3
Graph library
NetworkX, igraph
Notebook environment
JupyterLab, marimo, a free hosted notebook service
Hint-only tutor
an open-weight model in tutor mode, an approved assistant with a tutor instruction

How the work is assessed

  • Weekly problem sets (20%)
  • Two supervised AI-off tests of proofs and short problems (40%)
  • Capstone portfolio, rubric-scored (30%)
  • Oral defence (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each supervised test

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for first attempts at problem sets and proofs and in both tests. AI-tutor in labs, pointing at gaps without writing proofs. AI-full in the portfolio, which is defended orally.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
