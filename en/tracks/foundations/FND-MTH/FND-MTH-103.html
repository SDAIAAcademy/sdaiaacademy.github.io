FND-MTH-103 Probability for Data and AI

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Extended
  • 45 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Foundations

Stream

FND-MTH Mathematics and Statistics

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Extended: 8 weeks part-time, one session and one lab a week, weekly graded problem sets and a capstone

Total hours

45 h

Prerequisites

  • School algebra and reading graphs of functions; area under a curve is introduced in the pre-work
  • Writes short Python programs with lists, loops and functions (FND-PRG-101 or equivalent)

Description

Participants build probabilistic reasoning from conditional probability to random variables, distributions, expectation and the limit theorems, checking every analytic answer with a seeded simulation. Screening and alert scenarios show base rates at work, categorical sampling shows how a language model picks tokens, and likelihood and cross-entropy connect probability to training losses. The capstone is a simulation model of a queue or alert system whose analytic and simulated answers must agree.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain conditional probability, independence and Bayes rule, and identify base-rate errors in worded claims.

    Given eight screening and alert scenarios.

    Evidence supervised AI-off test, six of eight correct with working.

  2. Awareness

    Describe the common discrete and continuous distributions and identify which fits a described process.

    Given ten processes such as arrivals, counts and waiting times.

    Evidence a justified matching table, eight of ten correct.

  3. Awareness

    Explain the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem and identify where the normal approximation fails.

    Given simulations from skewed and heavy-tailed distributions.

    Evidence annotated plots marked against a rubric.

  4. Applied

    Compute expectations, variances and conditional probabilities analytically and confirm them by seeded simulation.

    Given the weekly problem sets.

    Evidence notebooks in which both answers agree within the reported simulation error.

  5. Applied

    Implement reproducible Monte Carlo simulations that report each estimate with its standard error.

    Given a queue or alert model.

    Evidence a notebook that reruns identically and states an error bar for every estimate.

  6. Applied

    Compute likelihood, entropy, cross-entropy and Kullback-Leibler divergence and relate them to training losses.

    Given classifier probabilities and a next-token distribution at three temperatures.

    Evidence a notebook with the values and the effect of temperature explained.

Topics

Events and conditioning

  • Sample spaces and events
  • Conditional probability and independence
  • Bayes rule, base rates and likelihood ratios
  • Updating a proportion with a beta prior

Random variables and distributions

  • Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson and categorical
  • Uniform, normal and exponential
  • Joint, marginal and conditional distributions
  • Expectation, variance and covariance

Limit theorems and simulation

  • Pseudo-random generators and seeds
  • Monte Carlo estimation and its error
  • Law of large numbers and central limit theorem

Probability inside models

  • Likelihood and maximum likelihood
  • Entropy, cross-entropy and Kullback-Leibler divergence
  • Sampling with temperature
  • Calibrated probabilities versus scores

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Random generation and distributions
NumPy, SciPy stats, R
Notebook environment
JupyterLab, marimo, a free hosted notebook service
Problem-set checking
nbgrader, pytest
Hint-only tutor
an open-weight model in tutor mode, an approved assistant with a tutor instruction

How the work is assessed

  • Weekly problem sets, auto-checked (20%)
  • Two supervised AI-off tests, on paper and at the keyboard (40%)
  • Capstone model and note, rubric-scored (30%)
  • Oral check (10%)
To pass
60% overall and at least 50% on each supervised test

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for first attempts at problem sets and in both tests. AI-tutor in labs, giving hints without answers. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended orally.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
