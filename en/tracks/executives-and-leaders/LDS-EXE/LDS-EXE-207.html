LDS-EXE-207 Scaling AI from Pilots to Production

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

leadership

Format

Workshop: one and a half days with labs and a tracked follow-up

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • Has sponsored, managed or reviewed an AI pilot
  • Has used and checked AI assistants (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)

Description

Participants find why a fictional organisation's AI pilots stalled and plan how the best two reach production: they re-test one on realistic cases, score data readiness, choose an operating model, set scaling gates and redesign the process around one pilot so its value does not rest on workarounds.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify why pilots stall: problem choice, data, integration, evaluation, ownership or unchanged processes.

    Given case notes on eight stalled pilots.

    Evidence main cause and evidence per pilot.

  2. Applied

    Test a pilot's claimed performance on realistic cases against its demonstration.

    Given 30 realistic cases and a hosted model.

    Evidence pass rates on both sets and failures seen only on realistic cases.

  3. Applied

    Analyse two pilots' data readiness for access, quality, classification, lineage and refresh.

    Given their data inventories.

    Evidence a readiness score with blocking items and owners.

  4. Applied

    Compare central, federated and hub-and-spoke operating models for size, skills and risk.

    Given the case organisation's structure.

    Evidence a one-page recommendation with decision rights.

  5. Performance

    Design scaling gates on evaluation results, governance approval, cost per successful task and process redesign.

    Given the pilot portfolio.

    Evidence a two-pilot scaling plan with gates, owners and a stop rule, defended in review.

Topics

Why pilots stall

  • No path to production
  • Demonstration versus realistic cases
  • Ownership and funding after the pilot

Readiness

  • Data access, quality, classification, lineage
  • Integration with systems of record
  • Agent identity; run cost at volume

Operating model

  • Central, federated, hub-and-spoke
  • One owner for the path to production
  • Hand-over to operations (delivery is LDS-PRD-204)

Gates and redesign

  • Evaluation thresholds on realistic sets
  • Approval by risk level; monitoring, rollback
  • Redesign the process, not add a tool
  • Scale, fix or stop against a baseline

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Hosted model for re-testing
a hosted open-weight model, a commercial API
Evaluation harness
promptfoo, Inspect, a spreadsheet harness
Process mapping
bpmn.io, draw.io

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised diagnosis of an unseen pilot, AI-off (25%)
  • Lab artefacts (30%)
  • Scaling plan and review (45%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the plan

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the diagnosis; AI-tutor for readiness scoring; AI-full for the plan, defended in review.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
