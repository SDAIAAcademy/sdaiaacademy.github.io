LDS-EXE-206 Governing AI in the Organisation: Boards and Executives

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

leadership

Format

Workshop: two days with labs, a tabletop and a tracked follow-up

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • Sits on, reports to or supports a board or executive committee
  • Has used and checked AI assistants (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)

Description

Participants set up board oversight of AI for a fictional organisation: risk appetite, inventory review, impact assessment triggers, and agent and vendor expectations, tested by running an agent under two permission settings and in a board tabletop. Operating controls are in LDS-AIG-201; full incident exercises in LDS-EXE-209.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Draft a risk appetite statement with tolerances by risk level and decisions reserved for the board.

    Given a four-level risk scale and impact matrix.

    Evidence a one-page statement settling eight of ten scenario decisions.

  2. Applied

    Analyse an AI inventory for missing systems, owners, risk levels and review needs.

    Given a 20-system inventory with seeded gaps.

    Evidence a gap list and review schedule.

  3. Applied

    Specify board expectations for agent controls and vendor assurance, from permissions and stopping to certificate scope.

    Given an agent under two permission settings and two assurance packs.

    Evidence expectations tied to observed agent behaviour and one scope gap per pack.

  4. Performance

    Decide escalation and disclosure in a vendor-model incident.

    Given a timed tabletop with injected updates.

    Evidence a decision log checked against the escalation path.

Topics

Accountability and appetite

  • Board, executive, owner and assurance roles
  • Reserved decisions; three lines of defence
  • Tolerances by risk level

Inventory, agents and assurance

  • The inventory as the board's view
  • Triggers for impact assessment and review
  • Least privilege, approvals, logging, stopping
  • Certificate scope, ethics badges, provider accreditation

Oversight in practice

  • Board reporting on measures and incidents
  • Escalation and disclosure
  • National rules first, foreign regimes for exporters

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Agent sandbox with adjustable permissions
an Academy sandbox, an open agent framework on an open-weight model
Inventory and risk register
a spreadsheet template, a governance, risk and compliance tool
Vendor assurance references
Cloud Security Alliance AI Controls Matrix, ISO/IEC 42001 certificate scope

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised scenario check, AI-off (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (30%)
  • Tabletop log (15%)
  • Oversight pack (35%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the pack

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and tabletop; AI-tutor for the reviews; AI-full for the pack, defended in review.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
