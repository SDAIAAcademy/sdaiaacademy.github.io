LDS-EXE-204 Leading AI Adoption and Change

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

leadership

Format

Workshop: one and a half days with labs and a tracked 30-day follow-up

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • Has used AI assistants on own work (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)
  • Leads a unit, a function or a change programme

Description

Participants design an AI adoption programme for a fictional organisation whose staff use AI unevenly and mostly without guidance. From synthetic usage, survey and help-desk data they find shallow, unsafe or blocked use, then write role-based literacy outcomes, a manager kit, incentives that reward value rather than activity, a champions plan, an adoption dashboard and a 90-day plan.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Analyse adoption data to find shallow, unsafe or blocked use and its barrier.

    Given synthetic records for six teams.

    Evidence a diagnosis table with evidence per team.

  2. Applied

    Draft role-based literacy outcomes, each with a check, for what staff must do with AI.

    Given the all-professionals baseline and case roles.

    Evidence outcomes and checks for three groups.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a manager support kit and incentives that reward measured value and safe use.

    Given the diagnosis and performance framework.

    Evidence a kit and two incentive options with side effects.

  4. Applied

    Configure a dashboard reporting use, skill and value separately, each defined.

    Given the synthetic data.

    Evidence at least one measure of each kind.

  5. Performance

    Design a 90-day adoption plan with champions, a community of practice, measures and decision points.

    Given the lab outputs.

    Evidence a plan defended in review and started in the follow-up.

Topics

Why adoption stalls

  • Use is not value
  • Unguided and unsanctioned use
  • Managers and culture decide

Literacy by role

  • The all-professionals baseline
  • Outcomes for staff, managers, specialists
  • Checking skill with and without AI

Managers, rules and incentives

  • Manager time, permission and examples
  • Data rules people can follow
  • Rewarding value and safe use

Champions and measurement

  • Champion networks (training champions is USE-FLU-303)
  • Baselines and comparison groups
  • Scale, fix or stop

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Hosted assistant for the literacy check
a hosted open-weight model, an approved enterprise assistant
Dashboard or spreadsheet
Apache Superset, Metabase, a spreadsheet
Survey tool
LimeSurvey, Microsoft Forms

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised scenario check, AI-off (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Plan and review (45%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the plan

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check; AI-tutor for the diagnosis; AI-full for the kit and plan, defended in review.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
