LDS-EXE-208 Sovereignty, Infrastructure and Dependency Decisions

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 8 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

leadership

Format

Workshop: one day, three labs and a defended memo

Total hours

8 h

Prerequisites

  • Places a described dataset in a data classification scheme
  • Has used AI assistants and checked their output (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)

Description

Leaders decide where their organisation's AI runs, whose models it relies on and what happens if access is lost. Participants place synthetic workloads by data class, compare a hosted closed model with a local open-weight model on one bilingual task set, rehearse losing their main provider, and defend a sourcing memo. Contracts are in LDS-EXE-205; architecture in DEV-INF-405.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Classify workloads by data class and personal data, assigning each a permitted model type and placement.

    Given ten synthetic workloads and a classification scheme.

    Evidence a placement table citing the deciding rule for each row.

  2. Applied

    Compare a hosted closed model with a local open-weight model on quality, latency, cost and data path.

    Given 20 bilingual items with reference answers.

    Evidence a results sheet from a logged run.

  3. Applied

    Analyse withdrawal risk from export restrictions, model retirement, price change, outage and compute or energy limits.

    Given a provider profile and four scenarios.

    Evidence a dependency register with a warning signal and fallback per scenario.

  4. Performance

    Decide a sourcing and hosting position for three workloads with exit triggers and a tested fallback.

    Given the lab results and a stated risk appetite.

    Evidence a two-page memo defended in a ten-minute review.

Topics

Sovereignty as control

  • Control of data, operations, models and jurisdiction
  • Domestic hosting does not mean control of the model

Models and hosting

  • Closed, open-weight and open-source models
  • Public API, in-country region, government cloud, on-premises
  • Placement by classification and transfer rules
  • Self-hosting versus API cost by volume

Dependency, compute and energy

  • Export controls, retirement and price change
  • Portability, routing and exit drills
  • Accelerator supply, energy and site outages

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

a hosted open-weight model, any commercial API on a trial tier

How the work is assessed

  • Lab outputs (40%)
  • Memo on a rubric (45%)
  • Live review (15%)
To pass
60% overall and a pass on the exit-trigger criterion

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the placement table. AI-tutor in the comparison and withdrawal labs. AI-full for the memo, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
