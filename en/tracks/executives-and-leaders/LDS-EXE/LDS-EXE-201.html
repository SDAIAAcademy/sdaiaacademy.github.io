LDS-EXE-201 AI Strategy and Use-Case Portfolio

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 24 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

leadership

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

24 h

Prerequisites

  • Has used AI assistants on own work and checked their output (LDS-EXE-101 or equivalent)
  • Reads an income statement and a departmental budget

Description

Participants build an AI strategy and use-case portfolio for a fictional organisation from synthetic strategy, process and financial records. They score its maturity, triage use cases by ambiguity and risk, prototype two candidates on hosted models to test feasibility, choose build, buy or partner, and trace expected value to the accounts. The capstone is a portfolio and two business cases defended before a mock investment committee.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Analyse an organisation's AI maturity across strategy, governance, data, infrastructure, people, applications and impact.

    Given synthetic interview notes, system lists and budgets.

    Evidence a scored profile with evidence per score and the three gaps that most limit the portfolio.

  2. Applied

    Classify use cases by ambiguity and risk and assign each a mode: automate, manage with guardrails, enable exploration or keep human-led.

    Given 25 candidate use cases.

    Evidence a triage matrix with a rationale per placement and a named owner for each high-risk item.

  3. Applied

    Test feasibility claims by prototyping two use cases on a hosted model against ten realistic cases each.

    Given two triaged use cases and synthetic documents.

    Evidence a feasibility note per case with pass rate, failure types and a go, rework or stop call.

  4. Applied

    Compare build, buy and partner options on capability fit, total cost of ownership, data control, lock-in and time to value.

    Given three option sheets with indicative prices.

    Evidence a scored option matrix and the condition the choice depends on.

  5. Performance

    Build a portfolio sequenced by horizon, with funding gates and value traced to income-statement lines.

    Given the triage, the maturity profile and a fixed budget.

    Evidence a portfolio and two business cases with baseline, costs, risks and stop criterion, defended before a committee.

Topics

Where value comes from

  • Redesigned work and decisions, not deployed tools
  • Measuring value, not adoption
  • Reading surveys: samples, sponsors, definitions (figures in a dated annex)

Maturity and readiness

  • Maturity dimensions scored from evidence
  • Data readiness as the usual constraint
  • Advisory maturity assessment is LDS-ADV-202

Triaging use cases

  • Sources: pain points, decisions, documents, contacts
  • Ambiguity-by-risk triage and its four modes
  • Where agents work: an environment, full context, verifiable results
  • Risk level and a named owner as filters

Build, buy or partner

  • Capability fit and proof of concept
  • Total cost of ownership, lock-in and exit (procurement is LDS-EXE-205)

Portfolio and business cases

  • Baselines, value hypotheses and tracing to revenue, cost and risk lines
  • Horizons, funding gates and stop criteria
  • Presenting the strategy to a board

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Hosted language model for prototyping
a hosted open-weight model, a commercial assistant with file upload
Spreadsheet for scoring and business cases
LibreOffice Calc, Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised case check, AI-off (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (30%)
  • Portfolio and business cases (40%)
  • Committee defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the portfolio and business cases

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the case check. AI-tutor for maturity scoring and triage. AI-full for prototyping and business cases, which the participant defends unaided.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
