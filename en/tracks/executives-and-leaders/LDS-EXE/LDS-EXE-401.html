LDS-EXE-401 Leading the Enterprise AI Office

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Extended
  • 60 h
  • Audience: leadership

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-EXE Executive Leadership

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

leadership

Format

Extended: 10-week part-time cohort with weekly labs, two simulation days and a panel defence

Total hours

60 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-EXE-201 AI Strategy and Use-Case Portfolio
  • LDS-EXE-206 Governing AI in the Organisation: Boards and Executives
  • LDS-AIG-201 may replace LDS-EXE-206
  • Has prepared and defended a budget for a function or programme

Description

A cohort programme for the officer who runs, or will run, an organisation's AI office. Each week participants write one section of an office plan for their organisation or a fictional entity, from roadmap and mandate to agent governance, literacy, incidents and the board. Labs keep leaders hands-on: they build and measure a small agent workflow and sit on portfolio and incident simulations.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design a three-horizon AI roadmap with budgets to build, run and retire, sequenced from a maturity profile.

    Given the entity's maturity profile on a national adoption framework and a budget envelope.

    Evidence a roadmap with milestones, dependencies and yearly budget lines.

  2. Performance

    Design the AI office's mandate, operating model and decision rights alongside data, technology, security and risk functions.

    Given the roadmap and the entity's structure.

    Evidence an operating-model chart and decision-rights table.

  3. Performance

    Build an AI portfolio with stage gates, kill criteria and value traced to the accounts by cost per successful outcome.

    Given twelve use cases with synthetic cost, volume and risk data.

    Evidence a committee paper that funds five and stops one, with reasons.

  4. Performance

    Design governance for generative AI and agents: approved tools, data rules, agent permissions, evaluation ownership and coding agents.

    Given the entity's queue of tool requests.

    Evidence a policy and permission matrix that resolves eight test scenarios consistently.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate an agent workflow by building it on a hosted model and measuring success rate, cost and failure modes.

    Given a fixed task set and a call budget.

    Evidence an evaluation note from two logged runs.

  6. Performance

    Lead the leadership team's response to an AI incident and a regulator inquiry.

    Given a simulation day with live injects.

    Evidence a decision log and regulator reply rated by observers.

  7. Performance

    Defend an AI office plan covering roadmap, portfolio, governance, people and budget.

    Given a panel of an executive, a governance specialist and an engineer.

    Evidence a plan of at most 15 pages and a 30-minute defence.

Topics

Mandate and operating model

  • Reporting line and links to data, technology, security and risk
  • Central, hub-and-spoke and federated models
  • Decision rights by ambiguity and risk

Strategy and portfolio

  • Roadmaps by horizon on a national adoption framework
  • Stage gates, kill criteria and value tracing
  • Build, buy or partner; budgets to build, run and retire

Generative AI and agent governance

  • Approved tools and data rules
  • Agent permissions and human approval
  • Evaluation suites owned by the business
  • Coding agents and staff-built tools
  • Supplier and model-version review after each release

People, incidents and the board

  • Role-based literacy and protecting expertise
  • Incident oversight and model risk review
  • Regulator engagement and board reporting

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Hosted model through a standard chat API
a hosted open-weight model, any commercial model API
Agent workflow builder
a notebook with an open-source agent framework, a no-code workflow tool
Trace viewer
Langfuse, Arize Phoenix

How the work is assessed

  • Weekly plan sections (40%)
  • Observed simulations (15%)
  • Agent evaluation note (10%)
  • Final plan and defence (35%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the defence

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the entry check on portfolio and governance fundamentals and in the simulations. AI-tutor in weekly labs. AI-full for drafting plan sections, defended before the panel.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
