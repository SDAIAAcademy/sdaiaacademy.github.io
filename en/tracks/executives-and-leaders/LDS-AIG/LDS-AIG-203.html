LDS-AIG-203 Third-Party AI: Licences, Supply Chain and Retirement

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-AIG AI Governance, Risk and Assurance

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: one and a half days with labs and a tracked follow-up review

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-AIG-201 AI Governance Foundations: Principles, Roles and Lifecycle Controls
  • Reads a contract clause or terms of service and identifies the obligations on each party
  • Runs provided commands in a terminal or a notebook cell and reads their output

Description

Participants review the parts of AI that their organisation does not build: open-weight and proprietary models, datasets, code, prompts, tool servers and hosted services. They read model and dataset licences and acceptable-use terms, check provenance records and bills of materials, assess a supplier's assurance evidence, set change-notice and exit conditions, and plan the controlled retirement of a model. Labs follow a fictional organisation adopting three external models and one hosted agent service. Contract negotiation is in LDS-EXE-205; pipeline signing is in DEV-SEC-304.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Compare permissive, copyleft, restricted open-weight and proprietary terms for models, datasets and code.

    Given six licence texts and model cards from public repositories.

    Evidence a table stating, for each, commercial use, redistribution, attribution and prohibited uses, matching the reference on at least five of six.

  2. Applied

    Analyse the provenance of a model and its training data using model cards, dataset cards and a bill of materials, and record unresolved gaps.

    Given a downloaded open-weight model package and its published documentation.

    Evidence a provenance record listing sources, release history, declared data, signatures checked and open questions.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a supplier assurance review from a standard questionnaire, a management-system certificate and the supplier's evaluation results.

    Given a fictional supplier's response pack, including one certificate whose scope excludes the service offered.

    Evidence a review memo that rates each evidence item for relevance and sufficiency and flags the scope gap.

  4. Applied

    Draft change-notice, evaluation-on-change and exit clauses for an AI service, and an exit plan with data return and replacement steps.

    Given a hosted model service whose provider retires models on short notice.

    Evidence clause text and a one-page exit plan with triggers, steps, owners and a test date.

  5. Performance

    Design a retirement plan for an AI model covering rollback, archiving, record retention, user communication, dependency removal and residual risk.

    Given a production model with downstream consumers and a successor model.

    Evidence a retirement plan approved by a mock change board after one round of challenge.

Topics

What third-party AI includes

  • Foundation models called through an API
  • Open-weight models
  • Datasets and benchmarks
  • Libraries, plugins, tool servers and prompt libraries
  • AI features inside purchased software

Licences and terms

  • Permissive and copyleft code licences
  • Open-weight model licences with use restrictions and thresholds
  • Dataset licences and terms of collection
  • Acceptable-use policies of hosted services
  • Data use, retention and training on customer data
  • Data residency and cross-border transfer for hosted services

Provenance and integrity

  • Model and dataset cards
  • Bills of materials for AI and machine learning
  • Signatures and hashes
  • Mislabelled or poisoned artefacts in public hubs

Supplier assurance

  • Assurance questionnaires and controls matrices
  • Reading AI management-system certificates, their scope and the accreditation of the certifying body
  • National accreditation of AI service providers and entity ethics badges as supplier evidence
  • Supplier evaluation evidence and its independence
  • Assurance after signature

Change and exit

  • Silent model updates and deprecation
  • Re-evaluation on change
  • Portability, data return and lock-in
  • Exit plans and how to test them

Retirement and decommissioning

  • Triggers for retirement
  • Rollback, archiving and record retention
  • Dependency removal and user communication
  • Residual-risk sign-off

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Model and dataset hub with cards
public open model hubs, an internal model registry
Bill of materials standard
CycloneDX machine learning bill of materials, SPDX AI and dataset profiles
Licence and dependency scanning
ScanCode Toolkit, OSV-Scanner, any software composition analysis tool
Artefact signature verification
Sigstore cosign, open-source model signing tools built on Sigstore
Supplier assurance questionnaire
Cloud Security Alliance AI Controls Matrix and its questionnaire, the organisation's own questionnaire

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised licence quiz on unseen terms, without AI assistance (25%)
  • Graded lab artefacts (40%)
  • Review file and follow-up review (35%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the supervised quiz

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the licence quiz. AI-tutor for reading long licence and terms texts, with each claim checked against the clause. AI-full for drafting clauses and the review file, which the participant defends.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
