LDS-ADV-301 Data Products, Valuation and Monetisation

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-ADV Advisory and Value

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-DMG-101 Data Management Foundations
  • Builds a spreadsheet financial model with net present value and scenarios
  • States what data classification and sharing rules permit

Description

Participants take a fictional transport authority's synthetic ridership, ticketing and fleet-location data from asset to priced product. They score assets for readiness, analyse demand, design a product, value an asset three ways and set licence terms and prices. They test each proposal's lawfulness, including a re-identification test on a supposedly anonymised extract, and model break-even and sensitivity. The capstone is a business case defended before a mock approvals committee.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Classify data assets by expected value and assess their readiness for productisation.

    Given 15 synthetic assets with metadata covering quality, currency, classification and rights of use.

    Evidence a scored inventory naming the three most investable assets and their blocking gaps.

  2. Applied

    Analyse demand, beneficiary segments and substitutes to define use cases for a data product.

    Given synthetic market research and competing offers.

    Evidence a value proposition and two prioritised use cases with sourced demand estimates.

  3. Applied

    Compute the value of a data asset by cost, market and income approaches and reconcile them.

    Given cost records, comparable licence prices and a forecast.

    Evidence a workbook with three estimates and a reconciled range.

  4. Performance

    Design a product, licence and pricing model that balances cost, value, demand and service level.

    Given the prioritised use case and policy limits on what may be sold, to whom and at what charge.

    Evidence a product sheet with licence terms and a justified price schedule.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate product proposals against personal data, classification, sharing and intellectual property conditions and decide approvability.

    Given three proposals, one of which re-identifies travellers.

    Evidence an assessment with required approvals and a lawful redesign of the failing proposal.

  6. Performance

    Build a business case with forecast, costs, break-even, sensitivity and portfolio indicators, and recommend launch, redesign or stop.

    Given the product design and synthetic cost and demand data.

    Evidence a financial model and case defended before a committee that changes two assumptions.

Topics

Data as products

  • Improve, wrap and sell
  • Datasets, interfaces, analytical products and insight services
  • Products rather than raw data

Readiness and demand

  • Asset inventory and readiness criteria
  • Beneficiary segments, demand evidence and substitutes
  • Partners and delivery channels

Valuation and pricing

  • Cost, market and income approaches
  • Non-rival goods with near-zero marginal cost
  • Subscription, tiered, usage-based and licence pricing

Licences and regulatory conditions

  • Licence terms, including use for AI training
  • Anonymisation limits and re-identification risk
  • Classification, sharing rules and intellectual property
  • Monetisation governance: roles, approval paths and escalation

Business case and portfolio

  • Forecasts, break-even and sensitivity
  • Portfolio indicators: revenue, cost, usage, satisfaction
  • Repricing and retirement

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Spreadsheet for valuation and financial models
LibreOffice Calc, Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets
Anonymisation and re-identification risk tool
ARX, sdcMicro, a provided k-anonymity notebook

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check, AI-off: break-even, valuation and a regulatory case (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (30%)
  • Business case and model (40%)
  • Committee defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the business case and a correct break-even in the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor for spreadsheet formulas and demand analysis. AI-full for drafting the business case, which the participant defends unaided.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
