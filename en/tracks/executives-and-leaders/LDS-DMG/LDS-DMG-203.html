LDS-DMG-203 Data Quality Management

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 24 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-DMG Data Management and Governance Professions

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

24 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-DMG-101 Data Management Foundations
  • Writes simple SQL with filters, joins and grouping (FND-DAT-101 or the provided SQL pre-work)

Description

Participants run a quality programme for one service domain of a fictional agency whose monthly report disagrees with its source systems. They turn requirements into measurable rules, profile synthetic tables, execute the rules and log issues. They trace a recurring error to its origin, agree corrective and preventive actions with owners, and judge whether a dataset and a document collection are fit to train or ground an AI system. Pipeline-level contracts and observability are DEV-DAT-204.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare measurable quality rules with scope, method, threshold and frequency.

    Given requirements for ten critical data elements.

    Evidence a catalogue of 15 or more rules, each with a passing criterion and owner.

  2. Applied

    Analyse column, relationship and dependency profiles to find anomalies.

    Given four tables with ten seeded defects.

    Evidence at least eight defects found, each with the query that shows it.

  3. Applied

    Implement rules as executable checks and report results by dimension.

    Given a declarative check engine and the profiled tables.

    Evidence a run log covering every catalogued rule and a scorecard by dimension and data element.

  4. Performance

    Diagnose the root cause of a recurring issue and define corrective and preventive actions.

    Given an issue log, lineage and an entry-form specification.

    Evidence an analysis naming the origin, actions, owners and a recurrence measure.

  5. Applied

    Measure the fitness of data for training or grounding an AI system.

    Given a tabular dataset and a document collection.

    Evidence measured gaps in representativeness, labels, duplication and currency, with a use, fix or reject call.

  6. Performance

    Design a quality plan covering priorities, rules, monitoring and issue workflow for a domain.

    Given the lab results.

    Evidence a plan defended before a mock committee.

Topics

Requirements and rules

  • Dimensions: accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity, uniqueness
  • Critical data elements
  • Thresholds, tolerances and exceptions
  • Cost and service impact of poor quality

Profiling and measurement

  • Column, relationship and dependency profiling
  • Sampling when full checks cost too much
  • Scorecards that do not mislead

Issue management

  • Lifecycle, severity and escalation
  • Five whys, cause-and-effect diagrams, lineage tracing
  • Prevention at entry and integration points

Quality in operation

  • Pre-release checks before sharing or publication
  • Quality clauses in sharing agreements
  • Settling priority disputes with owners

Data used by AI

  • Quality characteristics for analytics and machine learning data (ISO/IEC 5259)
  • Representativeness, label noise, duplication, leakage
  • Currency and conflicting versions in retrieval collections
  • AI-suggested rules under review

a locally served open-weight model, any organisation-approved assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised practical without AI assistance: rules for five elements and one profile (20%)
  • Lab outputs (30%)
  • Capstone plan and defence (50%)
To pass
70% overall, and at least 60% on the practical and on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the practical and the first profiling lab. AI-tutor in rule-writing and root-cause labs. AI-full for extra rules and plan drafting in the capstone; the participant justifies each accepted suggestion.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
