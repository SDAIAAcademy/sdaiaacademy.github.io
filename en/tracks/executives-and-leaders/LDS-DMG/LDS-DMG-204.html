LDS-DMG-204 Data Classification in Practice

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-DMG Data Management and Governance Professions

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: one and a half days with labs, plus a short follow-up task

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-DMG-101 Data Management Foundations
  • Explains in plain words what access control and encryption protect against

Description

Participants classify a fictional agency's data end to end: scope structured and unstructured assets, rate disclosure impact, map ratings to levels, set handling controls and record owner-approved decisions in a register. They then measure how often a machine-labelling pipeline misses against their own decisions, and set rules for human review.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Apply an impact assessment to propose a classification level for each asset.

    Given 20 synthetic structured and unstructured assets.

    Evidence ratings, level and rationale matching the reference on at least 16.

  2. Applied

    Prepare handling requirements per level for storage, transfer, sharing, retention and disposal.

    Given the agency's systems and a control catalogue.

    Evidence a handling matrix with a named control for each level and stage.

  3. Applied

    Prepare register entries with owner approval, exceptions and declassification dates.

    Given the lab decisions and two disputed cases.

    Evidence complete entries and an escalation note per dispute.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate an assisted classification pipeline and set when a person must review.

    Given machine labels for 200 synthetic items.

    Evidence miss and false-alarm rates per level and a review policy with routing rules.

Topics

Scope

  • Structured, semi-structured and unstructured data
  • Discovery from registers and file shares
  • Classification versus personal data sensitivity

Impact and levels

  • Categories: national interest, organisational activities, individuals, infrastructure
  • Ratings mapped to four levels, public to top secret
  • Restricted sub-categories; older texts say confidential
  • Aggregation raising a level

Controls and register

  • Labelling files, records and fields
  • Access, encryption, anonymisation, retention, disposal
  • Exceptions, declassification and review

Assisted classification

  • Pattern and model-based detection
  • Misses and false alarms, notably in Arabic text
  • Human review rules and audit trail

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Personal data detection
Presidio, pattern rules in a notebook
Language model for label suggestions
a locally served open-weight model, any approved hosted model
Register and matrices
LibreOffice Calc, Microsoft Excel

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised case test without AI assistance: classify ten unseen assets (30%)
  • Lab outputs (30%)
  • Classification pack (40%)
To pass
70% overall and on the case test, with no asset at the two highest levels rated public

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the case test and first assessments. AI-full in the assisted lab, where machine labels are under test. AI-tutor elsewhere.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
