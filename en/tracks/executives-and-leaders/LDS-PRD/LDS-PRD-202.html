LDS-PRD-202 Evaluation, Experiments and Economics for AI Products

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 24 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Executives & Leaders

Stream

LDS-PRD AI Product and Delivery

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

24 h

Prerequisites

  • LDS-PRD-201 AI Product Discovery and Specification
  • Computes proportions and percentage differences in a spreadsheet
  • Reads a chart that shows a confidence interval

Description

Participants own the evidence for an AI assistant in pilot. With 300 synthetic traces, a 20,000-session experiment log and three price sheets, they code failure modes, turn them into a versioned evaluation set with graders and thresholds, plan and read an online experiment, and model cost per successful task against quality and latency, ending in a defended launch recommendation. DEV-EVL-301 builds the harness; this course trains its owner.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain why variable outputs need repeated trials, binary criteria and validated graders.

    Given four vendor-style accuracy claims.

    Evidence a critique naming what each claim omits.

  2. Applied

    Analyse traces into a failure taxonomy with frequency and severity.

    Given 100 of the pilot traces, coded with a facilitator acting as engineering lead.

    Evidence five or more failure modes, each with a definition, example trace ids and a count.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a versioned evaluation set that assigns a code check, model grader or human review and a release threshold to each criterion.

    Given the taxonomy, acceptance criteria and a grader calibration report.

    Evidence 120 examples with grader choices, thresholds and a change log.

  4. Applied

    Analyse an online experiment against primary, guardrail and cost metrics planned in advance.

    Given a synthetic two-arm log of 20,000 sessions.

    Evidence a pre-registered plan and a readout with intervals and the decision it supports.

  5. Applied

    Compute cost per successful task and latency percentiles for alternative model configurations.

    Given pass rates, token counts, timings and three price sheets.

    Evidence a unit-economics sheet with sensitivity to volume, success rate and price.

  6. Performance

    Decide whether to launch, iterate or stop by weighing quality, cost, latency and risk.

    Given all course evidence, a budget and a service level.

    Evidence a two-page launch memo defended live.

Topics

Reading traces

  • What a trace records
  • Open and axial coding with engineers
  • Frequency against severity

Owning the evaluation set

  • Code checks, model graders and human review
  • Grader agreement with human labels
  • Versioning, leakage and refresh from production
  • Consistency over repeated trials

Online experiments

  • A/B tests and shadow deployments
  • Guardrail and cost metrics
  • Minimum effect, sample size and peeking; depth in SPC-EXP-301

Cost and latency

  • Billed tokens versus user input
  • Cost per successful task, not per call
  • Model tiers, routing and caching as product levers
  • Pricing models and cost governance are LDS-EXE-202

The launch decision

  • Cost as a distribution under volume scenarios
  • Release criteria and their owners
  • Usage, cohort and feedback analysis after launch

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Trace viewer
Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, MLflow tracing
Evaluation harness
promptfoo, Inspect, a spreadsheet with a scoring script
Experiment analysis
a notebook with SciPy or statsmodels, a spreadsheet with a sample-size calculator
Model gateway logging tokens and latency (provided)
LiteLLM, Agent Router

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check: decisions supported by four readouts (20%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Launch memo on a five-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Live defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the check and the memo

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first coding of traces. AI-tutor in the experiment and costing labs. AI-full for the memo, defended without assistance.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
