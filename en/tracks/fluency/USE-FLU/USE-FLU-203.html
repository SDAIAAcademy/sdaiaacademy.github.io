USE-FLU-203 Research and Verification with AI

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of four sessions, each with a 25-minute lab, plus a tracked research task and a live spot-check

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-201 Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own
  • Participants who completed USE-FLU-202 meet the USE-FLU-201 prerequisite
  • Searches the web confidently and reads sources in English as well as Arabic

Description

Participants run research with AI tools and practise standing behind every claim they pass on. They write a research brief, classify the claims in an AI-generated report by type, read laterally to judge unfamiliar sources, trace statistics and quotations to their origin, audit citations for existence and support, and test how often a source-grounded notebook's answers are supported by the passage they cite. They finish with a verified brief on a question from their own work, built from public sources, with a verification log and a confidence statement for each key finding.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a research brief that sets the question, scope, source standards and the decision it will inform.

    Given a synthetic policy question from a public agency.

    Evidence a one-page brief that meets a five-point checklist.

  2. Applied

    Classify the claims in an AI-generated report by type and choose the check that each type needs.

    Given a deep-research report containing 20 claims.

    Evidence a claim table with type and planned check, matching the reference on at least 16 claims.

  3. Applied

    Apply lateral reading to judge the reliability of unfamiliar sources and trace each claim to its original.

    Given eight sources of mixed quality, including a fabricated study and a superseded official page.

    Evidence a source log with a reliability judgement and the original for each source.

  4. Applied

    Test every citation in an AI answer for existence, correct attribution and support for the claim it is attached to.

    Given an answer with 12 citations, some invented or misquoted.

    Evidence a citation audit table that flags every planted fault.

  5. Applied

    Use a source-grounded notebook on a curated document set and measure how often its answers are supported by the cited passage.

    Given ten public documents and twenty test questions.

    Evidence a support-rate count with three annotated failures.

  6. Performance

    Deliver a verified research brief with a verification log and a confidence statement for each key finding.

    Given a question from the participant's work, answered from public sources only.

    Evidence the brief and log, checked live by re-verifying three claims the assessor chooses.

Topics

Framing the research

  • Research briefs: question, scope, decision, deadline
  • What counts as evidence for this question
  • A search plan across languages, including Arabic sources

Claim types and their checks

  • Facts, statistics, causal claims, quotations, rules and predictions
  • Matching each claim type to a check
  • Primary versus secondary sources

Lateral reading

  • Stop, investigate the source, find better coverage, trace to the original
  • Judging unfamiliar sites, authors and outlets
  • Outdated and superseded official pages

Citations that exist and say what is claimed

  • Resolving digital object identifiers (DOIs) and links
  • Archived versions of changed pages
  • Misattribution and quotation drift
  • Invented references
  • Retracted papers and predatory outlets

Working with AI research tools

  • How deep-research agents plan and where they fail
  • Source-grounded notebooks over a curated document set
  • Measuring whether answers are supported
  • Images and screenshots: reverse search and provenance

Reporting what you verified

  • The verification log
  • Confidence statements and remaining limitations
  • Disclosure of AI assistance
  • Keeping personal and classified data out of research tools

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Deep-research assistant
an open-source deep-research agent on an open-weight model, the deep-research mode of an approved commercial assistant
Source-grounded notebook
Open WebUI knowledge collections, NotebookLM, the project workspace of an approved assistant
Scholarly and identifier lookup
OpenAlex, Crossref, Google Scholar
Web archive
Internet Archive Wayback Machine, Perma.cc
Reverse image search and provenance
TinEye, Google Lens, a Content Credentials inspector
Reference and log keeping
Zotero, a shared spreadsheet log

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check: verify three claims with ordinary search in 30 minutes (20%)
  • Lab artefacts: brief, claim table, source log, citation audit (30%)
  • Final verified brief and log (40%)
  • Live spot-check of three claims (10%)
To pass
70% overall, and no invented or unsupported citation in the final brief

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised verification check and the lateral-reading lab. AI-tutor for drafting the research brief and search plan, where the assistant questions the scope but does not write it. AI-full in the research and notebook labs and the final brief, where every AI-produced claim must appear in the verification log.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
