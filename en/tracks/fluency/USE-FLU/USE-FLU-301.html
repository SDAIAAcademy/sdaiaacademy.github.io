USE-FLU-301 Redesigning a Team Workflow with AI and Measuring the Gain

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Studio
  • 30 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Studio: five-week coached team project with three workshops, two coach reviews, two pilot runs and a panel defence

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-202 AI Productivity for Knowledge Work
  • USE-FLU-201 with USE-FLU-204 is accepted instead
  • A team of three to six from one unit, with a sponsoring manager

Description

Teams from one unit redesign a recurring workflow, such as handling enquiries or a monthly report, under coaching. They baseline time, quality and errors, map the workflow, assign each step to a person, assistant, agent or fixed rule on a delegation card with the check each AI step needs, and pilot the redesign twice against the baseline. They report the measured result, including checking and rework time, decide to scale, change or stop, and defend it before a panel. Organisation-wide redesign is LDS-EXE-203.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Measure a baseline for a team workflow: time per case, rubric quality, error rate and rework.

    Given synthetic or anonymised cases over one week.

    Evidence a baseline sheet of at least ten cases.

  2. Applied

    Analyse the workflow into steps with input data, classification, decision and accountable person.

    Given observation notes.

    Evidence a process map with a step table.

  3. Performance

    Design a delegation card assigning each step to a person, assistant, agent or rule, with the check each AI step needs.

    Given the map, approved tools and the organisation's AI rules.

    Evidence a card accepted by the coach before the pilot.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate the redesign in two pilot runs against the baseline, counting checking and rework time and errors passed on.

    Given a fixed case sample with blind quality scoring.

    Evidence a results sheet with a statement of uncertainty.

  5. Performance

    Decide whether to scale, change or stop the redesign and defend the decision with evidence.

    Given pilot results, tool costs and team feedback.

    Evidence a two-page memo defended before a panel.

  6. Applied

    Prepare a 30-day plan with owners, measures, AI-off practice for skills to keep and a stop rule.

    Given the decision memo.

    Evidence a plan with a recorded 30-day check-in.

Topics

Choosing and baselining

  • Frequent, bounded, checkable work
  • Time, quality, error and rework measures
  • What a small sample can show

Mapping and splitting

  • Steps, inputs, decisions and owners
  • Data classification per step
  • Person, assistant, agent or rule

Designing the checks

  • Verification by step and risk
  • Unchecked output passed to colleagues
  • Skills the team must keep

Running the pilot

  • Fixed samples and two runs
  • Logging checking time
  • Blind quality scoring

Deciding and sustaining

  • Scale, change or stop
  • Tool cost against time saved
  • Stop rules

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Process mapping
diagrams.net, Excalidraw
Measurement and feedback
a shared spreadsheet with LimeSurvey, the organisation's form tool
Approved assistant and agent workspace
an open-weight model behind Open WebUI, the organisation's enterprise assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Individual supervised AI-off check: interpret a pilot results table (15%)
  • Milestones (20%)
  • Team folder on a six-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Panel defence with individual questions (25%)
To pass
70% overall, 60% on the folder rubric, and two logged pilot runs

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the results check and the baseline; AI-tutor for drafting measures and the map; AI-full in the pilot and memo, where every AI step follows the card and the team owns the result.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
