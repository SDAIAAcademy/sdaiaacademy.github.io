USE-FLU-101 Data and AI Literacy Baseline for Everyone at Work

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Primer
  • 3 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Primer: self-paced on web or phone in seven short modules, in Arabic and English, with a scenario test

Total hours

3 h

Prerequisites

  • None; written for any professional in any field

Description

A self-paced primer, usable on a phone, built one-to-one on the 40 items of an all-professionals data and AI literacy baseline. Participants work through short workplace scenarios: they decide whether a task involves data or AI and where their own role stops, pick an approved tool, strip personal and confidential details from a prompt, read a dashboard before trusting it, check an AI draft against a source, write a disclosure line and choose whom to escalate to. A scenario test closes the course, and its result can serve as an organisation's training record.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish everyday use of a data or AI tool from specialist design, validation and governance work that belongs to a responsible team.

    Given eight short workplace vignettes.

    Evidence vignettes sorted correctly in the scenario test, at least seven of eight.

  2. Applied

    Classify the personal, confidential or proprietary content in files, messages and prompts, and rewrite them with safer wording before using an approved tool.

    Given five synthetic work items containing names, identifiers and confidential case details.

    Evidence sanitised versions that remove or generalise every flagged detail, marked against a key.

  3. Applied

    Prepare a request to an approved assistant that states the intended output, context, audience, constraints and review needs.

    Given a routine low-risk task such as summarising a meeting.

    Evidence a request that meets all five elements of a checklist, with one recorded improvement.

  4. Awareness

    Identify the title, source, date, unit, legend, filter and scope of a chart, dashboard or AI output, and the red flags that make it unsafe to rely on.

    Given a synthetic dashboard, explored with its filters without changing the data, and an AI-written summary of it.

    Evidence a list of red flags that includes the planted missing label, outdated date and inconsistent total.

  5. Applied

    Apply a source, sensitivity, accuracy, authorisation and impact check to decide whether to use, revise, pause or escalate an AI output.

    Given six AI-assisted drafts, some with fabricated facts or biased wording.

    Evidence a decision per draft naming the check that triggered it, matching the key on at least five of six.

  6. Awareness

    Explain why responsibility stays with the person who submits or acts on AI-assisted work, and when policy requires disclosure.

    Given a finished output prepared with AI help.

    Evidence a one-line disclosure statement naming the assistance used and what was checked.

  7. Awareness

    Identify the right contact, such as data owner, AI owner, steward, compliance contact or supervisor, for a data or AI issue beyond personal authority.

    Given escalation scenarios that affect people's rights, safety, compliance or significant resources.

    Evidence correct escalation routes chosen in the scenario test.

Topics

What data and AI are, and where your role stops

  • Data as records that can be incomplete, duplicated or outdated
  • AI output as patterns from data and instructions
  • Using a tool versus designing, validating or governing it
  • Common terms for asking clear questions

Approved tools and protecting information

  • Approved tools, accounts and channels
  • Personal, confidential, sensitive and proprietary content
  • What providers may store, log or review
  • Safer wording and minimal disclosure
  • Reading tool warnings, permissions and limits
  • No workarounds: personal accounts and unapproved tools

Asking for help and treating the result as a draft

  • Stating output, context, audience, constraints and review needs
  • Draft support, not a final answer
  • Domain judgement before acting on a suggestion

Reading charts, tables, dashboards and outputs

  • Title, source, date, unit, legend, filter and scope
  • Filtering and sorting without changing the underlying data
  • Red flags: missing labels, inconsistent totals, unclear definitions
  • Facts, opinions, predictions, assumptions and AI content
  • Decision support is not a decision

Verifying and stopping

  • Comparing with an official source or original record
  • Bias and missing perspectives
  • The five-point check: source, sensitivity, accuracy, authorisation, impact
  • When to pause or stop use

Disclosure and accountability

  • Responsibility stays with the person who acts
  • When policy requires disclosure
  • AI-assisted conclusion versus established fact
  • Consequences of data and AI choices for people and trust

Escalation and human review

  • Who owns what: data owner, AI owner, steward, compliance contact, supervisor
  • Cases affecting rights, safety, compliance or significant resources
  • Requests beyond personal authority

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Learning platform with a randomised scenario-item bank
Moodle, Open edX, the organisation's own learning platform
Sandboxed AI assistant holding only synthetic material
a locally served open-weight model behind Open WebUI, the organisation's approved enterprise assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Scenario test of 20 items drawn at random from a bank, at least two per theme, taken AI-off (70%)
  • Three marked exercises: sanitised items, request, reliance decisions (30%)
To pass
70% overall, with no theme below 50%

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario test and the sanitising exercise. AI-full in the request-writing exercise, inside a sandboxed assistant that holds only synthetic material, where the participant judges and corrects what it returns.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
