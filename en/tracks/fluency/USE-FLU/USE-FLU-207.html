USE-FLU-207 Arabic Writing, Translation and Dialect Work with AI

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 8 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: one day of six short sessions with labs, guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up

Total hours

8 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-201 Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own
  • Writes formal Modern Standard Arabic and reads English documents

Description

Participants produce and check Arabic work with AI assistants: official letters, summaries of English reports, bilingual text and dialect transcripts. They set register for formal and plain-language Arabic, control translation in both directions with a glossary, compare two assistants, find where dialect output fails, check names, numbers, Hijri and Gregorian dates against the source, and fix bidirectional text faults. A tracked follow-up checks one real document.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare requests that set register, tone, audience and terminology for formal and plain-language Arabic.

    Given a synthetic letter and public notice.

    Evidence two drafts scored on a register checklist.

  2. Applied

    Draft a bilingual glossary and use it to control an AI translation in both directions.

    Given a three-page synthetic policy text.

    Evidence a 20-term glossary and a translation matching it on a sample check.

  3. Applied

    Test AI translations and summaries against the source for wrong names, numbers, dates, units and omissions.

    Given three outputs with ten planted errors.

    Evidence an error log catching at least eight.

  4. Applied

    Analyse two assistants on the same Arabic tasks and record where neither is usable without a native reviewer or translator.

    Given five tasks including dialect transcription.

    Evidence a scored comparison table.

  5. Applied

    Analyse and fix bidirectional faults such as reversed numbers and misplaced punctuation.

    Given a document and slide with seeded faults.

    Evidence files passing a direction check.

Topics

Register and tone

  • Formal and plain public Arabic
  • Official letter conventions
  • Arabic that reads as translated

Glossary-driven translation

  • Supplying a term list
  • Back-translation checks
  • When a professional translator is required

Dialects, names and dates

  • Saudi and other regional dialects
  • Names, digits and units
  • Hijri and Gregorian dates

Bidirectional text

  • Direction marks and mixed runs
  • Spreadsheets, slides and forms

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Arabic-capable assistants
an open-weight Arabic model such as ALLaM, served locally, two approved commercial assistants
Glossary and transcription
OmegaT, Whisper run locally, an approved translation service
Editor showing direction marks
Notepad++, Visual Studio Code

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off correction of a translation and a mixed-direction paragraph (30%)
  • Lab artefacts (40%)
  • Follow-up check of one anonymised document (30%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the AI-off check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check, the first pass of each error hunt and the first glossary draft; AI-tutor for register questions; AI-full in translation, comparison and the follow-up, checked against source and glossary.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
