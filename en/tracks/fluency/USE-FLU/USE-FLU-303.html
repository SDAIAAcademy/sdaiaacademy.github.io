USE-FLU-303 Leading AI Adoption in a Team: Champions and Trainers

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of four sessions with labs, an observed practice clinic and a tracked 30-day follow-up

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-202 AI Productivity for Knowledge Work
  • Uses AI assistants weekly in own work
  • Nominated by a manager who agrees to the follow-up

Description

A train-the-trainer workshop for nominated AI champions. Participants plan and run a 45-minute clinic on colleagues' real tasks, teach a short lesson with AI-off practice and give feedback on requests and checks. They curate a shared library of requests and delegation cards, measure use and value beyond self-report, and agree how to handle low-quality AI output passed between colleagues. Leaders' adoption programmes are LDS-EXE-204.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a 45-minute clinic plan with a short lesson, a worked example, an AI-off task and safe task collection.

    Given a template and the unit's AI rules.

    Evidence a plan meeting a checklist.

  2. Performance

    Lead a clinic and give feedback that improves colleagues' requests and checks.

    Given peers playing colleagues, including a reluctant and an over-confident user.

    Evidence a passing observation sheet.

  3. Applied

    Configure a shared library of requests, standing context and delegation cards with owner, data rules and review date.

    Given ten peer entries.

    Evidence a library with complete fields and two weak entries revised or retired.

  4. Applied

    Measure use and value with indicators, a survey and a time sample rather than self-report alone.

    Given synthetic usage and survey data.

    Evidence a measurement plan and a first reading.

  5. Performance

    Deliver a 30-day adoption report on clinics, library use, measured change and a unit agreement on checking AI work before sharing it.

    Given the participant's own unit and four cases of unchecked output.

    Evidence a report reviewed in a follow-up call.

Topics

The champion role

  • Champion, trainer and manager
  • Organisational conditions over individual skill
  • Escalation beyond the role

Teaching adults

  • Worked examples, then AI-off practice
  • Tutor mode versus answer mode
  • Reluctant and over-confident colleagues

Clinics and libraries

  • Collecting tasks without confidential data
  • Review dates and retirement

Measuring and sharing

  • Why self-report overstates gains
  • Time samples
  • Returning low-quality output without blame

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Shared library
Wiki.js, the organisation's intranet
Survey and usage figures
LimeSurvey, the approved assistant's usage report
Demonstration assistant
an open-weight model behind Open WebUI, the approved enterprise assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off critique of a request and its output (15%)
  • Observed clinic (35%)
  • Lab artefacts (25%)
  • 30-day report (25%)
To pass
70% overall and a pass on the observed clinic

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and for feedback in the observed clinic; AI-tutor for the clinic and measurement plans; AI-full for library entries and the report, which the participant owns and discloses.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
