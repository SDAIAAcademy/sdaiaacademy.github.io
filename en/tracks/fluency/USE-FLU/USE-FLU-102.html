USE-FLU-102 Reading Data, Charts and Claims at Work

  • Level 100 · Explain
  • Primer
  • 6 h
  • Audience: beginner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-FLU Fluency for Everyone

Level

Level 100 · Explain

Audience

beginner

Format

Primer: one facilitated day of six sessions or six self-paced modules, with a scenario test

Total hours

6 h

Prerequisites

  • Opens a spreadsheet, sorts a table and writes a simple formula

Description

Participants practise reading the numbers that reach them every day: tables, charts, dashboards, forecasts and the AI summaries written about them. They check source, date, unit, definition and filter; recompute a headline average in a spreadsheet; separate percentage change from percentage points; work out what a correlation can and cannot show; read forecast ranges; and redraw misleading charts honestly. Data come from public open-data portals and synthetic reports. The primer ends with a scenario test and an AI-written summary of a dashboard in which the participant must find the planted errors.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify the source, owner, date, unit, definition and filter scope of a chart, table or dashboard before using its numbers.

    Given six charts from public statistics and two synthetic dashboards.

    Evidence a completed reading card per display with no missing field.

  2. Applied

    Compute mean, median, percentage change and percentage-point change to check a stated headline figure.

    Given a small table with a skewed distribution and a headline that misuses the average.

    Evidence a corrected headline with the spreadsheet working shown.

  3. Awareness

    Distinguish correlation from causation and name the likely confounder or selection effect behind a stated link.

    Given five claims taken from news items and synthetic internal reports.

    Evidence a two-line note per claim that matches the reference reasoning on at least four of five.

  4. Applied

    Analyse a display for misleading design such as a truncated axis, dual axes, a selective time window or a missing denominator, and redraw it honestly.

    Given four misleading charts with their source data in a spreadsheet.

    Evidence redrawn charts with a one-line explanation of each fix.

  5. Applied

    Classify the statements in a report or AI summary as fact, opinion, prediction, assumption or AI-generated content, and test each factual statement against the underlying table.

    Given an AI-written summary of a synthetic dashboard containing three planted errors.

    Evidence a marked-up summary that catches at least two of the three errors.

  6. Awareness

    Explain what a forecast range, margin of error or sample size says about how far a number can be trusted.

    Given three forecasts and two survey results with stated uncertainty.

    Evidence scenario items on uncertainty answered correctly.

Topics

Before you read the number

  • Source, owner, date and refresh
  • Units, definitions and denominators
  • Filters and scope in dashboards
  • Using filters without changing the data

Summaries and averages

  • Mean versus median on skewed data
  • Percentage change versus percentage points
  • Absolute versus relative change in risk
  • Rates per population
  • Totals that do not add up

Relationships and causes

  • Correlation and causation
  • Confounders and selection effects
  • Small samples and chance
  • Survivorship
  • A pattern that reverses when groups are combined

Forecasts and uncertainty

  • Ranges, margins of error and scenarios
  • What a trend line assumes
  • Forecasts as decision support, not fact

Honest and misleading displays

  • Truncated and dual axes
  • Selective time windows
  • Area, three-dimensional and cumulative distortions
  • Missing baselines and comparisons

Claims about data, including AI summaries

  • Claim, evidence, assumptions, limitations
  • Fact, opinion, prediction, assumption and AI content
  • Checking an AI summary against the table

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Spreadsheet
LibreOffice Calc, Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets
Dashboard viewer
Metabase, Apache Superset, a commercial business intelligence viewer
Public statistical data
a national open-data portal, World Bank Open Data, Our World in Data
AI assistant that writes the summaries to be checked
a locally served open-weight model, any approved commercial assistant

How the work is assessed

  • Scenario test of 15 items on displays and claims, AI-off (60%)
  • Marked lab outputs: reading cards, recomputed headline, redrawn charts (25%)
  • Error hunt on an AI summary of a dashboard (15%)
To pass
70% overall and at least two of the three planted errors found

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario test and the recomputation and redrawing labs, so the participant reads the data unaided. AI-tutor allowed for explaining terms during the self-paced modules. AI-full only in the final exercise, where an assistant writes the summary and the participant checks it.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
