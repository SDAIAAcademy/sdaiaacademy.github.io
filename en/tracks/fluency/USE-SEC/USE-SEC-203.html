USE-SEC-203 AI in Government Services

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-103 How AI Systems Learn and Where They Fail
  • Works in or advises a public service

Description

Officials who commission and answer for public-service AI work on a fictional social support agency with synthetic Arabic and English data. They test a citizen assistant, measure document-extraction errors, count the households a benefit-fraud model wrongly flags and write a transparency record with an appeal route, ending with a defended map. Individual use is USE-PRO-201.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify which public-service AI uses affect rights or benefits and the duties that follow.

    Given ten agency use cases.

    Evidence a tiering table correct on eight rows.

  2. Applied

    Test a bilingual citizen service assistant against official answers and classify its errors.

    Given 40 synthetic enquiries and published rules.

    Evidence a test sheet scoring correctness and escalation.

  3. Applied

    Analyse a benefit-fraud model's false positives at a chosen threshold and who bears them.

    Given 10,000 synthetic scored applications.

    Evidence a threshold memo with wrongly flagged households by subgroup.

  4. Applied

    Draft a public transparency record and appeal route for a decision-support system.

    Given a system from the labs.

    Evidence a record covering purpose, data, human role and contest route.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking agency use cases by public value, data readiness and risk tier.

    Given the synthetic agency.

    Evidence the map, defended before a mock leadership board.

Topics

Uses

  • Citizen service assistants
  • Document automation
  • Policy and consultation analytics
  • Fraud and eligibility checks

Testing public-facing systems

  • Grounding in official sources
  • Arabic, dialect and English quality
  • Escalation to a person

Accountability

  • Transparency records and algorithm registers
  • The right to contest
  • Automated benefit-decision failures
  • Government data classification and sharing
  • Procurement terms for public AI

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Assistant over provided documents
Open WebUI with an open-weight model, an approved government assistant
Document text extraction
Tesseract, Docling
No-code analysis
Orange Data Mining, KNIME, a spreadsheet

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Map (35%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the map; no automated adverse decision lacks a reviewer and appeal route

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check; AI-tutor in labs; AI-full for the record and map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
