USE-SEC-212 AI in Cities, Tourism and Pilgrimage

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of eight sessions with labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-201 Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own
  • Knows how a city, venue or pilgrimage service plans for visitors; uses a spreadsheet

Description

City, venue, tourism and pilgrimage-service managers test forecasting, crowd simulation, multilingual assistance and energy-saving claims on synthetic and public data, weigh camera and biometric options against privacy rules and build an opportunity-and-risk map for a synthetic host city.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain which city and visitor AI uses carry the highest safety and privacy stakes.

    Given six described deployments.

    Evidence a ranked table naming data and affected people.

  2. Applied

    Compare an arrival forecast with a seasonal baseline and report the error at staffing peaks.

    Given three seasons of synthetic arrivals.

    Evidence a peak-error table and staffing buffer.

  3. Applied

    Use a pedestrian simulation to test whether a gate plan keeps density below a limit.

    Given a provided plaza model and two plans.

    Evidence peak densities and a chosen plan.

  4. Applied

    Test a multilingual visitor assistant for wrong answers and failed hand-over to staff.

    Given 40 scripted questions and answers in four languages.

    Evidence a scored sheet by language.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking use cases with controls for camera, location and biometric data.

    Given a synthetic host city profile.

    Evidence a one-page map defended in five minutes.

Topics

Uses

  • Crowd forecasting and pedestrian simulation
  • Multilingual visitor assistance
  • Building and district energy
  • Transport, heat stress and public safety

Privacy in public space

  • Counting versus identifying people
  • Retention and minimisation
  • Children and vulnerable visitors
  • Rules on biometric identification

Live operations

  • Human command and stop authority
  • Fallbacks when systems fail

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Pedestrian simulation
Vadere, JuPedSim
Assistant under test
an open-weight model behind Open WebUI, an approved enterprise assistant
Building energy data
Building Data Genome Project 2, an anonymised utility meter sample
Spreadsheet
LibreOffice Calc, Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check, ten cases (25%)
  • Lab records (35%)
  • Map and five-minute defence (40%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the AI-off check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check, the privacy review and scoring assistant answers. AI-tutor in the forecast, simulation and energy labs. AI-full for the map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
