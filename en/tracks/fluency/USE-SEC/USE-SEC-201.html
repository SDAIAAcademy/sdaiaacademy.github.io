USE-SEC-201 AI in Health Systems

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-103 How AI Systems Learn and Where They Fail
  • Works in or advises a health organisation

Description

Managers and clinical leads who choose and supervise health AI work on a fictional regional network with synthetic and public data. They compute what a triage alert means at local prevalence, trace a risk score to its label, check generated notes against transcripts and classify products under medical-device rules, ending with a defended opportunity-and-risk map. Individual clinical use is USE-PRO-209.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish AI products regulated as medical devices from administrative tools and explain what each requires.

    Given twelve product descriptions.

    Evidence a table correct on ten rows.

  2. Applied

    Compute predictive value, alert volume and missed cases for an imaging-triage tool at local prevalence.

    Given vendor sensitivity, specificity and the network's case mix.

    Evidence a worked calculation with a board statement.

  3. Applied

    Analyse a population-health risk score for label choice and error by patient subgroup.

    Given a cost-trained score and 5,000 synthetic records.

    Evidence an audit note naming the subgroups harmed.

  4. Applied

    Prepare a local validation and silent-trial plan for an ambient documentation tool.

    Given a vendor time-saving claim and twenty generated notes.

    Evidence a plan with measures, owner and stop criteria.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking health use cases by value, data readiness and risk tier.

    Given the synthetic network.

    Evidence the map, defended before a mock governance board.

Topics

Uses

  • Ambient documentation
  • Imaging triage
  • Population health risk scores

Clinical evidence

  • Predictive value at local prevalence
  • External validation across sites
  • Silent trials, alert fatigue, monitoring

Data and rules

  • Health data as sensitive data
  • Coded data and interoperability standards
  • De-identification limits
  • Software as a medical device and change control
  • Clinical accountability

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Synthetic and public health data
Synthea, NIH ChestX-ray14 with precomputed scores
No-code analysis
Orange Data Mining, KNIME, a spreadsheet

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Map (35%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the map; every high-risk use case names a human reviewer

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check and classification lab; AI-tutor in other labs; AI-full for the map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
