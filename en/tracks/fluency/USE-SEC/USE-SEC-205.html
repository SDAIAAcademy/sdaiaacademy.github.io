USE-SEC-205 AI in Mobility and Logistics

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-103 How AI Systems Learn and Where They Fail
  • Works in or advises transport or logistics

Description

Managers who plan networks, run fleets and answer for road safety work on a fictional city logistics operator with public trip data and synthetic telematics. They compare an optimised delivery plan with a manual one, test a holiday demand forecast, read incident data without statistical traps and place automated-driving claims on the automation levels, ending with a defended map.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Describe the driving automation levels and what a claim implies for the driver and operating domain.

    Given eight vendor and press claims.

    Evidence a table correct on six rows.

  2. Applied

    Configure a routing run with time windows and capacities and compare it with a manual plan.

    Given 120 synthetic deliveries on open map data.

    Evidence a comparison of distance and lateness naming constraints the solver missed.

  3. Applied

    Test a demand forecast against seasonal-naive baselines across a holiday period.

    Given public trip records and a provided forecast.

    Evidence an error table and a trust rule.

  4. Applied

    Analyse fleet incidents with exposure-adjusted rates and a regression-to-the-mean check.

    Given a synthetic fleet year for 200 vehicles.

    Evidence a note with rates per million kilometres.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking mobility use cases by value, data readiness, safety impact and risk tier.

    Given the synthetic operator.

    Evidence the map, defended before a mock safety committee.

Topics

Uses

  • Routing and dispatch
  • Trip and parcel forecasting
  • Telematics and driver monitoring
  • Traffic management and crash hotspots

Methods

  • Optimisation versus prediction
  • Routing constraints
  • Calendars and events in forecasts

Safety and automation

  • Exposure and before-and-after traps
  • Automation levels and operational design domains
  • Takeover and incident reporting (safety cases in DEV-PHY-401)
  • Location data and worker privacy

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Vehicle routing solver
VROOM, OR-Tools
Open map and transport data
OpenStreetMap, public trip and timetable data
No-code analysis
Orange Data Mining, KNIME, a spreadsheet

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Map (35%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the map; every safety-related pilot names a takeover role

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check and automation-levels lab; AI-tutor in other labs; AI-full for the map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
