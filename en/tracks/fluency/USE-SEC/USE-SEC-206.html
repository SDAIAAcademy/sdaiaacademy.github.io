USE-SEC-206 AI in Financial Services

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-103 How AI Systems Learn and Where They Fail
  • Works in or advises a financial services firm

Description

Managers who own risk in banking, payments and insurance work on a fictional digital bank with synthetic and public data. They set fraud and anti-money-laundering alert thresholds, compare a credit model with a scorecard, check decline reasons and test a service assistant for binding commitments, ending with a defended map. Individual finance work is USE-PRO-203.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the model risk lifecycle, from inventory to monitoring, and who holds each duty.

    Given five models at the bank.

    Evidence an owner, validator and measure table.

  2. Applied

    Compute what fraud and laundering models catch, miss and cost in review work at three thresholds.

    Given synthetic transactions and laundering data.

    Evidence a threshold sheet with a recommendation.

  3. Applied

    Analyse a machine-learning credit model against a scorecard for accuracy, stability, group approval rates and decline reasons.

    Given public credit data.

    Evidence a comparison table and five checked reason statements.

  4. Applied

    Test a customer service assistant for wrong commitments, unsupported advice and data disclosure.

    Given 30 scripted conversations and product terms.

    Evidence a classified failure log and escalation rule.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking financial use cases by value, data readiness, model risk tier and explanation duty.

    Given the synthetic bank.

    Evidence the map, defended before a mock risk committee.

Topics

Uses

  • Payment fraud
  • Credit scoring
  • Anti-money-laundering monitoring
  • Customer service

Model risk management

  • Inventory and tiering
  • Independent validation
  • Drift and challenger models
  • Generative models in the inventory

Explanation and fairness

  • Reasons for adverse decisions
  • Limits of post-hoc explanations
  • Proxy variables
  • Customer data in external models

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Synthetic and public financial data
PaySim, AMLSim, German credit data
No-code analysis with explanations
Orange Data Mining, KNIME, a notebook with SHAP
Assistant under test
an open-weight model over product terms, a sandboxed approved assistant

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Map (35%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the map; no decline or suspicious-activity report is left to a model alone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check; AI-tutor in labs; AI-full for the map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
