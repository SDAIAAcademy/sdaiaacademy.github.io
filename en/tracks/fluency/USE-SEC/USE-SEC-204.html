USE-SEC-204 AI in Education Systems

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-SEC AI in Sectors

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs, tracked pre-work and a defended map

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-103 How AI Systems Learn and Where They Fail
  • Leads or advises schools or universities

Description

Leaders who set policy, buy platforms and protect learners work on a fictional education directorate with synthetic and public data. They test a tutor for answer-giving and unsafe replies, check an essay scorer and an early-warning model by student group and draft integrity and safeguarding rules, ending with a defended opportunity-and-risk map. Classroom assessment is USE-EDU-201; building tutors is USE-EDU-204.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish learning-support, assessment, admission and administrative AI uses by who is affected.

    Given twelve described uses.

    Evidence a tiering table correct on ten rows.

  2. Applied

    Test a tutoring assistant for answer-giving, accuracy and age-appropriate behaviour.

    Given 30 scripted prompts across two age bands.

    Evidence a sheet counting full answers, errors and unsafe replies.

  3. Applied

    Analyse agreement and error by student group for an essay scorer and an early-warning model.

    Given double-marked synthetic essays and public learning data.

    Evidence an audit note with a use recommendation.

  4. Applied

    Draft an AI integrity and safeguarding policy covering minors' data, permitted uses, disclosure and incidents.

    Given the directorate's context.

    Evidence a two-page policy scored on a rubric.

  5. Performance

    Build an opportunity-and-risk map ranking education use cases by learning value, data readiness and risk tier.

    Given the synthetic directorate.

    Evidence the map, defended before a mock board of governors.

Topics

Uses

  • Tutoring
  • Scoring and feedback
  • Admissions and early warning
  • Teacher planning

Learning effect

  • Tutor mode versus answer mode
  • Unguided help and unaided performance
  • Pilots that measure learning

Decisions and safeguarding

  • Agreement with markers by group
  • Grading controversies and appeals
  • Minors' data and guardian consent
  • Age-appropriate design and incidents

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Tutor under test
an open-weight model with tutoring instructions, a sandboxed platform tutor
Learning data
Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, synthetic double-marked essays
No-code analysis
Orange Data Mining, KNIME, a spreadsheet

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off scenario check (20%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Map (35%)
  • Panel defence (10%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the map; every admission, grading or progression use has human review

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the scenario check; AI-tutor in labs; AI-full for the policy and map, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
