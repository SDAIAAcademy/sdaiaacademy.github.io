USE-PRO-209 AI for Clinical and Health Professionals

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-PRO Fluency for Professions

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs on one synthetic case, plus a tracked baseline

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-201 Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own
  • Practises or trains in a clinical or allied health profession

Description

Clinicians use AI on the work around care, not the clinical decision, in one synthetic diabetes clinic: notes from scripted consultations, patient information in Arabic and English, evidence lookup, and which patient data may enter which tool. Health-system strategy is USE-SEC-201.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Test an AI-drafted note against its consultation for omissions, fabrications, negation, laterality and dose errors.

    Given three synthetic recordings.

    Evidence an audit grading each error by harm, and a corrected note.

  2. Applied

    Prepare plain-language Arabic and English patient information and check it against the source guidance.

    Given a medication change.

    Evidence two versions passing an accuracy and readability checklist.

  3. Applied

    Use an evidence assistant and verify each claim against the guideline or trial cited.

    Given four structured clinical questions.

    Evidence evidence cards with checked sources and a certainty statement.

  4. Applied

    Apply health-data rules to choose a tool per task and de-identify a case.

    Given a synthetic record and three tools.

    Evidence a tool decision record and a de-identified extract passing the checklist.

  5. Performance

    Redesign one recurring documentation task with AI, measuring accuracy and time on two runs and keeping one unassisted step.

    Given the pre-work baseline.

    Evidence a redesign card, reviewed live.

Topics

Limits of AI in clinical work

  • Where decision support becomes a medical device
  • Automation bias and deskilling

Documentation and communication

  • Scribe errors and review before signing
  • Consent for recording
  • Arabic register and reading level

Evidence lookup

  • Guidelines, trials and reviews
  • Currency, applicability and disagreement

Health data protection

  • Health data as sensitive data
  • De-identification limits
  • Approved tools and retention

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Synthetic patient data
Synthea, course recordings
Note drafting from speech
Whisper with an open-weight model, an approved clinical scribe
Evidence sources
PubMed, Cochrane Library, national guidelines
De-identification
Presidio, a rule-based checklist

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off check: audit an AI note against its transcript and sort five tools by medical-device status (20%)
  • Labs (35%)
  • Redesign card (35%)
  • Live review (10%)
To pass
70% overall, and every high-harm error in the supervised check found

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and one deliberately unassisted capstone step. AI-tutor while framing clinical questions. AI-full in the labs, with the clinician reviewing every output.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
