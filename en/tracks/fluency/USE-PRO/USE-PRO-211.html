USE-PRO-211 AI for Engineers and Field Operations

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-PRO Fluency for Professions

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days of labs on one synthetic case, plus a tracked baseline

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-FLU-201 Working with AI Assistants: Delegate, Describe, Judge, Own
  • Works in engineering, maintenance or operations and reads procedures and drawings

Description

Engineers and technicians use AI on the documents of one synthetic pumping station, from manuals, procedures and logs to inspection photos and a piping and instrumentation diagram, checking every output before it reaches equipment. Sector strategy is USE-SEC-209.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Identify field tasks where AI output must never drive action, and the check each needs.

    Given twelve uses, from a toolbox talk to a set-point change.

    Evidence a risk-tier table matching the reference on ten.

  2. Applied

    Use a source-grounded assistant to update a procedure, checking each step, value and unit against the current revision.

    Given a change form and a revised manual.

    Evidence a redline listing the hazards the assistant missed.

  3. Applied

    Analyse free-text maintenance logs with AI-assisted coding and computed counts for recurring faults.

    Given two years of synthetic logs.

    Evidence a fault table checked on a 50-entry sample.

  4. Applied

    Test a multimodal assistant's reading of inspection photos, gauges and drawings against known values.

    Given thirty labelled photos and a diagram.

    Evidence a misread table and a rule for when a person must read.

  5. Performance

    Redesign one field documentation task, such as voice notes to a report, and measure it on two runs.

    Given the pre-work baseline.

    Evidence a redesign card naming the competent person's sign-off, reviewed live.

Topics

Safety and data limits

  • Drafting support versus control actions
  • Permit to work and management of change
  • Sensitive drawings and photos; local models

Manuals, procedures and logs

  • Grounded answers and revision control
  • Units, tolerances and bilingual terms
  • Fault counts from code

Images and drawings

  • Gauges, small text and symbols
  • Voice notes to structured reports

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Source-grounded assistant
Open WebUI with document collections, an approved assistant's project workspace
Multimodal and speech models
a local open-weight vision-language model, Whisper, an approved assistant
Computation of counts
a Python notebook, LibreOffice Calc

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off check: find wrong values, units and steps in an AI procedure (20%)
  • Labs (35%)
  • Redesign card (35%)
  • Live review (10%)
To pass
70% overall, with every safety-relevant error in the check found

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and the risk-tier lab. AI-tutor while redlining: it asks about hazards, never writes steps. AI-full elsewhere; no AI output reaches equipment unchecked.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
