USE-EDU-202 Delivering the Cross-Disciplinary Data and AI Curriculum

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-EDU Learners and Educators

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days with labs, guided pre-work and a micro-teach

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • USE-EDU-101 Data and AI Fluency for University Students
  • Or an AI-off entry check on the six components
  • Teaches or will teach a university course

Description

Faculty prepare to teach the national cross-disciplinary data and AI curriculum: they choose stand-alone, embedded or distributed delivery and map it to the outcomes, adapt the recommended labs, build a discipline track pack, calibrate marking of use-case plans and teach one component. Wider assessment redesign is USE-EDU-201.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain the six components, the six intended learning outcomes and their mapping to international frameworks.

    Given the curriculum and its appendices.

    Evidence an alignment summary checked against a key.

  2. Applied

    Prepare an alignment matrix from a chosen delivery model to the intended and component outcomes.

    Given the participant's course or programme.

    Evidence a matrix with every outcome covered and assessed.

  3. Applied

    Adapt one recommended lab and build a track pack with a local case and synthetic mini-dataset.

    Given the track rules and a discipline the curriculum lacks.

    Evidence a lab run by peers and a pack reviewed by another discipline.

  4. Applied

    Apply the curriculum's assessment principles to mark student use-case plans.

    Given six synthetic plans and a shared rubric.

    Evidence marks within one band of the reference on five plans.

  5. Performance

    Deliver a ten-minute hands-on micro-teach of one component.

    Given peers acting as students.

    Evidence a recorded micro-teach reviewed against an observation rubric.

Topics

The curriculum and its options

  • Intended and component outcomes
  • Stand-alone, embedded or distributed delivery
  • Optional enrichment topics

Teaching by doing

  • Prediction versus reality, red-team challenge, coverage test
  • Workflow map, prompt tournament, data-governance and tool-risk labs
  • Effortful labs feel less effective

Track packs

  • The five listed tracks and their rules
  • New packs for health, business, law, engineering and education

Assessment

  • Verification, disclosure, data safety, cultural and context sensitivity
  • Marking calibration and moderation

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

AI assistant for student labs
an open-weight model run locally, the university's licensed commercial assistant
No-code model builder
Orange Data Mining, a browser-based classifier trainer
Learning management system
Moodle, Canvas

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off check (15%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Delivery plan and track pack (30%)
  • Observed micro-teach (20%)
To pass
70% overall and 'meets' on every micro-teach criterion

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and individual calibration marks. AI-tutor while adapting labs. AI-full for drafting the pack and plan, disclosed.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
