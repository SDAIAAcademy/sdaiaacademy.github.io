Stream

USE-EDU Learners and Educators

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days with labs, guided pre-work and a tracked redesign task

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • Teaches or assesses learners in any setting
  • Brings one course's assessment briefs, without student data

Description

Educators stress-test a course's assessments with assistants and redesign them in two lanes: secure tasks that certify unaided skill, and open tasks with guided, disclosed and defended AI use. They see why detectors fail, practise orals and process-evidence review, and write a course AI policy. Credential policy is LDS-EXE-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain why AI-text detectors cannot support an integrity decision.

    Given a claimed false-positive rate and three cohort sizes.

    Evidence the expected false accusations per cohort.

  2. Applied

    Analyse each graded task for what it certifies and how far an assistant completes it.

    Given own briefs and the assistants learners use.

    Evidence an audit table with a stress-test result per task.

  3. Applied

    Classify tasks into secure and open lanes and draft a course AI policy with per-task use labels.

    Given the audit and an AI assessment scale.

    Evidence a lane map with a secure task at each stage gate, and a policy a peer applies unambiguously to three cases.

  4. Applied

    Prepare a short oral check and a process-evidence review.

    Given a synthetic submission with version history and AI transcript.

    Evidence a question bank, rubric and marks from a mock oral.

  5. Performance

    Design a replacement for an exposed assignment that rewards judgement, process and defence.

    Given an exposed assignment from the audit.

    Evidence the new brief and rubric, re-tested with an assistant and defended.

Topics

Why detection fails

  • Error rates at cohort scale
  • Bias against non-native writers
  • Human review before any finding

The two-lane model

  • Secure tasks at stage gates
  • Open tasks with an AI-use menu and disclosure
  • Why unenforceable bans damage validity
  • Tasks needing judgement, local context or critique of AI

Oral and process evidence

  • Orals: single questions, randomised cases, think time
  • Drafts, version history, transcripts
  • Accessible alternatives to voice orals

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

AI assistant for stress tests
an open-weight model run locally, the commercial assistants learners use
Task-level AI-use scale
AI Assessment Scale (AIAS), the institution's own scale
Editor with version history
Nextcloud Office, Google Docs

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off check (15%)
  • Lab artefacts (35%)
  • Capstone plan (35%)
  • Defence (15%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the check and mock orals; AI-full for stress tests, where the assistant is the method, and for the defended capstone.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
