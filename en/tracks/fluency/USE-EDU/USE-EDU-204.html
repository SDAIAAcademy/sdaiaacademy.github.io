USE-EDU-204 Designing AI Tutors and Course Assistants

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Workshop
  • 12 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

Data & AI Fluency

Stream

USE-EDU Learners and Educators

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two half-days with labs, guided pre-work and a tracked pilot

Total hours

12 h

Prerequisites

  • Can supply a course's worked solutions, rubric and practice problems
  • Has written standing instructions for an assistant, or completed USE-FLU-201

Description

Educators configure a course assistant that tutors rather than solves and test it before learners meet it: hint-ladder and explain-back instructions, worked solutions and a rubric for accurate hints, guardrails, and learner-style probes including answer extraction. They then plan how to measure its effect on unaided performance. Building tutor software is Developers work.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain what separates a tutor from an answer engine.

    Given two transcripts on the same problem.

    Evidence an annotated comparison naming worked solutions, single steps, explain-back and withheld answers.

  2. Applied

    Configure a hint-only tutor with standing instructions, worked solutions and a rubric.

    Given one unit of the participant's course.

    Evidence the configured tutor and its instruction file.

  3. Applied

    Test the tutor with learner-style questions, including answer-extraction attempts.

    Given a 30-question test set and the worked solutions.

    Evidence a log of correct-hint, leaked-answer and error rates, with fixes.

  4. Applied

    Implement guardrails for learner privacy, AI disclosure and escalation to the teacher.

    Given institutional rules and six misuse scenarios.

    Evidence guardrail text and a retest handling each scenario.

  5. Performance

    Design a small evaluation of whether the tutor improves unaided performance.

    Given one cohort and an AI-off post-test.

    Evidence a plan with a comparison group or crossover, consent and a decision rule, defended.

Topics

What makes a tutor work

  • Tutor trials versus open-assistant trials
  • Worked solutions and rubrics in context
  • Hint ladders and explain-back in Arabic and English

Guardrails and testing

  • Answer extraction and instruction override
  • Retention and teacher oversight
  • Leak and error rates, retest after each fix

Measuring learning effect

  • Practice scores versus unaided post-tests
  • Crossover and comparison groups, consent
  • AI that supports a human tutor instead

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Assistant with saved instructions and files
a self-hosted open-source chat interface, an approved commercial assistant's custom-assistant feature
Test log
promptfoo with a provided configuration, a shared spreadsheet template

How the work is assessed

  • AI-off check on two transcripts (20%)
  • Configured tutor and test log (40%)
  • Evaluation plan and pilot note (25%)
  • Review (15%)
To pass
70% overall and no full solution leaked in the final retest

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check; AI-full while configuring and testing, since the assistant is the object of the work.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
