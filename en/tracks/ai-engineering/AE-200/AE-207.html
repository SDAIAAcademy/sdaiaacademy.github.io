AE-207 Responsible and Secure AI Engineering Practice

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

AE-200 AI Engineering: courses that help prepare for the Assistant level

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • FN-106 Responsible Data and AI Practice in the Kingdom
  • AE-203 Building Applications on Language Models
  • Has built, or can read, a small language model application with retrieval
  • Can name the four national data classification levels
  • Computes error rates from a confusion matrix, as taught in FN-105 or AE-201

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants turn data protection, security and responsible-AI duties into engineering work on one application. For a fictional housing support fund that uses a language model to summarise applicant documents and an eligibility screening model, they convert Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) obligations into testable requirements, classify every data flow, add masking, pseudonymisation and retention controls, test the assistant against prepared injection and leakage attacks, add basic controls, check the screening model for subgroup errors, record risks in a register using the national framework, and write a system card and a plain-language statement of limits for case officers and applicants. Full attack and hardening work is left to AE-308.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain how PDPL principles, data classification levels and the AI Ethics Principles apply at each stage of a language model application.

    Given the architecture diagram of the case assistant.

    Evidence an annotated diagram scored against a rubric in the supervised check.

  2. Applied

    Classify the data flows of an AI application and implement the minimisation, masking and retention controls that follow from the classification.

    Given the assistant's document pipeline and synthetic applicant records.

    Evidence a classification table, code changes and tests showing that personal fields never reach the model or the logs.

  3. Applied

    Test a language model application against prepared injection and data-leakage cases mapped to a standard risk list, and record which succeed.

    Given the case assistant and 20 prepared attack documents.

    Evidence a test log with the outcome of every attack, mapped to the risk list.

  4. Applied

    Implement basic controls: separation of untrusted content, output validation, least-privilege tool access and a human approval step for decisions about people.

    Given the attack results and the assistant's code.

    Evidence a re-run showing fewer successful attacks, with each remaining one explained.

  5. Applied

    Analyse risks including subgroup errors of the screening model and third-party model terms, and record each in a risk register with likelihood, impact, treatment and owner.

    Given screening predictions with region and applicant-group fields and the published terms of two candidate models.

    Evidence a risk register that follows the phases of the national risk framework.

  6. Performance

    Defend the release conditions of an AI application to non-technical stakeholders using a system card, the risk register and a plain-language statement of limits.

    Given a mock release review with a case-officer lead and a governance officer.

    Evidence a system card and a five-minute briefing scored on a rubric.

Topics

From law and policy to requirements

  • Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) principles for builders
  • Lawful basis, purpose and minimisation
  • Retention, deletion and data subject rights
  • Transfers outside the Kingdom
  • Records of processing
  • Breach handling and notification duties

Classification in a build

  • The four national classification levels
  • Classifying data flows
  • What may reach a hosted model
  • Masking and pseudonymisation

Risks of language model applications

  • The Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) Top 10 for LLM Applications
  • Prompt injection and data leakage
  • Excessive agency and insecure tool use
  • Insecure output handling
  • The OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications at concept level

Basic controls

  • Separating untrusted content
  • Output validation
  • Least-privilege tools
  • Human approval for decisions about people
  • Logging for audit without personal data

Fairness and risk management

  • Subgroup error rates
  • Bias categories from the national bias guide
  • Risk tiers in the AI Ethics Principles
  • The four phases of the national AI risk framework
  • Likelihood and impact scoring
  • A risk register with owners

Third-party models

  • Licences and acceptable-use terms
  • Data-processing terms
  • Data residency
  • Build or buy notes

Documentation and communication

  • System cards
  • Statements of limits for users
  • Briefing non-technical stakeholders
  • Disclosure that users are talking to AI

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Data duties in a build
1.1From law to engineering requirementsTurn five PDPL obligations into testable requirements for the assistant: lawful basis, purpose, minimisation, retention and access requests
1.2Classify before you buildClassify each data flow of the assistant with the national levels and decide which may reach a hosted model
1.3Minimise, mask and pseudonymiseAdd masking and pseudonymisation to the document pipeline and measure how answer quality changes
1.4Records, retention and deletionWrite the processing record for the assistant and implement a retention rule with a deletion test
Day 2 Risks of language model applications
2.1A risk list for language model applicationsMap the assistant's components to the OWASP list and rank the five most relevant risks
2.2Prompt injection and data leakageRun ten prepared injection attempts hidden in applicant documents and record which change the output or leak data
2.3Basic controlsAdd content separation, output validation, least-privilege tools and an approval step to a supplied branch, then re-run the attacks
2.4Risk assessment with the national frameworkScore likelihood and impact for five supplied top risks and record treatments and owners in a risk register
Day 3 Fairness, documentation and stakeholders
3.1Fairness checks on the screening modelMeasure error rates by region and applicant group with intervals and match the gaps to categories in the bias guide
3.2Third-party models and licencesCheck the licence, data-processing terms and data residency of two candidate models and write a build or buy note
3.3System cardsWrite a system card for the assistant: purpose, data, evaluation, limits, controls and owner
3.4Communicating limits to stakeholdersRewrite the limits section of a supplied system card for case officers and for applicants in plain Arabic and English and rehearse the briefing

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Personal data detection and masking
Presidio, regular-expression and dictionary rules
Language model access
a locally served open-weight model, a free hosted open-weight model, any commercial API
Attack test harness for language model applications
promptfoo, PyRIT, garak
Output validation
Pydantic validators, rule-based output filters
Fairness metrics
Fairlearn, pandas group-by with bootstrap intervals
Risk register and documentation
Markdown templates in the repository, a spreadsheet

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised scenario check without AI assistance: classify data flows, name the PDPL duty a design breaks, spot the risk in a described feature (25%)
  • Capstone pack scored on a five-criterion rubric: classification and privacy controls 25, attack testing and controls 25, risk register 20, system card 15, statement of limits 15 (55%)
  • Stakeholder briefing with questions from a governance assessor (20%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the supervised check and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised scenario check and the first classification of data flows. AI-tutor in the attack and control labs: the course assistant hints but does not write controls. AI-full for drafting documentation in the capstone, where the participant verifies every claim and defends it in the briefing.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
