AE-202 Deep Learning and Generative AI Fundamentals

  • Level 200 · Apply
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

AE-200 AI Engineering: courses that help prepare for the Assistant level

Level

Level 200 · Apply

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • AE-201 Machine Learning Foundations and Model Evaluation
  • FN-103 Mathematics for Machine Learning: Vectors, Gradients and Probability
  • Multiplies matrices and computes a derivative with the chain rule
  • Has trained and evaluated a model with a train, validation and test split

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Assistant level.

Description

Participants go from tensors to a working generative model and learn what each layer of the stack does. For a fictional crafts cooperative they write a training loop by hand, debug a network that will not learn, adapt a pretrained image model to synthetic textile images, implement attention for a toy sequence, build bilingual semantic search with sentence embeddings, and generate text with a small open-weight model while varying sampling settings. They finish by comparing model families on quality, size, latency, licence and cost for three of the cooperative's use cases.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Explain how a transformer turns input tokens into next-token probabilities, naming the role of embeddings, attention, the context window and sampling.

    Given an annotated architecture diagram with five blanks and two generated outputs.

    Evidence a completed diagram and a short written explanation scored against a rubric in the supervised check.

  2. Applied

    Implement a training loop with a loss, an optimiser, mini-batches and validation checks, and use it to train a small network.

    Given a tensor library, synthetic image tensors and a starter script with no training code.

    Evidence a script whose loss and validation curves match the reference behaviour within a stated tolerance.

  3. Applied

    Apply a debugging checklist to a network whose loss does not fall and correct each fault found.

    Given a training script with three seeded faults in the loss input, the learning rate and data shuffling.

    Evidence a log showing the overfit-one-batch test, each fault and the corrected learning curve.

  4. Applied

    Use transfer learning to adapt a pretrained image model to a new label set with little data, and measure the improvement over training from scratch.

    Given 800 labelled textile images in eight classes and a laptop CPU time budget.

    Evidence a results table comparing frozen features, partial fine-tuning and training from scratch on the same test set.

  5. Applied

    Use sentence embeddings to build and test a bilingual semantic search over product descriptions.

    Given 4,000 Arabic and English descriptions and 50 labelled queries.

    Evidence recall at 5 for Arabic, English and mixed queries, with three failure cases and a likely cause for each.

  6. Applied

    Measure how temperature, nucleus sampling and the instruction change the outputs of a small open-weight language model.

    Given a locally run model and 20 fixed prompts sampled five times each.

    Evidence a table of variation, length and rule violations per setting, with an explanation of one surprising result.

  7. Performance

    Evaluate model families for three stated use cases on quality, size, latency, licence terms and cost, and recommend one per use case.

    Given a supplied shortlist of open-weight and hosted models and results measured in the labs.

    Evidence a model-choice table with a two-line justification per use case, defended in the capstone.

Topics

Tensors and automatic differentiation

  • Tensors, shapes and broadcasting
  • Computational graphs and gradients
  • Devices and number formats

Training neural networks

  • Losses and optimisers
  • Mini-batches and learning rates
  • Dropout, weight decay and augmentation
  • Learning curves and early stopping
  • Debugging training

Convolutional networks and transfer learning

  • Convolutions and pooling
  • Pretrained backbones
  • Freezing and fine-tuning
  • Evaluating a deep model and checking for near-duplicates

Sequence models and transformers

  • Tokenisation, including Arabic
  • Embeddings and position
  • Self-attention
  • Encoder, decoder and encoder-decoder models

Embeddings and search

  • Sentence embeddings
  • Similarity measures
  • Multilingual embeddings
  • Recall at k

How generation works

  • Next-token prediction
  • Greedy decoding, temperature and nucleus sampling
  • Context windows
  • Instruction and preference tuning at concept level
  • Reasoning effort and its cost
  • Fabricated answers and knowledge cut-off

Model families and choice

  • Encoders, decoders, diffusion and vision-language models
  • Open-weight versus hosted models
  • Size, latency and cost
  • Licences and data provenance

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Tensors and training
1.1Tensors, shapes and gradientsCompute a gradient by hand and with automatic differentiation for a two-layer network and compare the numbers
1.2Writing the training loopWrite a training loop with batching, a loss and an optimiser for the textile images and plot the loss curves
1.3Learning rates, batches and regularisationRun the same model at three learning rates, with and without dropout, and read the curves
1.4When training goes wrongFind three seeded faults in a broken script, starting with an overfit-one-batch test
Day 2 Vision and transfer
2.1Convolutions and what they detectTrain a small convolutional network and visualise its first-layer filters
2.2Pretrained backbonesExtract features with a pretrained image model and train a linear classifier on them
2.3Fine-tuning with little dataFine-tune the last blocks with augmentation on 800 images and compare with supplied frozen-feature results
2.4Evaluating a deep modelBuild a confusion matrix, inspect the ten most confident errors and check the splits for near-duplicates
Day 3 Transformers and embeddings
3.1Tokens, including ArabicTokenise the same Arabic and English sentences with three tokenisers and compare token counts
3.2Attention step by stepImplement scaled dot-product attention for a toy sequence and check it against the library function
3.3Sentence embeddingsEmbed the product descriptions and measure recall at 5 on 50 labelled queries
3.4Multilingual search and its failuresCompare Arabic, English and mixed queries and collect three failure cases with a likely cause
Day 4 Generation and model choice
4.1Next-token prediction and samplingGenerate from a small local model at four temperatures and measure variation across five samples
4.2From base models to assistantsCompare a base model and an instruction-tuned model of the same size on ten tasks
4.3Limits: fabrication, cut-off and contextProbe the model with questions beyond its training cut-off and inputs beyond its context window and record the failures
4.4Choosing a model familyFill a model-choice table for three cooperative use cases from supplied lab measurements of latency, size and quality and each model's licence, then choose the capstone task

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry quiz and exit check without AI assistance: shapes and gradients by hand, reading a learning curve, the transformer diagram, the effect of sampling settings (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a five-criterion rubric: working model and training 25, evaluation against a simpler alternative 25, measurement of latency and size 15, licence and limits 15, reproducibility 20 (60%)
  • Live defence in which the participant explains one curve or failure the assessor selects (20%)
To pass
70% overall, with at least 60% on the capstone rubric and at least 60% on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and for the first attempt at the hand-written training loop and attention labs. AI-tutor in the other labs: the course assistant explains and hints one step at a time. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
