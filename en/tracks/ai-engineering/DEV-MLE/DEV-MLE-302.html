DEV-MLE-302 Speech and Audio Systems Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-MLE Machine Learning Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-MLE-202 Deep Learning Engineering
  • Has containerised a small web service (DEV-OPS-201 or equivalent)
  • Reads Arabic script, or brings another language with dialect variation for the labs

Description

Participants build a speech pipeline end to end: they prepare audio and transcripts, benchmark and fine-tune open-weight recognisers, add voice activity detection and speaker diarisation, stream recognition within a latency budget, and test synthesised speech for read-back. Arabic is the main case, with Modern Standard Arabic, dialects, code-switching with English, and the normalisation rules that decide whether an error rate means anything. Voice agents and turn-taking are in DEV-LLM-205; training speech models from scratch is in SPC-GEN-303.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare a speech dataset with segmentation, transcript normalisation rules and speaker-disjoint splits.

    Given raw recordings in Modern Standard Arabic and two dialects.

    Evidence a versioned dataset with a data card and split statistics showing no speaker overlap.

  2. Applied

    Measure word and character error rates, real-time factor and latency by dialect, speaker group and channel.

    Given two open-weight recognisers and a held-out test set.

    Evidence a report with confidence intervals per slice and ten analysed errors.

  3. Performance

    Optimise an open-weight recogniser for a target domain and dialect by fine-tuning, and decide whether the improvement justifies its cost.

    Given a few hours of in-domain audio and a fixed notebook GPU budget.

    Evidence a run log, a before-and-after comparison on held-out slices and a decision memo.

  4. Applied

    Implement voice activity detection and speaker diarisation, and compute the diarisation error rate.

    Given multi-speaker recordings with reference turns.

    Evidence speaker-attributed transcripts and a scored report.

  5. Applied

    Test an open text-to-speech model with a recognise-back check and a small listening panel.

    Given prompts with numbers, names and diacritised text.

    Evidence a sheet of round-trip error rates and listener ratings per prompt type.

  6. Performance

    Build a streaming transcription service that meets a latency budget and degrades gracefully under load.

    Given a container runtime and a streaming client.

    Evidence a running service, a load-test log and a latency distribution against the budget.

Topics

Speech fundamentals

  • Spectrograms and log-mel features
  • Encoder-decoder, connectionist temporal classification and transducer recognisers
  • Self-supervised multilingual encoders
  • Acoustic models, vocoders and voice cloning

Arabic speech data

  • Dialects and code-switching
  • Normalising diacritics, letter variants and numerals
  • Punctuation and inverse text normalisation
  • Open corpora and their licences

Recognition and diarisation

  • Long-form segmentation
  • Speaker embeddings and diarisation
  • Adapters versus full fine-tuning
  • Noise, reverberation and data augmentation
  • Hallucinated text on silence

Streaming and deployment

  • Chunked and streaming decoding
  • Real-time factor and batching
  • Quantised models on CPU
  • Monitoring channel and accent drift

Evaluation and responsible use

  • Error rates with intervals
  • Listening tests
  • Voice as biometric personal data

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Open-weight speech recognition
Whisper-family models, wav2vec-family self-supervised encoders
Quantised CPU inference and load testing
whisper.cpp, ONNX Runtime, Locust or k6 for load tests
Speech toolkit
ESPnet, SpeechBrain
Voice activity detection and diarisation
pyannote.audio, Silero VAD
Error-rate scoring
jiwer, a custom scorer with Arabic normalisation
Public speech data
Mozilla Common Voice, Arabic broadcast corpora from shared tasks

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check on features, error rates by hand and normalisation (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone and defence (50%)
To pass
70% overall, 60% on the capstone and a pass on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first hand computation of word error rate. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the defended capstone.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
