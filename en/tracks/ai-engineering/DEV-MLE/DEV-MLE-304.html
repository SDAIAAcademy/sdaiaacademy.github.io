DEV-MLE-304 Multimodal Systems Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-MLE Machine Learning Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two days with labs and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-LLM-201 Building Applications on Language Models
  • Has fine-tuned or evaluated a pretrained image or text model

Description

Participants build applications that read images and video with vision-language models: field extraction from inspection and retail photos, answers grounded with boxes and points, and time-stamped questions over sampled video frames. They choose between an open-weight and a hosted model on their own test set. Document parsing is in DEV-LLM-204; model training in SPC-GEN-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Implement question answering and field extraction from images with schema validation.

    Given 100 labelled inspection and retail photos.

    Evidence validated JSON output with accuracy per question type.

  2. Applied

    Use boxes and points returned by the model to check that each answer refers to the right region.

    Given images with reference boxes.

    Evidence an intersection-over-union report and a list of ungrounded answers.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate an open-weight and a hosted vision-language model for accuracy, hallucination, cost and latency, and choose one.

    Given their own test set, a budget and a latency target.

    Evidence a decision memo with per-slice results and their uncertainty.

  4. Performance

    Build a video pipeline that samples frames, answers time-stamped questions and routes low-confidence segments to human review.

    Given public clips with reference events.

    Evidence a running pipeline and event-level precision and recall.

  5. Awareness

    Explain where image resolution, cropping and frame sampling lose information a model needs.

    Given three failure cases.

    Evidence correct explanations in the supervised quiz.

Topics

How vision-language models see

  • Visual tokens, resolution and token cost
  • Frame sampling and temporal context
  • Image-text embeddings for search and filtering

Building with images and video

  • Image prompts with structured outputs
  • Grounding with boxes and points
  • Pairing a general model with a specialist detector
  • Keyframes and time-stamped answers

Evaluation and limits

  • Test sets from own images
  • Object-existence and hallucination checks
  • Arabic text in images
  • Instructions hidden in images

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Vision-language model access
an open-weight model served with vLLM or llama.cpp, a hosted model API on a free tier or Academy credits
Detection and segmentation for cross-checks
an open-vocabulary detector, Segment Anything-family models
Evaluation harness
Inspect, pytest with custom scorers

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised quiz (15%)
  • Graded labs (40%)
  • Capstone and defence (45%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised quiz. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the defended capstone.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
