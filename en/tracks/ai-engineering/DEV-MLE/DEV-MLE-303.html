DEV-MLE-303 Recommender and Ranking Systems Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-MLE Machine Learning Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-MLE-201 Machine Learning Engineering: Baselines, Validation and Pipelines
  • DEV-MLE-202 Deep Learning Engineering
  • Writes SQL joins and window functions over event tables

Description

Participants build a two-stage recommender on a public interaction log: candidate generation with matrix factorisation, a two-tower model and an approximate nearest-neighbour index, then a learned ranker with business rules and diversity re-ranking. They evaluate offline with time-based splits, estimate a new policy's effect from logged data, and design an online test with guardrails. Cold start, popularity bias and feedback loops are treated as engineering problems with measurable controls. Experiment statistics in depth are in SPC-EXP-301, and policy learning in SPC-EXP-401.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare an interaction dataset with implicit-feedback labels, time-based splits and leakage checks.

    Given a public log of views, clicks and purchases.

    Evidence a split report showing no future interactions in training.

  2. Applied

    Implement candidate generation with matrix factorisation and a two-tower model served from an approximate nearest-neighbour index.

    Given a recall target at a fixed candidate count.

    Evidence recall at k and query latency for both methods against a popularity baseline.

  3. Performance

    Build a learned ranking stage with business rules and diversity re-ranking over retrieved candidates.

    Given candidate lists, item features and stated constraints.

    Evidence a pipeline whose normalised discounted cumulative gain (NDCG), coverage and diversity beat the retrieval-only baseline or are explained.

  4. Applied

    Compute an off-policy estimate of a new ranking policy from logged data, with its variance.

    Given logged recommendations with propensities.

    Evidence a report comparing inverse-propensity and self-normalised estimates.

  5. Performance

    Design an online experiment for a ranking change, with primary metric, guardrails, duration and feedback-loop risk.

    Given a proposed change and daily traffic figures.

    Evidence a peer-reviewed experiment plan with a sample-size calculation.

  6. Performance

    Diagnose cold-start and popularity-bias failures and select controls for new users, new items and long-tail exposure.

    Given slice metrics for new and long-tail items.

    Evidence a memo with before-and-after slice metrics for one control.

Topics

Framing

  • Explicit and implicit feedback
  • Relevance, diversity, novelty and value
  • Multi-stage architecture and latency per stage

Candidate generation

  • Matrix factorisation and item similarity
  • Two-tower models and negative sampling
  • Approximate nearest-neighbour indexes
  • Sequence-aware and language-model-based retrieval

Ranking

  • Pointwise, pairwise and listwise losses
  • Gradient-boosted rankers
  • Re-ranking for diversity, freshness and rules

Evaluation

  • Time-based splits and leakage
  • Recall, NDCG and coverage
  • Why sampled-negative metrics mislead
  • Position bias and click models
  • Off-policy estimation
  • Interleaving and guarded online tests

Feedback loops and operations

  • Cold start
  • Popularity bias and exposure fairness
  • Feature freshness and retraining cadence
  • Detecting self-reinforcing loops

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Analytical data processing
DuckDB, Polars, pandas
Learning-to-rank objectives
LightGBM, XGBoost
Recommender model libraries
RecBole, TorchRec, implicit
Vector index
FAISS, hnswlib
Off-policy evaluation
Open Bandit Pipeline, estimators written in NumPy
Public interaction data
MovieLens, Open Bandit Dataset, public retail event logs

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check on ranking metrics and leakage, computed by hand (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone and defence (50%)
To pass
70% overall, 60% on the capstone and a pass on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first implementation of the ranking metrics. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the defended capstone.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
