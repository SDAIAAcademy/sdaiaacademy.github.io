DEV-MLE-301 Computer Vision Engineering

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 36 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-MLE Machine Learning Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

36 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-MLE-202 Deep Learning Engineering
  • Reads precision-recall curves and computes precision and recall by hand

Description

Participants build vision systems that survive field conditions. On public inspection images and street-scene video they audit annotations, fine-tune convolutional and transformer backbones, train detectors and segmenters, track and count objects, and fit a model to a latency budget. Every lab ends with a test under changed lighting, blur or camera position. Vision-language models are DEV-MLE-304; on-device runtimes are DEV-INF-303.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare an image dataset by auditing labels, near-duplicates and leakage by site, and writing annotation guidelines.

    Given a public detection dataset and an annotation tool.

    Evidence an audit report and a guideline with two-annotator agreement on 100 images.

  2. Applied

    Apply transfer learning from convolutional and transformer backbones with a stated augmentation policy.

    Given a classification task and a GPU-hour budget.

    Evidence a tracked comparison with error by lighting, blur and viewpoint.

  3. Applied

    Compute mean average precision over intersection-over-union thresholds and analyse detection errors by type.

    Given outputs of a fine-tuned detector and segmenter.

    Evidence an evaluation notebook with an error-type table.

  4. Performance

    Build a video pipeline that detects, tracks and counts objects crossing a line.

    Given a public clip with labelled tracks.

    Evidence a running pipeline with counting error, identity switches and frames per second.

  5. Performance

    Optimise a vision model to a latency, memory and accuracy budget through resolution, export and quantisation.

    Given a target device class.

    Evidence a trade-off table and a configuration that meets the budget on the assessor's run.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate a vision system under field conditions and decide whether it is fit for release.

    Given a test set extended with blur, brightness, occlusion and viewpoint shifts.

    Evidence a go or no-go memo with results per condition, limits and drift signals to monitor.

Topics

Visual data

  • Boxes, masks, keypoints and annotation formats
  • Guidelines, agreement and label noise
  • Near-duplicates and leakage by scene or site
  • Augmentation and what it assumes; sensors are DEV-PHY-201

Backbones and tasks

  • Convolutional networks and vision transformers
  • Self-supervised open-weight backbones and their licences
  • Detection, semantic, instance and promptable segmentation
  • Keypoints and pose in brief; document text is DEV-LLM-204
  • Open-vocabulary detectors as zero-shot baselines

Video

  • Frame sampling against latency
  • Tracking by detection and identity metrics
  • Counting and event rules

Evaluation and release

  • Precision-recall, average precision and overlap metrics
  • The gap between test and production
  • Corruption and shift tests; monitoring camera drift

Deployment constraints

  • Resolution, batch size and numeric precision
  • Export to an interchange format and quantisation
  • Measuring latency and memory on CPU and accelerators

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

torchvision detection models, Ultralytics (check its copyleft licence)

Dataset inspection

Image and video processing
OpenCV, Pillow
Interchange format and inference runtime
ONNX Runtime, ExecuTorch, LiteRT
Compute
free notebook GPU, Academy GPU hours

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check: overlap and average precision by hand, reading a precision-recall curve (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone with live defence on a five-criterion rubric (50%)
To pass
70% overall, 60% on the capstone, and the latency budget met on the assessor's run

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first annotation exercise. AI-tutor in labs. AI-full in the capstone, defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
