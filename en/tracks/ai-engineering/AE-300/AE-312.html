AE-312 AI Cost Engineering and Model Routing

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 21 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

21 h

Prerequisites

  • AE-203 Building Applications on Language Models
  • AE-205 Deploying AI Services: Containers, Serving and Monitoring
  • Groups and joins usage logs with SQL or dataframes
  • Has run an evaluation set against a language model application

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Associate level.

Description

Participants make the cost of language model applications measurable, attributable and smaller without losing quality. For a fictional telecom operator running three applications behind one gateway, a bilingual care assistant, a call-summary job and a billing-dispute agent, they work from a month of synthetic usage traces: they model cost per request and session, attribute spend to teams, set budgets, evaluate model tiers and reasoning settings on their own tasks, route with fallbacks, measure prefix and semantic caches, compare hosted and self-hosted serving and report cost per successful outcome. Cluster capacity planning is AE-307; engine-level optimisation is AE-401.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute cost per request and per session from prompt, output and cached tokens, retries, agent steps and accelerator-hours.

    Given a month of usage traces and a synthetic invoice.

    Evidence a cost model that reconciles with the invoice within 2%.

  2. Applied

    Configure attribution, budgets and limits per team, application and environment on a model gateway.

    Given three applications sharing one gateway.

    Evidence a showback table and a replayed spike that trips the hard limit.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate model tiers, reasoning settings and a routing policy on the operator's own tasks, choosing by cost per successful outcome at a fixed quality floor.

    Given 100 labelled tasks per application.

    Evidence a results table and a router configuration with its measured savings.

  4. Applied

    Measure how prefix and semantic caching change cost, latency and answer correctness, including isolation between customers.

    Given replayed traffic for the care assistant.

    Evidence hit rates, a wrong-answer rate and a passing cross-customer isolation test.

  5. Performance

    Decide between hosted APIs and a self-hosted open-weight model with a break-even analysis that includes utilisation and data-placement rules.

    Given price sheets, a throughput benchmark and the operator's classification register.

    Evidence a break-even chart and a recommendation.

  6. Performance

    Deliver a cost review with three measured savings options to engineering and finance stakeholders.

    Given an unseen capstone scenario.

    Evidence a notebook and a two-page review reporting energy beside cost, defended in ten minutes.

Topics

The cost model

  • Prefill, decode and cached tokens
  • Retries, agent steps and context growth
  • Accelerator-hours and utilisation

Attribution and budgets

  • Keys and tags per team and application
  • Showback before chargeback
  • No settled allocation method for multi-agent work

Model choice and routing

  • Own-task evaluation at several settings
  • Cascades, tiers and fallbacks
  • Cache cost of switching models

Caching

  • Prompt layout for prefix reuse
  • Response and semantic caches
  • Staleness and isolation between customers

Hosting and reporting

  • Self-hosting break-even
  • Batch versus interactive tiers
  • Cost per successful outcome
  • Energy beside money

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Where the money goes
1.1The token cost modelCompute cost per request for the three applications and reconcile it with the synthetic invoice
1.2Agents, retries and long contextsFind the ten most expensive sessions and attribute their cost to steps, retries and context growth
1.3Attribution and showbackTag spend by team, application and environment through gateway keys and produce a showback table
1.4Budgets and limitsSet soft and hard budgets per application and trip the hard limit with a replayed spike
Day 2 Route and cache
2.1Own tasks, several settingsScore supplied outputs of three model tiers at two reasoning settings on 100 tasks for quality, latency and cost, and rerun ten tasks live to check them
2.2Routing and cascadesBuild a two-tier router with a quality check and fallback and measure cost per successful outcome
2.3Prompt layout for prefix cachingMove stable content to the front of a prompt and measure the cache hit rate and cost
2.4Semantic caches and their risksAdd a semantic cache, measure hit and wrong-answer rates and test that no answer crosses customers
Day 3 Host or buy, and report
3.1Self-hosting economicsFill a cost model for serving an open-weight model and find break-even utilisation against API prices
3.2Batch versus interactiveMove the call-summary job to a batch tier and recompute cost and turnaround
3.3Cost per successful outcomeJoin cost with evaluation outcomes and report cost per resolved dispute
3.4The cost reviewDraft a two-page review with three measured savings options, then choose the capstone scenario

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance on cost arithmetic and caching trade-offs (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a five-criterion rubric: cost model accuracy 20, routing evaluation 25, caching evidence 20, hosting analysis 20, review quality 15 (60%)
  • Live defence of the review (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first cost model. AI-tutor in the labs: the assistant explains queries and configuration but does not produce the analysis. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
