Description

Participants build vision systems for a fictional municipal roads and traffic department that must keep working in glare, dust, rain and at night. On public road-damage images and synthetic traffic-camera clips they audit and extend annotations, fine-tune convolutional and transformer backbones, train a pothole detector and a crack segmenter, track and count vehicles, and fit the chosen model to the latency and memory budget of a processor-only roadside unit. Each lab ends with a test under changed lighting, blur or camera position. Vision-language models and multimodal applications are AE-408.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Prepare an image dataset with annotation guidelines, an agreement check, splits that keep each road segment in one split, and augmentations that preserve label meaning.

    Given road images with partial labels and near-duplicate frames.

    Evidence a dataset card, an agreement score and a split report with no road segment shared across splits.

  2. Applied

    Implement transfer learning with two pretrained backbones and compare them by condition slice with intervals.

    Given defect crops and a free notebook GPU.

    Evidence a comparison table by lighting and weather slice with bootstrap intervals.

  3. Performance

    Build a detector and a segmenter and diagnose their errors by type to choose the next improvement.

    Given pothole boxes and crack masks.

    Evidence average precision and overlap metrics, an error breakdown and a justified next step.

  4. Applied

    Measure tracking and counting accuracy for vehicles crossing a line in video.

    Given synthetic camera clips with ground-truth counts.

    Evidence count error per clip and the number of identity switches.

  5. Performance

    Optimise a model to a per-frame latency and memory budget on a processor through export, input resolution and quantisation, re-measuring accuracy at each step.

    Given the roadside unit's stated budget.

    Evidence a benchmark table and the chosen operating point.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate the model under corruption and camera shift and decide whether to release it.

    Given rain, glare, night and moved-camera test sets.

    Evidence a model card with results per condition, privacy controls and a release decision defended live.

Topics

Visual data

  • Boxes, masks and annotation formats
  • Guidelines, agreement and label noise
  • Near-duplicates and leakage by site
  • Augmentation and what it assumes

Backbones and transfer

  • Convolutional networks and vision transformers
  • Linear probe, partial and full fine-tuning
  • Open-vocabulary models as zero-shot baselines
  • Licences of open-weight backbones

Detection, segmentation and video

  • Non-maximum suppression and average precision
  • Semantic and instance segmentation and overlap metrics
  • Tracking by detection and identity switches
  • Counting rules

Deployment constraints

  • Export to an interchange format
  • Resolution, batch size and numeric precision
  • Latency and memory on processors and accelerators

Field reliability and privacy

  • Corruption and camera-shift tests
  • Monitoring camera drift
  • Blurring faces and plates before storage
  • Model cards for vision systems

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Visual data first
1.1How vision systems fail in the fieldRun a pretrained classifier on 200 road images and sort its errors by lighting, blur and viewpoint
1.2Annotation guidelines and agreementAnnotate 30 images for a new defect class with a partner and compute agreement
1.3Leakage by site and near-duplicatesFind near-duplicate frames and re-split the data by road segment
1.4Augmentation and what it assumesBuild an augmentation pipeline and find the transform that changes what a label means
Day 2 Backbones and transfer
2.1Convolutional networks and vision transformersFine-tune two pretrained backbones on defect crops and compare them by slice
2.2How much to fine-tuneTrain a linear probe on 10% of the labels and compare it with supplied full fine-tuning runs on 10% and 100%
2.3Zero-shot baselinesScore an open-vocabulary model on the defect classes as the floor to beat
2.4Licences of open-weight backbonesCheck the licence and data terms of three backbones and record which the department may deploy
Day 3 Detect, segment and track
3.1Detection and its metricsTrain a pothole detector on a short schedule and compute average precision at two overlap thresholds
3.2Detector error analysisSort 50 supplied detector errors into localisation, class, duplicate and missed, and choose a fix
3.3Segmenting cracksFine-tune a segmenter and compare its overlap score with a thresholding baseline
3.4Tracking and countingTrack and count vehicles across a line in a clip and measure count error against the labels
Day 4 Fit the budget and release
4.1Export to an interchange formatExport the supplied detector and check its outputs match the original within tolerance
4.2Resolution and precision against latencyMeasure latency and accuracy at three resolutions and two precisions on a laptop processor
4.3Corruption and shift testsRun the model on rain, glare, night and moved-camera sets and report the drop for each
4.4Privacy and monitoring in the fieldAdd face and plate blurring before storage and define two camera-drift monitors, then choose the capstone task

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks without AI assistance on metrics, splits and error types (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a five-criterion rubric: data and splits 20, model quality and error analysis 25, latency budget 20, field-condition tests and privacy 25, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Live defence on a failure case the assessor picks (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the annotation and error-analysis labs. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains code and errors but does not write the training pipeline. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
