Description

Participants run the shared AI platform of a fictional regional health authority, where a training team, a bilingual patient-letter assistant and a nightly embedding job compete for scarce accelerators. On single-node clusters with simulated accelerators, small open-weight models and recorded traffic, they allocate and share devices, admit jobs fairly under quotas, place distributed jobs all at once, autoscale inference on the signals users feel, route requests through an inference-aware gateway, keep Restricted workloads on permitted nodes, and size capacity and cost for three hosting options. The requirements of the public Kubernetes AI conformance checklist that a simulated cluster can demonstrate serve as the acceptance rubric. Token-level cost and model routing are AE-312.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Configure accelerator allocation, sharing and health tainting for two tenants on a container cluster.

    Given a single-node cluster with simulated accelerators and a device driver that reports faults.

    Evidence manifests and a test run showing allocation, sharing and the eviction of a pod from a tainted device.

  2. Applied

    Configure queues, quotas, borrowing and all-or-nothing admission for three teams.

    Given a recorded week of training and batch job submissions.

    Evidence a replay report with wait time per team and no distributed job left partly placed.

  3. Performance

    Optimise autoscaling of an inference service on a queue or service-objective metric, including scale to zero and cold start.

    Given a recorded traffic trace and a time-to-first-token objective.

    Evidence a before-and-after comparison of minutes over objective and replica-hours.

  4. Performance

    Evaluate an inference-aware gateway against round-robin routing for goodput, latency and fairness between tenants.

    Given a mixed interactive and batch trace replayed against several replicas.

    Evidence a measured goodput table and a routing recommendation.

  5. Applied

    Compute memory, concurrency, replica count, power and monthly cost for a serving workload under cloud, on-premises and sovereign-provider options.

    Given model size, context length, a traffic forecast and price sheets.

    Evidence a capacity and cost model that reproduces the reference figures within 10%.

  6. Performance

    Design a placement policy driven by data classification and audit the cluster against the conformance checklist.

    Given the authority's classification register and a provided cluster.

    Evidence policy tests that reject a violating deployment and a conformance audit with automated evidence, defended in a design review.

Topics

Accelerators as cluster resources

  • Device plugins versus dynamic resource allocation
  • Time-slicing, partitioning and virtualised devices
  • Device health, taints and per-accelerator metrics

Admission and scheduling

  • Queues, quotas, cohorts and borrowing
  • Preemption and fairness between teams
  • Gang and topology-aware placement

Serving and autoscaling

  • Serving operators and model weights as registry artefacts
  • Scaling pods on queue and service-objective metrics
  • Scale to zero and cold start
  • Node scaling by accelerator type

Inference-aware traffic

  • Endpoint picking on queue and prefix-cache state
  • Priority, fairness and load shedding
  • Goodput against a service objective

Capacity, cost and placement

  • Weights and key-value cache arithmetic
  • Power, cooling and tokens per watt
  • Rented, owned and sovereign capacity
  • Classification before placement

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Accelerators on a shared cluster
1.1Why AI workloads strain a clusterCreate a single-node cluster with simulated accelerators and deploy a small CPU-served model
1.2Requesting devices through resource claimsRequest a simulated accelerator through a resource claim and read back the allocation
1.3Sharing and isolating devicesShare one device between two inference pods, then taint a faulty device and watch the pods move
1.4Accelerator metricsScrape per-device metrics from the supplied simulated exporter and build a utilisation panel per tenant
Day 2 Queues, quotas and placement
2.1Admission before schedulingDefine queues and quotas for three teams and replay a recorded week of jobs
2.2Borrowing and preemptionEnable borrowing between cohorts and compare each team's wait time with the baseline
2.3Gang and topology-aware placementSubmit two four-worker jobs that are each partly placed and block each other, then fix it with all-or-nothing admission
2.4Placement under data rulesLabel nodes by hosting zone and write a policy that rejects a Restricted workload on a disallowed node
Day 3 Serve and scale on the right signal
3.1Serving operators and weights as artefactsDeploy a small open-weight model through a serving operator with weights pulled from an artefact registry
3.2Why processor load is the wrong signalAutoscale on processor load, then on queue depth, against the same trace and compare minutes over objective
3.3Scale to zero and cold startMeasure cold start with and without cached weights and choose a minimum replica count
3.4Node scaling by accelerator typeSimulate node scale-up for pending accelerator pods and compute idle accelerator-hours
Day 4 Route, size and cost
4.1Inference-aware gatewaysReplay a trace through round-robin and through a queue- and prefix-aware picker and compare goodput
4.2Priority and load sheddingGive interactive traffic priority over batch and shed load at a goodput threshold
4.3Capacity from first principlesCompute memory, concurrency per accelerator and replicas for a target request rate
4.4Cloud, on-premises or sovereignFill a cost model with break-even utilisation and showback per tenant, then choose the capstone scenario

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks without AI assistance on scheduling, memory arithmetic and placement rules (20%)
  • Capstone repository on a five-criterion rubric: conformance evidence 25, scheduling and quotas 20, autoscaling and routing measurements 25, capacity and cost plan 20, reproducibility 10 (60%)
  • Design review defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the capacity arithmetic. AI-tutor in the labs: the assistant explains manifests and errors but does not write the configuration. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
