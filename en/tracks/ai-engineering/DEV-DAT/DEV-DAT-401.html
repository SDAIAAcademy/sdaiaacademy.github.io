DEV-DAT-401 Data Architecture

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 36 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-DAT Data Engineering and Architecture

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a review-and-defence day, with guided pre-reading and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

36 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-DAT-201 Modern Data Engineering
  • DEV-DAT-301 Data Modelling: Conceptual to Semantic
  • Has designed and operated a production pipeline or platform component

Description

Participants act as data architect for a synthetic group of public service entities moving from a central warehouse and scattered lakes to a governed platform. They turn interviews into quality-attribute scenarios, compare a central lakehouse, federated data products and a hybrid on five-year cost, design the catalogue as the control point across engines, place workloads by classification, set standards for federated teams, review a peer design and a vendor proposal, and defend a roadmap. Solution-architecture practice is shared with DEV-INF-404.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Analyse business, regulatory and operational requirements into quality-attribute scenarios.

    Given six stakeholder interviews and a classification register.

    Evidence a driver list with a measurable scenario per attribute.

  2. Performance

    Design a target architecture covering storage, catalogue, engines, integration, semantic layer and serving to analytics and AI.

    Given the drivers and a current-system inventory.

    Evidence views at three levels of detail tracing each driver to a design element.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate three architecture options on five-year cost of ownership and risk.

    Given synthetic price, usage and staffing data.

    Evidence an options paper and decision records naming rejected alternatives.

  4. Performance

    Design standards for federated ownership: data product descriptors, contracts, naming and the supported platform path.

    Given two simulated domain teams of different maturity.

    Evidence a standards pack applied to both domains in a lab, with the gaps it exposed.

  5. Performance

    Review a design and a vendor proposal against the standards and decide on deviations.

    Given proposals with seeded flaws.

    Evidence a risk-rated review with mitigations and a deviation decision.

  6. Performance

    Design a migration roadmap with transition states, legacy retirement and measurable milestones.

    Given a three-year delivery constraint.

    Evidence a roadmap defended before a panel.

Topics

Foundations

  • Replication, partitioning, transactions and consistency
  • Batch, streaming and serving paths
  • Integration: event-driven, API-based and extract-load-transform
  • Quality attributes and trade-offs

Platform shapes

  • Warehouse, lakehouse on open table formats, multi-engine designs
  • The catalogue as control point for names, credentials and policy
  • Semantic layers serving reports, APIs and agents

Federated ownership

  • Data products, domain teams and the platform team
  • Contracts and interoperability
  • When federation pays; data fabric claims tested

Placement and cost

  • Classification-driven placement and residency; sovereign deployment is DEV-INF-405
  • Security zones, encryption and access architecture
  • Cost of ownership and unit cost
  • Lock-in, open formats and exit plans

Architecture practice

  • Decision records and reference architectures
  • Reviews and deviation approval
  • Roadmaps and retirement
  • Reviewing AI-generated designs

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Architecture views and decision records
C4 model with Structurizr, Mermaid, Markdown records in version control
Table formats and catalogues
Apache Iceberg, Delta Lake, Apache Polaris, Unity Catalog OSS
Engines
DuckDB, Trino, Apache Spark
Contracts and semantic models
Open Data Contract Standard, Apache Ossie specification (incubating), Cube Core

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check on distributed-data trade-offs and cost arithmetic (15%)
  • Labs and peer review (30%)
  • Capstone and panel defence (55%)
To pass
70% overall, 65% on the capstone and a pass on the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, where assistant-drafted material is labelled and every decision is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
