DEV-SEC-302 AI Red Teaming and Adversarial Testing

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-SEC Security and Privacy Engineering for AI

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-SEC-201 Secure Development of AI Applications
  • Writes Python scripts that call an API
  • Computes a proportion with a confidence interval

Description

Participants plan and run a red-team exercise against two synthetic targets: a bilingual Arabic and English citizen-services assistant and a tool-using agent. They set scope and rules of engagement, pick objectives from a catalogue of adversary techniques, probe manually and with automated generators and scorers, measure attack success over repeated trials, rate findings and report them so a product owner can act. Every confirmed finding becomes a regression test. Building controls is DEV-SEC-301; governance-side testing is LDS-AIG-302.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Awareness

    Distinguish security, safety, privacy and misuse objectives and the harm each tests.

    Given four product descriptions.

    Evidence an objectives table checked against the reference.

  2. Performance

    Design an exercise plan with scope, rules of engagement, stop conditions and handling of harmful outputs.

    Given a product brief and risk register.

    Evidence a test plan approved by the system owner.

  3. Applied

    Apply manual techniques including multi-turn escalation, encoding, indirect injection and attacks in Arabic dialect and transliteration.

    Given the citizen-services assistant.

    Evidence reproducible attempts logged by technique identifier.

  4. Applied

    Configure automated probing and compute attack success with intervals.

    Given an open probing tool and a call budget.

    Evidence a run report per technique, corrected for scorer error.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate findings for severity, likelihood and reproducibility and prioritise fixes.

    Given the exercise logs.

    Evidence a findings report reviewed by a panel.

  6. Performance

    Build regression tests from confirmed findings.

    Given a repository with continuous integration.

    Evidence runs that fail before and pass after the fix.

Topics

Planning

  • Objectives tied to harms
  • Scope, rules of engagement and stop conditions
  • Handling harmful outputs and tester welfare

Attack techniques

  • Technique catalogues
  • Jailbreak families and multi-turn escalation
  • Indirect and cross-modal injection
  • Hidden-context extraction and agent goal hijack
  • Arabic, dialect, transliteration and code-switching

Automated probing

  • Generators, converters and scorers
  • Attacker models and their limits
  • Budgets and repeated trials

Scoring and reporting

  • Success rates with intervals
  • Severity, likelihood and reproducibility
  • Disclosure to suppliers

From findings to regression

  • Findings as test cases
  • Suites that survive model updates
  • Red-team suites in release gates

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Automated probing
garak, PyRIT, promptfoo
Evaluation harness
Inspect, pytest
Target and attacker models
locally served open-weight models, a hosted open-weight model
Technique catalogues and guides
MITRE ATLAS, OWASP AI Testing Guide, OWASP GenAI Red Teaming Manual
Statistics
SciPy, statsmodels
Continuous integration
GitLab CI, GitHub Actions

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check: classify ten attack transcripts by technique and severity (20%)
  • Labs (30%)
  • Capstone package and panel presentation (50%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off in the supervised check; AI-tutor in manual-attack labs so participants form their own ideas first; AI-full in automated probing and the capstone, where attacker models are part of the method.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
