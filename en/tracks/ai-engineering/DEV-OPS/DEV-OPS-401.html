DEV-OPS-401 Reliability Engineering for AI Services

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 36 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-OPS Operations and Reliability

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

36 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-OPS-302 Operating Language Model Applications: Tracing, Evaluation Gates and Safe Releases
  • Has carried on-call or incident duties for a production service
  • Reads latency percentiles and queueing behaviour; DEV-INF-401 and DEV-SEC-304 are recommended

Description

Participants set and defend reliability standards for AI services that other teams build. They write service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, load-test a self-hosted language model to find the concurrency at which goodput meets the objective, design degradation and recovery paths, lead incident drills and blameless reviews, and admit models only after verifying signatures and bills of materials. Labs use small open-weight models on a provided GPU notebook and recorded traffic. Engine tuning is in DEV-INF-401; the full go-live review across disciplines is DEV-PRJ-401.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, with error budgets.

    Given a case-study service and an online quality signal.

    Evidence an objectives document defended against a reviewer.

  2. Performance

    Evaluate service capacity by load-testing to a goodput target and setting an admission limit.

    Given a self-hosted open-weight model and a recorded traffic trace.

    Evidence a goodput-versus-concurrency curve with the operating point justified.

  3. Performance

    Design degradation for overload and dependency failure using load shedding and a smaller fallback model.

    Given three failure scenarios.

    Evidence chaos tests showing each path keeps the minimum service.

  4. Performance

    Lead an incident drill from detection to recovery and write a blameless review.

    Given a silent quality regression after a provider update.

    Evidence a timeline, communications log and owned corrective actions.

  5. Performance

    Build an admission check that verifies model signatures, an AI bill of materials and licences.

    Given signed and tampered artefacts.

    Evidence a pipeline that admits one and rejects the other.

  6. Performance

    Review another team's AI service for reliability readiness and set launch conditions.

    Given a peer's service and its telemetry.

    Evidence ranked findings and a launch decision defended before a panel.

Topics

Objectives for AI services

  • Time to first token and time per output token
  • Quality indicators from online evaluation
  • Error budgets, burn-rate alerts and release policy
  • Objectives for agents: completion and cost

Capacity under load

  • Queueing, batching and tail latency
  • Goodput against an objective
  • Trace-replay load and admission control

Designing for failure

  • Provider, retrieval and tool failures
  • Fallback models and degraded modes
  • Retry storms and chaos experiments
  • Restoring indexes, session state and model artefacts

Incidents

  • Detecting silent quality regressions
  • Roles and decision logs
  • Incidents turned into regression tests

Integrity and standards

  • Signing a manifest of hashed model files
  • AI bills of materials and admission policy
  • Readiness reviews; provenance depth in DEV-SEC-304

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Inference engine
vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp server
Load generation
inference-perf, k6, Locust
Metrics and fault injection
Prometheus with Grafana, Chaos Mesh or Toxiproxy
Model signing and bills of materials
OpenSSF Model Signing, CycloneDX ML-BOM
Cluster admission policy
Kyverno, Open Policy Agent Gatekeeper

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check: decide from a goodput curve and an alert history (15%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone review (40%)
  • Observed incident leadership (15%)
To pass
70% overall and 60% on each component

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and while leading the drill. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, defended before the panel.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
