DEV-EVL-401 Evaluation Programmes and Release Gates

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-EVL Evaluation and Quality Engineering for AI Systems

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-EVL-301 Evaluation-Driven Development for Language Model Applications
  • Has run an evaluation suite as a gate in continuous integration
  • Reads paired comparisons, confidence intervals and power calculations

Description

Participants design how an organisation decides that an AI system is fit to release and stays so. For a synthetic public-service agency with a citizen assistant, a document-triage model and an internal coding agent, they tier systems by risk, back-test release criteria, audit a public benchmark, plan monitoring and brief a mock risk committee. Pipeline implementation is in DEV-OPS-302; benchmark science is in SPC-TRU-301.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design a risk-tiered evaluation policy stating the suites, sample sizes, human review and sign-off each tier requires.

    Given an inventory of 12 synthetic AI systems and a risk management framework.

    Evidence a policy and tiering register with a rationale per system.

  2. Performance

    Design release criteria with blocking thresholds, non-inferiority margins and re-evaluation triggers fixed in advance, and back-test them.

    Given results for eight past release candidates, including a model-provider upgrade.

    Evidence a criteria file and a back-test with every false block or false pass explained.

  3. Performance

    Audit a public benchmark or vendor claim for validity, contamination, harness sensitivity and dependence on token budget.

    Given a benchmark slice, its published scores and two harness settings.

    Evidence an audit note with re-run results and a recommendation on the claim's use.

  4. Performance

    Design a monitoring plan linking production signals to offline suites and turning incidents into regression cases.

    Given a synthetic trace stream with a seeded drift and incident.

    Evidence a monitoring specification, an alert that fires on the drift, and new regression cases.

  5. Applied

    Analyse the running cost of an evaluation programme across grader calls, review hours and infrastructure, and allocate it by tier.

    Given the tiering register and unit costs.

    Evidence a cost model showing what coverage each allocation buys.

  6. Performance

    Defend a release recommendation, presenting the evidence with its uncertainty and residual risk.

    Given a model-upgrade decision for the citizen assistant.

    Evidence a two-page scorecard and a recorded mock committee session.

Topics

Evaluation as an organisational system

  • Inventory and owners; tiering by impact, autonomy and reach
  • Evidence per tier: capability, regression, adversarial, subgroup, human review, monitoring
  • Shared harness, dataset registry and grader calibration records

Release criteria

  • Blocking and advisory checks; criteria fixed in advance
  • Non-inferiority margins, noise floors and many checks at once
  • Change classes: prompt, model, retrieval, tool, provider upgrade
  • Sign-off, exceptions and their expiry

Auditing benchmarks and claims

  • Construct validity and task ambiguity
  • Contamination and models that recognise a benchmark
  • Harness, token budget and infrastructure effects on rankings

Monitoring after release

  • Sampled grading of production traffic
  • Drift, feedback and guardrail-trigger signals with named owners
  • Incidents turned into regression cases

Reporting and governance

  • Scorecards that show uncertainty and residual risk
  • System cards and evaluation records
  • Mapping tiers and gates to a risk management framework

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Evaluation harness, including benchmark runs
Inspect, lm-evaluation-harness, promptfoo
Trace store with online evaluation
Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, MLflow
Monitoring and alerting
Prometheus with Grafana, OpenTelemetry collectors with any alerting back end
Statistics and power analysis
SciPy, statsmodels

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised AI-off check: do three evaluation reports support their release claims (15%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone and panel defence (45%)
  • Follow-up gap assessment (10%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 65% on the capstone

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone and follow-up, with every tier and threshold defended by the participant.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
