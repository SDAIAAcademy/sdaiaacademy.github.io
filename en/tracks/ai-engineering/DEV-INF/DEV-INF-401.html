DEV-INF-401 Inference Performance and Cost Engineering

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 40 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-INF Infrastructure, Performance and Architecture

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

40 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-INF-201 Machine Learning Systems on One Machine
  • DEV-OPS-201 Deploying AI Services: Containers, Serving and Monitoring
  • Serves an open-weight model behind an HTTP endpoint and reads its metrics
  • Explains latency percentiles and the difference between latency and throughput
  • DEV-LLM-303 recommended

Description

Participants take a language model deployment that is too slow and too costly and make it meet a service objective at the lowest cost they can defend. They build a first-principles cost model, measure goodput curves on replayed traffic, locate bottlenecks, and apply batching, caching, quantisation, speculative decoding, routing, disaggregated prefill and decode, and autoscaling, re-checking task quality after every change.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute prefill cost, decode cost and key-value cache memory, and predict the binding resource.

    Given a model card, an accelerator specification sheet and a traffic profile.

    Evidence a sizing sheet within 25% of a measured run.

  2. Performance

    Benchmark a serving configuration as a goodput curve over concurrency against latency objectives.

    Given an engine, an open-weight model and a replayed synthetic trace.

    Evidence a repeatable curve of at least seven points, with method and variance.

  3. Performance

    Diagnose why a deployment misses its service objective.

    Given three seeded faults: queueing, cache-pressure preemption and prefill interference.

    Evidence a diagnosis per case with its evidence, and a fix confirmed by a rerun.

  4. Performance

    Optimise batching, admission, prefix caching, cache precision and speculative decoding while holding task quality.

    Given a multi-tenant chat trace and a task evaluation set.

    Evidence before-and-after goodput, cost per million tokens and quality scores with their uncertainty.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate compressed variants against the base model and decide which to serve.

    Given provided quantised and distilled variants, Arabic and English evaluation sets and a cost target.

    Evidence a quality and cost table by language and a written decision.

  6. Performance

    Design a serving topology covering disaggregation, routing, replica count and scaling policy.

    Given a daily demand pattern, a service objective and a budget.

    Evidence a design document with predicted and measured goodput and cost per successful request, defended live.

Topics

The cost model of inference

  • Prefill and decode: compute-bound and memory-bound
  • Key-value cache memory arithmetic
  • Queueing and the latency-throughput trade-off

Measuring serving performance

  • Time to first token, time per output token, goodput
  • Load patterns and trace replay
  • Pareto curves and benchmark validity

Scheduling and caching in the engine

  • Continuous batching, chunked prefill, admission control
  • Paged attention, cache precision and offload to host memory
  • Prefix and semantic caching, and tenant isolation

Cheaper models

  • Quantisation formats per accelerator
  • Speculative decoding and acceptance rates
  • Distilled and multi-adapter serving (distillation itself: DEV-LLM-301)

Serving across replicas

  • Disaggregated prefill and decode; cache-aware routing
  • Autoscaling on queue and service-objective metrics, scale to zero, cold starts
  • Cost and energy per successful request

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: size three deployments by hand (10%)
  • Graded labs (30%)
  • Capstone on a six-criterion rubric (45%)
  • Live defence (15%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the capstone rubric, and the service objective met on the assessor's held-out trace

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised sizing check and lab 1's hand calculation; AI-tutor in labs 2 to 5; AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
