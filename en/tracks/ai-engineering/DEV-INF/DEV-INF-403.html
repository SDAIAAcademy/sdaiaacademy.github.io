DEV-INF-403 Designing an Internal AI Platform

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Studio
  • 40 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-INF Infrastructure, Performance and Architecture

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Studio: six weeks part-time with weekly coaching, three design reviews and a final defence before a panel

Total hours

40 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-OPS-301 Machine Learning Operations: Pipelines, Registries and Delivery
  • Has used or operated a shared machine learning platform in a team
  • Reads container cluster manifests; has configured a model gateway and read a usage-based bill (DEV-OPS-304 covers both)

Description

Participants design the shared platform through which many teams reach models safely and at known cost. For a fictional organisation with twelve product teams and three data classification levels, each participant designs the model catalogue and admission rules, a gateway with quotas and fallback, tenant isolation, golden paths with evaluation and tracing built in, and showback, builds a thin working slice, and defends the design after three coached reviews.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Analyse platform demand into a matrix of workloads, tenants, classifications and service objectives.

    Given interview notes and synthetic usage logs.

    Evidence a requirements matrix and a capability map ranked by demand and risk.

  2. Performance

    Design a model catalogue and gateway with admission criteria, routing, quotas, fallback and audit logging.

    Given open-weight, hosted and fine-tuned models and the organisation's classification rules.

    Evidence an admission checklist and a gateway slice that refuses a restricted tenant's call to an external model.

  3. Performance

    Design tenant isolation, quotas and fair sharing of accelerators.

    Given three tenants with different classifications, budgets and load.

    Evidence a tenancy model walked through four failure scenarios, including a leaked key.

  4. Performance

    Build golden-path templates with evaluation, tracing and governance gates on by default.

    Given a retrieval application and a batch inference job.

    Evidence two templates another participant uses to ship a service within an hour.

  5. Performance

    Design cost attribution and showback with a tagging scheme and unit metrics.

    Given synthetic invoices, usage logs and tags.

    Evidence a showback report that reconciles to the invoice within 2% and names untagged spend.

  6. Performance

    Defend the platform design, its build-or-buy decisions and a phased roadmap.

    Given a panel of experienced engineers and a governance specialist.

    Evidence a design document, at least six decision records and the panel's scored rubric.

Topics

The platform as a product

  • Tenants, users and the platform team
  • Demand analysis and phasing
  • Adoption, lead time and utilisation metrics

Catalogue and gateway

  • Admission: licence, evaluation evidence, permitted classification, cost tier
  • Provider abstraction, credentials, quotas, fallback, protocol-server mediation
  • Models as versioned, signed artefacts; deprecation and migration

Tenancy and shared capacity

  • Isolation of data, keys and compute
  • Noisy neighbours and fair sharing
  • Self-hosted open-weight capacity beside hosted APIs

Golden paths

  • Templates for retrieval, agent, batch and fine-tuning work
  • Gates built into the default
  • Paved roads against mandates

Cost attribution

  • Tags and one key per team and application
  • Showback before chargeback
  • Cost per successful outcome; agent workloads without an accepted method

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

AI gateway
LiteLLM, Agent Router (formerly Envoy AI Gateway)
Model registry
MLflow, ModelPack artefacts in an OCI registry
Developer portal and templates
Backstage, Copier
Tracing and evaluation service
Langfuse, Arize Phoenix
Policy engine
Open Policy Agent, Kyverno
Cost allocation
OpenCost, billing exports in a notebook

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: critique a platform diagram (10%)
  • Lab artefacts (20%)
  • Working slice (20%)
  • Panel design review on a six-criterion rubric (40%)
  • Written review of a peer's design (10%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% on the panel rubric and no unresolved critical finding on isolation or data placement

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised diagram critique. AI-tutor in the demand and showback exercises. AI-full for the slice and documents, which the participant defends before the panel.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
