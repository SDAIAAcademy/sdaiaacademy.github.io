DEV-INF-304 Embedded Machine Learning on Microcontrollers

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-INF Infrastructure, Performance and Architecture

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Workshop: two bench days with one board per pair and a live demonstration

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-INF-201 Machine Learning Systems on One Machine
  • Reads and edits simple C or C++ code, or completes the provided two-hour firmware pre-work

Description

Participants build a sensing device that runs a neural network in a few hundred kilobytes of memory and a few milliwatts of power. On a low-cost board they record and label sensor data, train and quantise a tiny model, deploy it as firmware and measure latency, memory and energy per inference.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute the flash, RAM and energy budget of a sensing application, including the tensor arena and duty cycle.

    Given a board datasheet and a target battery life.

    Evidence a budget sheet within 15% of the build's memory map and measured current.

  2. Applied

    Prepare a sensor dataset and feature pipeline with a documented collection protocol.

    Given consenting cohort recordings and a public keyword dataset.

    Evidence a dataset card and a split by speaker or session that prevents leakage.

  3. Performance

    Deploy a full-integer quantised model to a microcontroller and diagnose the memory, latency and accuracy problems that appear.

    Given a firmware template and a board.

    Evidence running firmware, serial logs and a fault log.

  4. Performance

    Deliver a sensing device that meets a stated memory, latency and battery-life target under real conditions.

    Given a scenario from a provided list.

    Evidence a live bench demonstration on a fresh recording and a one-page trade-off note.

Topics

Constraints of tiny devices

  • Flash, SRAM, clock and power envelopes
  • Always-on sensing and duty cycling
  • Benchmarks for tiny inference

From sensor to model

  • Windowing, spectral and inertial features
  • Small convolutional networks and autoencoders
  • Quantisation-aware training and integer kernels

Deployment and measurement

  • Model as firmware: arena sizing and operators
  • Energy per inference and battery life
  • Desk accuracy versus field accuracy

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Microcontroller kits (provided)
Seeed XIAOML Kit, Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense
Microcontroller inference runtime
LiteRT for Microcontrollers, ExecuTorch with Cortex-M support, CMSIS-NN kernels
Training and firmware toolchain
PyTorch or Keras, PlatformIO, Arduino CLI
Power measurement
USB power profiler, shunt resistor and multimeter

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised budget calculation without AI assistance (20%)
  • Lab checkpoints (30%)
  • Live bench demonstration and trade-off note (50%)
To pass
70% overall, with the device meeting at least two of its three budget targets live

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised budget calculation. AI-tutor in labs. AI-full for capstone code, explained on request at the bench.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
