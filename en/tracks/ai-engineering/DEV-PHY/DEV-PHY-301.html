DEV-PHY-301 Simulation and Digital Twins for AI Systems

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 30 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-PHY Physical AI and Robotics Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 3 teaching days and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

30 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-PHY-201 Sensors, Perception and Compute for Physical AI
  • Trains and evaluates a neural network, as taught in DEV-MLE-202
  • Reads basic rigid-body mechanics: mass, friction, torque

Description

Participants use simulation to produce data and decisions they can trust. They build a simulated arm and conveyor cell, generate labelled synthetic images with domain randomisation, measure how a detector trained on them fares on real public images, fit simulator parameters to logged trajectories of a low-cost arm, and keep a digital twin synchronised with replayed telemetry. The capstone is a validated twin that answers one operational question and states where it must not be used.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Configure a physics simulation of an arm and conveyor cell, choosing time step, contact and solver settings.

    Given robot and scene description files.

    Evidence a seeded, reproducible scene with a sensitivity table for three parameters.

  2. Applied

    Generate a labelled synthetic image dataset with randomised lighting, texture, pose and camera placement.

    Given 3D models of parts and an inspection task.

    Evidence the dataset, its configuration and automated label and coverage checks.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate the synthetic-to-real gap of a detector and decide the mix of synthetic and real training data.

    Given a small labelled set of real public images.

    Evidence results across data mixes with confidence intervals and a decision memo.

  4. Applied

    Apply system identification to fit the friction, damping and mass of a simulated arm to logged trajectories.

    Given fitting and held-out runs from a low-cost arm.

    Evidence held-out trajectory error before and after fitting.

  5. Performance

    Build a digital twin that ingests replayed telemetry, stays synchronised and flags divergence from the asset.

    Given an asset information model and telemetry with seeded faults.

    Evidence a running twin whose divergence monitor catches the seeded faults.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate a twin against acceptance criteria for one stated use and decide the domain in which its answers hold.

    Given acceptance criteria and held-out real data.

    Evidence a validation report naming the validity domain and the uses it must not support.

Topics

Simulation foundations

  • Rigid-body dynamics, contact and friction
  • Simulated cameras, depth and inertial noise
  • Determinism, seeds, speed and fidelity

Synthetic data

  • Procedural scenes and automatic labels
  • Domain randomisation versus photorealism
  • Label quality and coverage checks

Closing the reality gap

  • Measuring the gap on real data
  • System identification
  • Learned simulators and world models in brief

Digital twins

  • Models, shadows and twins
  • Asset information models and telemetry synchronisation
  • Co-simulation with Functional Mock-up Interface (FMI) units
  • What-if analysis and virtual commissioning

Validation and use

  • Validity domains and acceptance criteria
  • Comparing simulated and real runs statistically
  • Drift between twin and asset

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Physics simulator
MuJoCo, Gazebo, Isaac Sim
Robot and scene description formats
URDF, MJCF, OpenUSD
Synthetic image generation
BlenderProc, the simulator's own randomisation tools
Twin information model and runtime
Asset Administration Shell with Eclipse BaSyx, Eclipse Ditto
Fitting and training
SciPy and Optuna, PyTorch or JAX on a notebook GPU

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI: predict and explain the failure in three simulation configurations (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Capstone repository and validation report (25%)
  • Defence on a changed scenario (25%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone and the defence

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first scene configuration. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
