DEV-PHY-302 Robot Learning Systems: Data, Policies and Deployment

  • Level 300 · Develop
  • Intensive
  • 36 h
  • Audience: practitioner

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-PHY Physical AI and Robotics Engineering

Level

Level 300 · Develop

Audience

practitioner

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days and a capstone day at robot stations, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

36 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-PHY-201 Sensors, Perception and Compute for Physical AI
  • Trains and debugs a neural network, as taught in DEV-MLE-202
  • Has run a physics simulation; DEV-PHY-301 is recommended

Description

In teams of two or three at a station with a low-cost open arm and a leader arm for teleoperation, participants collect and audit demonstrations, train an imitation policy, fine-tune a small pretrained action model, evaluate both over many trials in simulation and on the arm, and deploy the better one with a timed control loop, limits and a stop. Methods research belongs to SPC-GEN-401 and safety cases to DEV-PHY-401; this course is about data, evaluation and deployment.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Collect teleoperated demonstrations and audit them for synchronisation, task success and coverage of starting conditions.

    Given a leader-follower arm pair and a pick-and-place task.

    Evidence a versioned dataset of at least 50 episodes with a dataset card and rejection log.

  2. Applied

    Train an imitation policy and fine-tune a small pretrained action model on the same data at matched compute.

    Given a fixed budget of accelerator hours.

    Evidence tracked runs with configurations, checkpoints and learning curves.

  3. Performance

    Evaluate policies with randomised starting conditions and enough trials to separate them.

    Given both policies, a simulated task and the real arm.

    Evidence success rates with intervals, a coded failure taxonomy and every trial recorded.

  4. Performance

    Deploy a policy with a control loop that meets its rate, enforces joint, workspace and speed limits and stops on command.

    Given the station's compute.

    Evidence run logs with loop timing, zero limit violations and a passed stop test.

  5. Performance

    Diagnose the dominant failure of a deployed policy and decide what data to collect next.

    Given a budget of 20 further demonstrations.

    Evidence a data plan and a retrained policy whose change in success rate carries an interval.

Topics

Robot learning as a system

  • Observation and action spaces, control rates and latency
  • Where learned policies fail: contact, long horizons, novelty
  • Control, imitation or reinforcement learning

Demonstration data

  • Teleoperation with leader arms, phones and headsets
  • Episodes, synchronisation and dataset formats
  • Quality audits, coverage and licences

Policies

  • Behaviour cloning and compounding error
  • Action chunking and diffusion policies
  • Fine-tuning pretrained vision-language-action models

Evaluation

  • Trial protocols, randomised starts and seeds
  • Success rates with confidence intervals
  • Simulation benchmarks versus real trials

Deployment

  • Inference latency and asynchronous action execution
  • Limits, watchdogs and stops
  • Logging for replay and collecting corrections

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Robot learning library
LeRobot, a course-supplied policy codebase
Open arm with leader arm (provided, one station per two or three)
SO-101 pair, another open-hardware arm
Simulated benchmark tasks
MuJoCo tasks shipped with the library, LIBERO
Pretrained action model
a small open-weight vision-language-action model, an openly licensed generalist policy
Experiment tracking (training GPUs provided)
MLflow, TensorBoard

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI: diagnose three faults from training curves and trial logs (15%)
  • Graded labs (35%)
  • Team capstone and evaluation report (25%)
  • Individual oral at the station (25%)
To pass
70% overall, at least 60% in the oral, and a passed stop test

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check, the oral and the first data audit. AI-tutor in the labs. AI-full for capstone code, which the participant must explain.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
