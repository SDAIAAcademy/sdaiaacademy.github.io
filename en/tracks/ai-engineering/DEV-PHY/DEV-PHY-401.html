DEV-PHY-401 Safety Engineering for Autonomous Systems

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Workshop
  • 16 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

DEV-PHY Physical AI and Robotics Engineering

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Workshop: two days of analysis and simulation labs, ending in a defended safety case

Total hours

16 h

Prerequisites

  • DEV-PHY-301, DEV-PHY-302, or work on a system that senses and acts
  • Edits Python simulation scripts
  • Has applied a risk assessment method (LDS-AIG-204)

Description

On a synthetic warehouse vehicle with learned perception, participants analyse hazards, specify the operational design domain, write a safety case linked to test evidence, fault-test a runtime monitor, measure human takeover in simulation and turn a public incident into new hazards and tests.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Apply system-theoretic hazard analysis to a system with learned perception and specify its operational design domain.

    Given a vehicle design and perception results by condition.

    Evidence unsafe control actions, constraints and measurable domain boundaries.

  2. Performance

    Build a structured safety case linking claims to test evidence, with its defeaters.

    Given the hazard analysis and an evidence pack.

    Evidence an argument diagram and evidence register that survive peer challenge.

  3. Performance

    Design and fault-test a runtime monitor and fallback that keep the system in its safety envelope.

    Given a simulated system with injectable faults.

    Evidence violation and false-intervention counts with and without the monitor.

  4. Applied

    Measure human takeover time and mode errors and derive interface requirements.

    Given a simulated supervision task.

    Evidence ten measured handovers and derived requirements.

  5. Performance

    Review a published incident and decide the changes to hazards, tests and the safety case.

    Given a public incident report.

    Evidence a memo tracing each change to the safety case.

Topics

Learned components and safety

  • Functional safety and safety of the intended functionality
  • Distribution shift and specification gaps
  • IEC 61508, ISO 26262, ISO 21448, ISO/PAS 8800, UL 4600, ISO 3691-4

Hazard analysis

  • Fault trees and failure modes and effects
  • System-Theoretic Process Analysis (STPA)
  • Operational design domains and triggering conditions

Safety cases and runtime assurance

  • Goal Structuring Notation (GSN) and defeaters
  • Scenario-based testing and domain coverage
  • Simplex architectures and safety filters
  • Out-of-distribution monitors

Oversight and incidents

  • Takeover time and mode confusion
  • Automation bias and nominal oversight
  • Just culture and incident feedback into tests

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Simulation
Gazebo, CARLA
Diagrams as code
Mermaid, Graphviz
Incident records
AI Incident Database, OECD AI Incidents Monitor, safety board investigation reports

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI: find unsafe control actions in an unseen scenario (15%)
  • Lab artefacts (30%)
  • Safety case brief (30%)
  • Panel defence (25%)
To pass
70% overall and a pass from the panel

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and panel; AI-tutor in analysis labs; AI-full for drafting the brief, which the participant defends.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
