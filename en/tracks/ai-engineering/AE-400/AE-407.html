AE-407 AI Assurance, Red Teaming and Governance for Engineering Leads

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

AE-400 AI Engineering: courses that help prepare for the Expert level

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • AE-308 Securing Language Model and Agent Applications
  • AE-303 Evaluation-Driven Development for Language Model Applications
  • Has written a threat model for a system they built
  • Reads a risk register and a likelihood and impact matrix

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants lead the assurance of AI systems that act on an organisation's data. For the fictional Northern Region Benefits Agency (a bilingual benefits assistant and a case-worker agent that reads case files and drafts letters) they plan and run a red-team exercise in Arabic and English, manually and with automated probes, assess the risks with the National AI Risk Management Framework, review the security architecture for agent identity, least privilege, isolation and egress, and assemble an assurance case that ends in a residual-risk briefing. Targets run in a sandbox on synthetic case data. Hardening a single application is AE-308.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Plan a red-team exercise with objectives tied to harms, scope, rules of engagement and stop conditions.

    Given a benefits assistant and a tool-using agent with their intended uses.

    Evidence a test plan that a reviewer approves against a planning checklist.

  2. Performance

    Conduct manual and automated adversarial testing in Arabic and English and quantify attack success over repeated trials.

    Given sandboxed targets, technique catalogues and a fixed budget of model calls.

    Evidence attack logs, a findings report with success rates, intervals and severity, and three regression tests.

  3. Performance

    Assess the risks of an agent with the national framework, from context to treatment and residual sign-off.

    Given the agent's design, its supplier contract and a control library.

    Evidence a risk register with calibrated 4 by 4 scores, treatments, residual scores and named owners.

  4. Performance

    Review a security architecture for agent identity, delegated authority, least privilege, isolation and egress control.

    Given the agent's design documents and three delegation chains.

    Evidence a review report with findings, severity and decision records for the proposed fixes.

  5. Performance

    Build an assurance case that links each claim to evidence and maps the evidence to a control framework.

    Given the test, risk and review outputs and a control framework.

    Evidence an assurance case diagram and a gap list an auditor would accept as complete.

  6. Performance

    Present residual risk and a recommendation with conditions to an accountable executive.

    Given the assurance pack and a scripted supplier model change.

    Evidence a one-page residual-risk statement and a briefing scored by the panel.

Topics

Assurance as argument

  • Claims, arguments and evidence
  • Independence of testers and reviewers
  • Evidence that defeats a claim

Red-team practice

  • Objectives, rules of engagement and stop conditions
  • Technique catalogues: OWASP lists and MITRE ATLAS
  • Arabic, dialect and transliteration attacks
  • Automated probing and success over trials
  • Findings turned into regression tests

Risk assessment with the national framework

  • Context and scope
  • The seven risk categories
  • Likelihood and impact scoring and calibration
  • Treatment and residual sign-off
  • Supplier and model-change risk

Security architecture review

  • Agent identity and delegated authority
  • Least privilege derived from data classification
  • Isolation tiers and egress control
  • Action-level monitoring and kill switches

Evidence and reporting

  • Crosswalks to ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF and the CSA AI Controls Matrix
  • Assurance cases and gap lists
  • Briefing accountable executives

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Plan the exercise
1.1Assurance is an argument with evidenceWrite the top claim for the benefits assistant and list evidence that would support or defeat it
1.2Scoping from the threat modelReview the agent's provided threat model, add missing paths where private data, untrusted content and an exit path meet, and rank them for testing
1.3Objectives, scope and rules of engagementWrite a test plan with objectives tied to harms, stop conditions and handling of harmful outputs
1.4Choosing techniques from cataloguesMap ten planned attacks to OWASP and ATLAS entries and drop two that serve no objective
Day 2 Run the red team
2.1Manual probing in two languagesAttack the assistant in English, Arabic and transliterated Arabic and log every attempt
2.2Indirect injection and goal hijackPlant an instruction in a case file and try to make the agent send data out of the sandbox
2.3Automated campaigns over repeated trialsRun an automated probing campaign and report attack success with intervals
2.4Findings people can act onRate three provided findings for severity, likelihood and reproducibility and turn one into a regression test
Day 3 Assess the risk
3.1The national framework as methodSet context, scope and stakeholders for the case-worker agent using the framework's phases
3.2Scoring and calibrationScore twelve risks alone on the 4 by 4 matrix, then calibrate with a partner
3.3Treatment and residual riskChoose controls for the three highest risks in a provided register and state residual scores and signatories
3.4Supplier and model-change riskAssess a supplier's model update notice and decide what must be retested before it goes live
Day 4 Review and assemble the evidence
4.1Security architecture reviewReview the agent design against least privilege, isolation and egress criteria and write three findings
4.2Identity and delegated authorityFind the broken hop in three delegation chains where the agent acts for a case worker
4.3Assurance evidence packsMap a provided evidence set to a control framework and list the gaps an auditor would raise
4.4Briefing the accountable executiveDraft a one-page residual-risk statement with conditions, owners and dates from a provided assurance pack

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Automated probing
garak, PyRIT, promptfoo
Technique catalogues and testing guides
MITRE ATLAS, OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, OWASP AI Testing Guide, OWASP GenAI Red Teaming Manual
Target and attacker models
locally served open-weight models, a hosted open-weight model
Policy engine for least-privilege tests
Open Policy Agent, Cedar
Control frameworks
NIST AI RMF with its generative AI profile, CSA AI Controls Matrix, ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A
Risk register and assurance case
any spreadsheet, an open diagram tool such as draw.io

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: threat-model a short design and score four risks on the national matrix (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a rubric: test plan 15, testing and findings 30, risk register 20, architecture review 20, assurance case 15 (60%)
  • Live defence before a panel acting as accountable executive and auditor (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the first manual probing lab. AI-tutor in the other labs. AI-full in the capstone, including attacker models, provided the participant reproduces and defends every finding.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
