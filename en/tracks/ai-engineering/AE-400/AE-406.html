AE-406 Post-Training Open Models on Domain Data

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • AE-310 Model Adaptation: Fine-Tuning, Distillation and Small Models
  • AE-303 Evaluation-Driven Development for Language Model Applications
  • Writes and debugs a training loop in a deep-learning framework
  • Has tuned a model with adapters and evaluated it against its base

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants post-train open-weight models on domain data and prove the result is worth serving. For the fictional Eastern Coast Permits Authority they turn synthetic bilingual permit records, officer edits and agent traces into tuning mixtures, then run full and sharded supervised tuning, preference optimisation, a short reinforcement learning loop with verifiable rewards, distillation from a larger teacher and tuning on filtered agent traces. Each model is compared with its base and a prompting and retrieval baseline, for task gains and for regressions in general ability, safety and Arabic quality. AE-310 covers single-GPU adapters and serving; this course goes beyond them.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Decide whether post-training is justified over prompting and retrieval for a task, and which method fits it.

    Given baseline results, a quality target and serving cost limits.

    Evidence a decision memo with the measured gap, the chosen method and the cost of training and serving.

  2. Performance

    Build a validated tuning mixture of domain records, teacher-generated examples and general data with licence, deduplication and leakage checks.

    Given synthetic permit records, officer edits, a general bilingual instruction set and an open-weight teacher model.

    Evidence a versioned dataset with a data card, mixture ratios and a leakage report against the evaluation set.

  3. Applied

    Configure supervised tuning at scale, choosing full-parameter or adapter tuning and a sharding strategy that fit a stated accelerator memory budget.

    Given a memory limit, a GPU count and three candidate model sizes.

    Evidence a memory and throughput calculation, a training configuration and loss curves from a completed run.

  4. Performance

    Train with preference data or verifiable rewards and diagnose reward hacking in the result.

    Given a supervised checkpoint, officer preference pairs and a schema-based reward.

    Evidence run logs, rubric win rates before and after, and a documented fix for one reward exploit.

  5. Performance

    Produce a small model by distillation and by tuning on outcome-filtered agent traces that meets a latency and cost target.

    Given a larger open-weight teacher and 300 permit-agent traces.

    Evidence a table of quality, latency and cost per thousand requests comparing the small model with the teacher.

  6. Performance

    Evaluate an adapted model against its base for task improvement and for regressions in general ability, safety and Arabic dialect quality.

    Given the course evaluation suite with paired comparison.

    Evidence an evaluation report with paired intervals and a model card stating a release recommendation.

Topics

Deciding to post-train

  • Prompting, retrieval and tuning as alternatives
  • Cost of training against cost of serving
  • Open-weight licences and acceptable-use terms

Data for post-training

  • Curation, deduplication and mixing against forgetting
  • Synthetic data from teachers and its validation
  • Leakage against evaluation sets

Supervised tuning at scale

  • Full tuning, adapters and quantised adapters
  • Memory accounting for weights, gradients and optimiser state
  • Sharded multi-GPU training
  • Forgetting and safety regressions

Preferences and rewards

  • Preference pairs and annotator agreement
  • Direct preference optimisation and its variants
  • Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards
  • Reward hacking

Distillation, traces and merging

  • Distillation from a teacher
  • Rejection sampling of agent traces
  • Merging methods and interference between tasks

Evaluating adapted models

  • Task, general, safety and Arabic dialect slices
  • Paired comparison with the base
  • Model cards for adapted models

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Data decides the result
1.1When post-training beats prompting and retrievalRun prompt-only and retrieval baselines on the 200-item evaluation set and record the gap tuning must close
1.2Data mixtures against forgettingBuild three mixtures of permit pairs and general bilingual instructions and predict, then check on provided runs, which forgets least
1.3Synthetic data and its validationGenerate 200 teacher examples, filter them with rules and a grader, and measure duplication and leakage
1.4Memory and compute accountingCompute memory for full tuning, adapters and quantised adapters at three model sizes, with and without sharding over four GPUs, and pick a set-up
Day 2 Supervised tuning
2.1Full tuning against adaptersTune a 0.5-billion-parameter model fully and with an adapter for the same step budget and compare loss, memory and task score
2.2Hyperparameters that matterCompare learning rates and adapter ranks from provided run logs and one short run of your own
2.3Forgetting and regressionsEvaluate a provided tuned checkpoint on general bilingual and safety refusal sets and quantify what got worse
2.4Scaling outRead logs of a sharded multi-GPU run, find the throughput bottleneck and estimate cost per thousand examples
Day 3 Preferences and rewards
3.1Preference data from expert editsBuild 100 chosen and rejected pairs from officer edits and measure agreement on 20 of them
3.2Direct preference optimisationRun a short preference tuning from a provided checkpoint and compare rubric win rates with the base
3.3Verifiable rewardsWrite a reward for schema validity and field accuracy and run a short reinforcement learning loop on a tiny model
3.4Reward hackingInspect samples from a provided run whose reward rose while quality fell, and repair the reward
Day 4 Distillation, traces and the decision
4.1Distilling a teacherDistil teacher answers into a small model and compare quality, latency and cost per thousand requests
4.2Training on agent tracesFilter 300 permit-agent traces by outcome and tool-call validity and tune on the successful ones
4.3Merging task adaptersMerge two provided task adapters by two methods and score each merged model on both tasks against the separate adapters
4.4Evaluating the adapted modelRun task, general, safety and dialect slices on a provided adapted model with paired intervals against the base and the prompting baseline

Tools and platforms

These are examples. Any equivalent tool may be used instead.

Post-training library
TRL with PEFT adapters, a plain PyTorch training loop
Sharded training
PyTorch FSDP, DeepSpeed ZeRO
Open-weight models
small open-weight instruction models, a larger open-weight teacher served locally or hosted
Inference engine for generation and evaluation
vLLM, llama.cpp
Model merging
mergekit, task arithmetic in a notebook
Evaluation harness
Inspect, lm-evaluation-harness
Experiment tracking
MLflow, TensorBoard
Compute
a free notebook GPU for labs, Academy-provided multi-GPU hours for the capstone

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: memory accounting, choosing a method for four scenarios and reading loss curves (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a rubric: decision and data 25, training 25, evaluation against base and baseline 35, model card 15 (60%)
  • Live defence in which the assessor picks one regression and the participant explains its cause and a remedy (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the day 1 memory accounting lab. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant explains errors and does not write training or reward code. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
