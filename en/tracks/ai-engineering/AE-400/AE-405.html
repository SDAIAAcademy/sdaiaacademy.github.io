AE-405 Evaluation Programmes and Release Decisions

  • Level 400 · Evaluate
  • Intensive
  • 26 h
  • Audience: specialist

Track

AI Engineering

Stream

AE-400 AI Engineering: courses that help prepare for the Expert level

Level

Level 400 · Evaluate

Audience

specialist

Format

Intensive: 4 teaching days of four sessions and a capstone day, with guided pre-work and a tracked follow-up task

Total hours

26 h

Prerequisites

  • AE-303 Evaluation-Driven Development for Language Model Applications
  • AE-305 Operating Machine Learning and Language Model Systems
  • Computes a bootstrap interval and a paired difference in Python
  • Has maintained an evaluation suite or release gate for a deployed system

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants design how an organisation decides that an AI system is good enough to release, and how it keeps knowing afterwards. For the fictional Wadi Noor Utilities (a bilingual customer assistant, a complaint-triage classifier, a meter-anomaly model and an internal coding agent) they tier systems by risk, report results with clustered, paired and grader-corrected uncertainty, audit a public benchmark before it informs a model choice, write and back-test release criteria, turn incidents into regression cases and brief a mock board. All logs, traces and incidents are synthetic. Single-system gates are AE-303; service objectives are AE-403.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute clustered, paired and grader-corrected intervals for evaluation results and the number of items needed to detect a stated effect.

    Given item-level logs with five samples per item for two configurations and a grader with measured error rates.

    Evidence a notebook that a reviewer re-runs to the same intervals, including the sample size for a follow-up evaluation.

  2. Performance

    Design a risk-tiered evaluation programme that states the evidence each tier needs before and after release.

    Given an inventory of twelve synthetic systems scored with the national likelihood and impact matrix.

    Evidence a tiering rationale and evidence matrix that a second reviewer applies to two new systems with the same result.

  3. Performance

    Audit a public benchmark for construct validity, label errors, contamination signals and sensitivity to harness and token budget.

    Given a 40-item benchmark slice and two harness configurations.

    Evidence an audit note with an estimated label-error rate, the rank change between configurations and a verdict on fitness for the decision.

  4. Performance

    Specify blocking and advisory release criteria with non-inferiority margins and back-test them against history.

    Given results for eight past release candidates, one of them inside the noise floor.

    Evidence a criteria file and back-test table showing which candidates would have shipped and why.

  5. Performance

    Design post-release monitoring that grades sampled production traffic and turns incidents into regression cases.

    Given synthetic production traces with a seeded drift.

    Evidence a monitoring plan with signals, thresholds and named owners, and regression cases merged into the suite.

  6. Performance

    Defend a release recommendation with its uncertainty, residual risk and cost before a decision board.

    Given a model upgrade with mixed results across languages and customer groups.

    Evidence a one-page scorecard and a five-minute briefing that withstands two challenges from the board.

Topics

Evaluation as an organisational system

  • Inventory of systems and owners
  • Tiering by impact, autonomy and reach
  • Evidence per tier: capability, regression, adversarial, subgroup, human review
  • Shared harness, dataset registry and grader records

Statistics of evaluations

  • Items as a sample and clustered standard errors
  • Repeated trials and consistency
  • Paired comparisons, power and minimum effect
  • Correcting pass rates for grader error

Auditing benchmarks and claims

  • Construct validity and task ambiguity
  • Contamination and models that recognise a test
  • Harness, token budget and infrastructure effects on rankings

Release criteria

  • Blocking and advisory checks
  • Non-inferiority margins and noise floors
  • Change classes: prompt, model, retrieval, tool, provider
  • Sign-off, exceptions and their expiry

After release and reporting

  • Sampled grading of production traffic
  • Incidents turned into regression cases
  • Scorecards with uncertainty, residual risk and cost
  • Linking gates to the national risk framework

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Evaluation as a system
1.1An inventory comes before a suiteList the four Wadi Noor systems with owners, users and change rate, and mark the evidence each lacks
1.2Tiering by impact, autonomy and reachTier twelve system descriptions with the national matrix and resolve two disagreements with a neighbour in writing
1.3The evidence each tier needsWrite the evidence matrix for three tiers, from capability tests to human review
1.4A shared harness and dataset registryRegister two golden sets with version, owner, licence, language mix and a leakage check
Day 2 Statistics of evaluations
2.1Items are a sampleCompute naive and clustered standard errors on the assistant's item-level results
2.2Repeated trialsDecompose variance over five samples per item and report success in every trial and in any trial
2.3Paired comparisons and powerCompare two configurations with a paired bootstrap and size a follow-up for a stated minimum effect
2.4Graders are instrumentsCorrect a model-graded pass rate with measured sensitivity and specificity and carry the uncertainty through
Day 3 Benchmarks and release criteria
3.1Auditing a public benchmarkInspect 40 items of a public benchmark slice for label errors, ambiguity and contamination signals
3.2Harness and budget effectsCompare provided results for three models under two templates and two token budgets, re-run one cell and record the rank change
3.3Writing release criteriaWrite blocking and advisory checks with non-inferiority margins for the complaint-triage classifier
3.4Back-testing criteriaBack-test a provided criteria set on eight past candidates and explain the one inside the noise floor
Day 4 After release and to the board
4.1Change classes and exceptionsClassify six proposed changes, set the gates each triggers and the expiry of any exception
4.2Monitoring that feeds the suiteDetect a seeded drift in sampled production grades and turn the linked incident into three regression cases
4.3Scorecards with uncertaintyBuild a one-page scorecard with intervals, residual risk and cost per task for a provided release
4.4Briefing a release boardPresent a provided upgrade case in five minutes to a mock board and answer two challenges

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised check without AI assistance: hand calculation of intervals and sample size, and a benchmark-audit scenario (20%)
  • Capstone repository scored on a rubric: tiering 20, statistics 25, release criteria and back-test 25, monitoring 15, scorecard 15 (60%)
  • Live panel defence in which the participant applies the criteria to an unseen release candidate (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on the capstone rubric

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised check and the day 2 hand calculations. AI-tutor in the other labs: the assistant hints at the next step and does not write analyses or criteria. AI-full in the capstone, which the participant defends live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
