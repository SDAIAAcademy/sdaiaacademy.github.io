Description

Participants take the bilingual visitor assistant of a fictional national events authority, too slow at evening peaks and too costly, and make it meet its service objective at the lowest cost they can defend. They build a first-principles cost model, measure goodput curves on replayed traffic, diagnose seeded bottlenecks, then apply batching, caching, quantisation, speculative decoding, distillation, routing, disaggregated serving and autoscaling, re-running an Arabic and English task evaluation after every change.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Applied

    Compute prefill cost, decode cost and key-value cache memory, and predict the binding resource.

    Given a model card, an accelerator specification sheet and a traffic profile.

    Evidence a sizing sheet whose predicted concurrency limit is within 25% of a measured run.

  2. Performance

    Evaluate a serving configuration by measuring its goodput curve over concurrency against latency objectives.

    Given an engine, a small open-weight model and a replayed synthetic trace.

    Evidence a repeatable curve of at least seven points with raw data and run-to-run variance.

  3. Performance

    Diagnose why a deployment misses its service objective and confirm the fix.

    Given three seeded faults: queueing, preemption under cache pressure and prefill interference.

    Evidence a diagnosis per fault with its evidence, and a rerun that meets the objective.

  4. Performance

    Optimise batching, admission, prefix caching and cache precision while holding task quality.

    Given a multi-tenant trace and Arabic and English evaluation sets.

    Evidence before-and-after goodput, cost per million tokens and quality with intervals, and a passed cross-tenant cache test.

  5. Performance

    Evaluate quantised, distilled and speculative-decoding variants against the base model and decide which to serve.

    Given a quality floor per language and a cost target.

    Evidence a quality and cost table by language and a one-page serving decision.

  6. Performance

    Design and defend a serving topology covering disaggregation, routing, replica count and scaling policy.

    Given a daily demand pattern, a service objective and a budget.

    Evidence a design document with predicted and measured goodput and cost per successful request.

Topics

The cost model of inference

  • Prefill is compute-bound, decode is memory-bound
  • Key-value cache arithmetic
  • Roofline and queueing reasoning

Measuring serving performance

  • Time to first token, time per output token, goodput
  • Profiling where a request's time goes
  • Trace replay and load patterns
  • Pareto curves and benchmark validity

Inside the engine

  • Continuous batching, chunked prefill, admission control
  • Paged attention, cache precision and tiering
  • Prefix and semantic caching; tenant isolation

Cheaper models at the same quality

  • Quantisation formats per accelerator
  • Speculative decoding and acceptance rates
  • Distillation and multi-adapter serving

Across replicas and over the day

  • Disaggregated prefill and decode
  • Cache-aware routing
  • Autoscaling, cold starts and scale to zero
  • Cost per successful request and energy per token

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

1.1Prefill and decode are different problemsCompute operations and bytes per token for two models and predict what bounds each phase
1.2Key-value cache arithmeticPredict the concurrency limit at three context lengths and check it against the engine
1.3Goodput against an objectiveLoad the baseline at three concurrency levels and compute goodput
1.4Curves, not single numbersBuild a seven-point goodput curve from supplied runs plus one of your own
Day 2 Scheduling and caching in the engine
2.1Continuous batching and admissionVary the concurrency cap and plot tail latency against throughput

3.1Quantising weightsCompare two weight precisions on memory, goodput and bilingual task scores
3.2Cache precision buys concurrencyLower cache precision and measure concurrency gained and long-context accuracy lost

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks, AI-off: memory and prefill arithmetic, reading a goodput curve, choosing a first optimisation (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: cost model 15, benchmark method 25, optimisations with re-evaluation 25, topology design 20, reproducibility 15 (60%)
  • Live 20-minute defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and the day 1 hand sizing. AI-tutor in the other labs: it explains engine options and profiles but does not choose settings. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
