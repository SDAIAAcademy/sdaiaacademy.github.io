Prerequisites

  • AE-305 Operating Machine Learning and Language Model Systems

  • Has written alerting rules on service metrics

  • Has released a service through a canary or blue-green rollout
  • Reads a goodput-versus-concurrency curve (the measurement method is taught in AE-401)

Badge

Helps prepare for the SDAIA AI Engineering badge, Expert level.

Description

Participants set and defend reliability standards for AI services that other teams build. For a fictional postal and parcels operator running a bilingual tracking assistant on a self-hosted open-weight model with retrieval, they write service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, set admission limits with headroom from measured goodput, design and chaos-test degradation paths, lead incident drills and blameless reviews, release through canaries with automated rollback, and admit models only after verifying signatures and bills of materials.

Learning outcomes

By the end, the participant can:

  1. Performance

    Design service level objectives for availability, latency and answer quality, with error budgets and a release policy.

    Given a month of synthetic telemetry and a sampled online quality signal.

    Evidence an objectives document defended against a reviewer's challenges.

  2. Performance

    Decide an admission limit and shedding priorities that keep headroom for the busiest hour.

    Given the self-hosted model, a recorded traffic trace and the service objectives.

    Evidence a justified operating point and margin, and an overload replay in which priority traffic still meets its objective.

  3. Performance

    Design degradation for overload and dependency failure using load shedding, a smaller fallback model and a cached-answer mode.

    Given overload, retrieval outage and provider timeout scenarios.

    Evidence chaos test results showing each path keeps the minimum service, with quality measured in each mode.

  4. Performance

    Lead an incident drill from detection to recovery and write a blameless review.

    Given a silent quality regression after a model update.

    Evidence a timeline, a decision log, owned corrective actions and a new regression test.

  5. Performance

    Build a release gate that verifies model signatures, a bill of materials and licences before deployment.

    Given signed, tampered and unlicensed model artefacts.

    Evidence a pipeline run that admits the valid artefact and rejects the others, with logs.

  6. Performance

    Review another team's AI service for production readiness and set launch conditions.

    Given a peer's service, its telemetry and a readiness checklist.

    Evidence ranked findings and a launch decision defended before a panel.

Topics

Objectives for AI services

  • Time to first token, time per output token, availability
  • Answer quality from sampled online grading
  • Error budgets and release policy
  • Objectives for multi-step tasks

Capacity under load

  • Queueing and tail latency
  • Headroom for the busiest hour
  • Admission control and load shedding

Designing for failure

  • Provider, retrieval and tool failures
  • Fallback models and degraded modes
  • Retry storms and chaos experiments

Incidents and releases

  • Burn-rate alerts on latency and quality
  • Incident roles and decision logs
  • Canary releases with automated rollback
  • Reviews turned into regression tests

Integrity and readiness

  • Signing a manifest of hashed model files
  • Machine learning bills of materials and licences
  • Admission policy at deployment
  • Readiness reviews and launch conditions

Day by day

Four sessions a day. Each session presents the concepts with a demonstration, then ends in a lab.

#SessionLab
Day 1 Objectives that match what users need
1.1AI services fail differentlyRead a week of synthetic telemetry and list the incidents an uptime check would miss
1.2Indicators for latency and qualityDefine indicators for first-token time, per-token time, availability and a sampled quality grade
1.3Objectives and error budgetsSet objectives, compute budget burn from a supplied month and write the release policy
1.4Objectives for multi-step tasksDefine completion, cost and escalation indicators for the redelivery booking flow
Day 2 Capacity and failure
2.1Capacity with headroomLoad-test the model at rising concurrency, find where goodput falls and set the operating point
2.2Admission control and sheddingAdd a concurrency cap and priority shedding, then replay the overload trace
2.3Degraded modesConfigure a smaller fallback model and a cached-answer mode and measure quality in each
2.4Chaos experimentsInject a retrieval outage, a provider timeout and a retry storm and check the minimum service holds
Day 3 Incidents and safe releases
3.1Finding silent regressionsBuild a burn-rate alert on the quality indicator and locate the regression in a replayed day
3.2Running an incidentRun a game day on a model-update regression as incident lead, operator and scribe
3.3Canary and rollbackRelease a new model version as a canary with automated rollback on a latency and quality gate
3.4Learning from incidentsWrite a blameless review of a supplied incident and turn its cases into a regression test
Day 4 Integrity and readiness
4.1The model supply chainTrace the provenance of three model artefacts and find the tampered file by its hash
4.2Signing and verificationSign a manifest of model files and show that a changed file fails verification
4.3Bills of materials and admissionWrite a bill of materials and a policy that rejects unsigned or unlicensed models
4.4Production readiness reviewsApply the readiness checklist to a supplied service and rank the findings by risk

How the work is assessed

  • Supervised entry and exit checks, AI-off: compute error budget burn, set an admission limit from a goodput curve, order first actions in an incident (20%)
  • Capstone rubric: objectives and alerts 20, capacity and degradation 25, incident drill and review 20, release integrity 15, readiness decision 20 (60%)
  • Panel defence (20%)
To pass
70% overall and at least 60% on both the capstone rubric and the supervised check

Use of AI in the course

AI-off for the supervised checks and during the game day. AI-tutor in the other labs: it explains telemetry and tool behaviour but does not set objectives or thresholds. AI-full in the capstone, which is defended live.

AI-off: no AI assistance. AI-tutor: an assistant that hints and explains but does not give the answer. AI-full: any assistance; the participant owns the result and defends it.
